Playbook

One model, run the same way every day.

This page collects the rules that the case studies have in common. It grows as more examples are added. Right now it is built from twelve trades: three USD/JPY longs (4H, 1H and a weekly-to-4H swing), the XAU/USD short, two GBP/JPY longs (15m and 5m), two BTC/USD longs (15m and 1H), the AUD/USD long, the AUD/USD short, the GBP/AUD long and the GBP/USD short. Treat it as a draft to be tested, not a finished system.

The model

HTF zone → LTF shift → order block

Constant in all twelve

A liquidity sweep comes first, then a lower-timeframe shift, then a limit order at the near edge of the order block usually with an imbalance beside it. Seven fills only just reached that edge. The first Bitcoin, AUD/USD and second USD/JPY fills went deep, and the second GBP/JPY trade dipped halfway into the block after filling, so the stop has to sit beyond the structure, not inside the block.

The target varies

It is whatever stands first in the way: an opposing zone, or a pool of untouched liquidity. With a liquidity target, exit a few pips before the level.

Liquidity in front of the entry

In both GBP/JPY trades, AUD/USD, GBP/AUD, the second USD/JPY and both BTC/USD trades, lows sitting above the zone were swept before the fill, after waits of under two hours to a day. A bounce above an untouched zone is not the entry. See Inducement. If stops rest between price and your OB, expect them to be taken first.

All twelve are confirmation entries. The order is always sweep, shift, zone; the second Bitcoin case spells that out. See Entry models for how that differs from an aggressive entry at the zone.

Daily routine

What to do, in order

Before the session

  1. Mark 4H structure on each pair you trade.Last break of structure, the current swing high and low, and the 50% level of that leg.
  2. Mark the fresh zones and the internal liquidity.Demand in discount, supply in premium. Also mark internal highs and lows left inside the last 4H leg. Delete any zone that has already been tapped.
  3. Set alerts at the near edge of each zone and at each internal high or low.Then close the chart. Pairs sitting in the middle of their range are not candidates today.
  4. Check the news calendar.Note high-impact releases for the currencies involved. Do not leave a limit order resting through one.

When an alert fires

  1. Map the 30m delivery into the zone.Lower highs, lower lows, the last low, and the opposing zone at the origin of the move.
  2. Wait for the sweep and the MSS.A low is taken, price fails to continue, and a candle closes above the last lower high with displacement.
  3. Refine on the 15m.Find the order block and imbalance left by the displacement. Mark the protected low, which is often a final sweep just before the shift.
  4. Measure before you place.Entry, stop, target. If it does not pay 1:2 to the first opposing zone, skip it.
  5. Place entry, stop and target as one order.Size for fixed risk. Write down the reason in one sentence.

After the trade

  1. Screenshot all three timeframes.Before and after. Do it for losers and for setups you skipped as well.
  2. Log it.See the journal fields below. Review the log weekly, not trade by trade.

Order rules

Where the stop and the target go

Entry

Limit order at the near edge of the order block.

  • Placed when the MSS candle closes.
  • Near edge fills more often than the midpoint. Use the midpoint only if the OB is unusually tall.
  • Buys fill on the ask: add the spread to a buy limit.
  • Cancel if price covers about half the distance to target without a fill.

Stop loss

Beyond the swing that launched the MSS.

  • Long: below the protected low. Short: above the protected high.
  • The swing is the real invalidation. The XAU/USD chart used a tighter stop just beyond the order block: more reward per unit of risk, and more often stopped by a deep wick. Pick one placement and test it.
  • Add a buffer of a few pips. On shorts add the spread too, because buy stops trigger on the ask.
  • Never widen a stop after entry. Never size the stop to fit a lot size; size the lot to fit the stop.

Take profit

Near edge of the first opposing zone.

  • Usually the supply or demand at the origin of the move that delivered price into your HTF zone.
  • Liquidity along the way (trendline highs, equal highs) supports the target. It does not replace it.
  • Set it a few pips short of the zone edge.
  • If no zone stands in the way, target untouched liquidity (old highs or lows) and exit a few pips before it.
  • Under 1:2, there is no trade.

Risk

The rules that keep you in the game

Per trade and per day

  • Risk a fixed 0.5–1% of the account on every trade. Same risk on the setup you love and the one you doubt.
  • Stop for the day after two losses.
  • Do not hold two trades that are the same bet, such as longs on USD/JPY and EUR/JPY together, at full risk each.

What the win rate has to be

Break-even win rate = 1 ÷ (1 + reward-to-risk), before costs.

Reward : riskBreak-even win rate
1:233.3%
1:325%
1:420%

At 1:3 you can lose most of your trades and still make money, but only if every loss is exactly 1R. Losing streaks of five or more are normal at these win rates.

On profitability

No pattern guarantees profit. An edge shows up only across many trades taken the same way. Backtest this model on at least 50 occurrences, record the real win rate and average R, then trade it small on live data before sizing up.

Glossary

Labels used on the charts

LabelMeaning as used here
HTF / LTFHigher timeframe (direction and location) and lower timeframe (confirmation and entry).
BoSBreak of structure. Price breaks a swing in the direction of the current trend. On the 30m chart it marks the final low broken on the way into demand.
MSSMarket structure shift. Price closes through the last swing against the current trend, for example above the last lower high in a downtrend. The first sign of reversal.
CHoCHChange of character. The first break against the current internal trend. Used on the entry models slides for the same event the other posts label MSS.
DisplacementA fast, large-bodied move that leaves an imbalance. Shows intent behind a break.
IDMInducement. The first pullback low (or high) after a structure shift. Early traders enter there and their stops become liquidity for the real pullback. See Inducement.
IMBImbalance, also called a fair value gap. A range price moved through too quickly for both sides to trade. Price often returns to it.
OBOrder block. The last opposing candle before a displacement move. Used as the refined entry zone.
Supply / demandZones where a strong move down or up began. Fresh means price has not returned since.
$$$Liquidity. Stops and breakout orders resting above highs (buy-side) or below lows (sell-side), often along a trendline.
XMarks a liquidity sweep: the spot where a wick ran a high or low and price reversed.
BSL / SSLBuy-side liquidity (stops above highs) and sell-side liquidity (stops below lows).
EQL / EQHEqual lows / equal highs. Two or more swings at the same price, with a thick layer of stops beyond them.
SweepPrice trades through a high or low to trigger the orders there, then reverses.
Premium / discountUpper and lower half of a price leg. Sell in premium, buy in discount.
Protected low / highThe swing that launched a break of structure. If it is taken, the break has failed.
ROne unit of risk: the distance from entry to stop. A 1:3 trade pays 3R.

Why this trade failed: context before the signal

A long that had everything the entry needs: a higher-timeframe low swept, a 5m shift, an order block with an imbalance. It lost. The 1H was still bearish, and the rally that looked like a reversal was price returning to supply. This page goes through the six slides, then turns the lesson into questions to ask before any entry. A second loss joins it below: a 5m XAU/USD short stopped by the inducement resting above its entry band — the trade that wrote this site's confirmation rule.

From the caption

“You can get the liquidity sweep right, spot the MSS, and still end up on the wrong side of the market. Why? Because spotting a reversal signal doesn’t mean understanding the market’s direction. A lower timeframe MSS can look convincing, but if the higher timeframe structure is still bearish, immediately looking for buys might not be the best decision. This is why reading the market matters more than simply spotting setups. Don’t just look for entry signals. Understand the context behind them.”

5m · The losing long

The trade that failed

What the slide shows

  1. HTF Sweep. Price drops below a higher-timeframe low.
  2. X. One more small low is taken on the 5m.
  3. MSS. Price rallies and closes above the last 5m lower high.
  4. OB and IMB. A demand order block sits at the base of the rally, with an imbalance above it.
  5. The trade. Buy at the order block, stop below it, target at the old high marked BSL.
  6. The result. Price returns to the block and falls through it in a few candles. The stop is hit.

Why it looked right

Every part of the entry sequence is present: sweep, shift, zone with an imbalance. On the 5m alone this passes. That is the point of the post. The entry steps were followed, and the trade still lost, because the steps that come before the entry were not.

Rule to carry forward

A sweep, a shift and a zone are the entry. They are the last third of the routine, not the whole of it.

5m · The signal

The signal on its own

What the slide shows

  1. BoS down first. Before the sweep, the 5m had broken a low. The trend on this chart was down.
  2. The sweep and the MSS. The same marks as slide 1, seen closer.
  3. $$$ above the block. A small low sits just above the order block. Price is expected to take it on the way back to the zone.
  4. BSL (target). The target is the highest point on the chart, far above everything else.

What is missing

Nothing on this slide says what the higher timeframe is doing, or what lies between the entry and the target. The target is the top of the whole decline. For price to get there, it has to undo every break of structure on the way down.

In the terms of internal and external structure, a 5m shift is an internal break on the 1H. It can start a reversal, but more often it is a pullback inside the larger move.

Rule to carry forward

Before you trust a shift, name the timeframe it happened on and ask what the timeframe above it is doing.

1H · The context

The 1H was still bearish

What the slide shows

  1. BoS down. The 1H closes below its previous low. This is the most recent break of structure.
  2. Lower highs. The bounce after the break stops well below the level price fell from.
  3. X. The low that was swept. This is the “HTF sweep” from the 5m chart.
  4. No shift up. No 1H candle has closed above a 1H lower high.

Why it matters

The first step of the routine is to mark the trend on the higher timeframe and trade only that way. Here that answer is “short”. The long was a trade against it.

A sweep of a low inside a downtrend is common: price takes a low, bounces, and then continues down. The sweep says stops were taken. It does not say the trend has changed. Only a 1H close above a 1H lower high would say that.

Rule to carry forward

Direction comes from the last higher-timeframe break of structure. A lower-timeframe shift against it is a pullback until the higher timeframe itself shifts.

5m · The same chart, read for shorts

The plan that fits the context

What the slide shows

  1. X and BSL at the top. A high was swept before the decline began.
  2. OB with IMB. The supply order block that started the fall and broke structure. It has not been revisited.
  3. rEqh. Relative equal highs just under the supply. This is liquidity in front of the zone.
  4. Two BoS down. The 5m agrees with the 1H.
  5. Eql. Equal lows at the bottom: the sweep low and the low beside it. Stops rest under them.
  6. The short. Sell at the bottom edge of the supply, stop above it, target below the equal lows.

Why this reading is stronger

The rally that produced the bullish “MSS” is, on this reading, the pullback into supply. It has somewhere to go: the equal highs and the order block just above them. And the long’s stop was sitting under equal lows, which is where price is most likely to be drawn next.

So the same rally means two things. Read alone, it is a bullish shift. Read with the 1H, it is price returning to a supply zone in a downtrend.

Rule to carry forward

When a shift appears against the trend, look up. If a fresh opposing zone and untaken liquidity sit just above, the move is probably heading there to sell.

5m · Result

The short reaches its target

What the slide shows

  1. The rally. Price runs up through the equal highs and into the order block.
  2. The reaction. A wick goes into the zone and price turns.
  3. The fall. Price drops through the equal lows and past the target.

What to take from it

The fall that paid the short is the same fall that stopped the long. The long’s order block was demand inside a downtrend with equal lows under it. The short’s order block was supply in a downtrend with equal highs in front of it.

Notice that the short shown here is entered on the touch of the zone. A confirmation entry would wait for a lower-timeframe sweep and shift down inside the supply first.

Rule to carry forward

Zones in the direction of the higher timeframe hold more often than zones against it. Trade the first kind.

Side by side

Same market, two readings

What the slide shows

  1. Top. The long: a sweep, a shift up and a demand block, read on the 5m alone.
  2. Bottom. The short: the 1H trend, the supply that broke structure, and the liquidity on both sides.

The difference

Neither reading needed different information. Both used the same candles. The second one started one timeframe higher and asked where the liquidity and the opposing zone were before looking for an entry.

Rule to carry forward

Do the reading in order: trend, zone, target, then entry. An entry found first will make you fit the rest around it.

Bringing it together

Four reasons the long failed

1

It was against the higher timeframe

The last 1H break was down. The 5m shift was an internal break inside a 1H down leg. Step 1 of the routine would have said “short only”.

2

There was no higher-timeframe demand

The sweep took a low, but no marked demand zone sat there. A sweep with nothing behind it, against the trend, is the weakest version of the sweep model.

3

Supply and liquidity were just above

Equal highs and a fresh supply block sat a short distance over the entry. The target was far beyond both. The trade needed price to go through a zone that had every reason to hold.

4

The stop was under equal lows

The sweep low and the low beside it formed equal lows. Stops under equal lows are liquidity, and in a downtrend they are the next target.

Keep this honest

The short is shown after the fact. A short with the trend can lose too, and a long against it sometimes wins. The lesson is about odds: the long needed four things to go its way, and the short needed them not to. This is one example chosen by its author to make that point.

Before any entry

The context questions

Ask these before you look at the lower timeframe. They take two minutes and would have stopped this trade at the first one.

  1. Which way was the last higher-timeframe break of structure?That is my direction. Here: down.
  2. Is price at a higher-timeframe zone in that direction?For a long I need demand in an uptrend. Here there was none.
  3. Is the lower-timeframe shift with or against that direction?Against means a pullback until the higher timeframe shifts too.
  4. What is the nearest fresh zone in front of the trade?If it is closer than my target, the target is wrong or the trade is.
  5. Where is the untaken liquidity?Equal highs above pull price up to supply. Equal lows below my stop pull price through it.
  6. Is my stop behind a swept point or in front of an unswept one?It must be behind a low that has already been swept and has caused a break.

If you do trade against the trend

The site’s rules allow it only in a reduced form. These come from the sweep model.

  • Half the normal risk.
  • The target is the nearest clear high, placed before the first supply zone. On this chart that was the equal highs, not the top of the decline.
  • If that nearer target does not give 1:2, there is no trade.
  • Close the trade when price reaches the opposing zone. Do not hold through it.

On this chart, by the time the long order was resting at the order block, price had already reached the supply. The nearer target had been met before the entry, which is one more reason to cancel the order.

Loss 2 · XAU/USD 5m · 18–19 Nov '26

The short the inducement above stopped out

What the frame shows

  1. A proper bearish board. Staircase of supplies from above 3,7740, protected high ≈ 3,7745, bos and MSS down through the morning. Direction was never the problem.
  2. One inducement paid, one left. The line at ≈ 3,7620 was swept on the way down (X at ≈ 3,7600). The line at ≈ 3,7520 — resting just above the third band, ≈ 3,7480–3,7520 — was not.
  3. The entry. A short at that third band, stop above it, placed while the 3,7520 inducement still sat between price and the zone above.
  4. The toll. From 18:00 the rally taps the band (red box ≈ 3,7480–3,7540), sweeps 3,7520, and pushes into the second band to ≈ 3,7640 (red box ≈ 3,7580–3,7640). The stop, parked in the inducement's path, is gone.
  5. The drop, after. At 21:30 price collapses to ≈ 3,6970 — the move the short was meant to ride, starting about ninety minutes after the stop-out.

What went wrong

Nothing in the analysis — everything in the order of operations. Liquidity resting in front of a zone gets taken first; a stop placed inside that liquidity is part of the payment. The entry needed two marks it never waited for: the sweep of 3,7520 and a shift away from the band.

Rule to carry forward

If there is inducement, always wait for confirmation — the sweep of that line plus the shift away from the zone. The full write-up, with the ticket that waited, is case study: XAU/USD, stopped by the inducement above.

Loss 2 · The counterfactual

The version that waits

Same board, more patience. The rally sweeps 3,7520, then takes the second band's own inducement at ≈ 3,7620 and tags ≈ 3,7640 around 20:00–21:00; the shift down prints after 21:00. A short from the second band — ≈ 3,7580–3,7600, stop above ≈ 3,7640 — enters eighty points higher than the stopped one, with the toll already paid in front of it, and the collapse to 3,6970 pays roughly 1:8 to the old low near 3,7200 and about 1:13 to the print.

The market offered both tickets on one frame. The gate decided which one got paid. That is the pairing to read this loss against: the inducement case that paid waited for exactly these two marks on the same metal, one session earlier.

Loss 2 · What it bought

The rule this loss wrote

  • Draw the inducement before the order, not after the stop. The nearest liquidity between price and your zone: above a supply you sell, below a demand you buy.
  • Unswept means wait. No limit, no market order at the zone. The pullback's first target is that line.
  • Confirmation is two marks. The sweep of the inducement, then structure away from the zone on your entry chart. Nothing less counts.
  • The rule now lives in three places: note 12 in the routine, the pinned sticky note on every case page, and the inducement concept page's family of examples.

Repeatable

Checklist

  1. Higher timeframe first.One word, long or short, from the last break of structure.
  2. A zone in that direction.Marked before price arrives.
  3. A target with a clear path.No fresh opposing zone between entry and target.
  4. Then the lower timeframe.Sweep, shift, zone, in the same direction as step 1.
  5. A shift against step 1.Treat it as a pullback. Look for where it is heading and whether that is my zone.
  6. Inducement in front of the entry.If liquidity rests between price and your zone — above a short, below a long — wait for its sweep and the shift. Confirmation first, entry second (Loss 2, note 12).
  7. After a loss.Check which of these was skipped before blaming the entry.

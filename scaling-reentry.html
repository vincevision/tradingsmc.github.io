Scaling in and re-entering

Two things traders do after the first order: add to a trade that is working, and try again after a stop. Both can improve results and both are common ways to lose more than planned. This page sets the conditions for each, so the total risk on one idea never grows past the limit for one trade.

About this page

The rules below are written for this site’s method and are stricter than many traders use. The video above is by its own creator and may suggest a different approach. Where they differ, test both in replay before choosing. The site has no results data on scaling or re-entry.

Part 1

One idea, one risk limit

An idea is one higher-timeframe direction, one zone and one target. Every order placed on it, first entry, additions and second attempts, shares one risk limit.

  • If your limit is 1% per trade, all open positions on the idea together must not be able to lose more than 1%.
  • If you re-enter after a stop, the first loss counts. Set a cap for the whole idea, for example 1.5% or two attempts, and stop when it is reached.
  • Positions on closely related pairs in the same direction are the same idea for this purpose.

Rule

Count risk per idea, not per order. Four small orders on one idea are one trade.

Part 2

Scaling in: adding to a trade that is working

All of these must be true

  • The first position is in profit and price has broken structure in your direction since the entry.
  • The pullback after that break has given a new setup of its own: a sweep, a shift and a fresh zone, as in the notes.
  • The stop on the first position has been moved behind the new protected low or high before the addition is placed.
  • The addition still has at least 1:2 to the original target.
  • Total open risk after the addition is within your limit for one trade.

How to do it

  • Make the addition the same size as the first position or smaller. A larger addition moves your average price close to the market.
  • Use one stop level for all positions: behind the newest protected point.
  • Keep the original target. Do not extend it because the position is bigger.
  • Two additions at most. Past that, price is usually close to the target.
  • Record each addition in the journal as its own line, linked to the same idea.

A real example is in the Volatility 75 case, where three shorts were added on the way down, each after its own small sweep and shift.

What goes wrong without the stop move

If the first stop stays where it was and you add 0.5%, the worst case becomes −1.5%. Add twice and it is −2%. A single normal pullback can then turn a winning trade into your largest loss of the month.

Part 4

Re-entering

There are three situations. Only the first two allow another order.

SituationWhat happenedWhat to do
Stopped by a sweepPrice took your stop with a wick, did not close beyond the higher-timeframe protected point, and has shifted back in your direction.One more attempt is allowed. It needs a new sweep, a new shift and a new zone. The stop goes behind the new sweep. Use the same size or smaller.
Missed entryPrice turned before your limit order and left.Do not chase with a market order. Wait for the next break and pullback, and treat it as a new entry at the new zone. If price reaches the target first, the idea is finished.
Idea invalidatedA candle closed beyond the protected low or high on the higher timeframe.No re-entry. The direction has changed. Go back to step 1 of the routine.

Before a second attempt, ask

  • Is the higher-timeframe protected point still intact on a closing basis?
  • Is there a new sweep and shift, or am I using the old ones?
  • Is the reward still at least 1:2 from the new entry?
  • Will the two attempts together stay inside my cap for this idea?
  • Why was I stopped the first time? If the stop was in front of unswept liquidity, fix that.

Stop for the day on this pair if

  • The second attempt is also stopped.
  • You notice you want the loss back. That is the reason for the trade, not the chart.
  • The cap for the idea has been reached.

What is not allowed

  • Adding to a losing position. Buying more because price is lower raises the size exactly when the idea is being proved wrong.
  • Adding without moving the stop. The total risk grows with each order.
  • Adding without a setup. “It is going my way” is not a sweep and a shift.
  • Adding close to the target. The addition has little to gain and the same distance to lose.
  • Re-entering at the same price after a stop with nothing new on the chart.
  • Making the second attempt bigger to recover the first loss.
  • Widening the stop so that a re-entry is not needed.

Repeatable

Checklist

  1. Is this the same idea as an open or recent trade?Then it shares that trade’s risk limit.
  2. Adding: has structure broken in my favour since entry?Yes, with a candle close.
  3. Adding: is there a new sweep, shift and zone?Yes, on the entry timeframe.
  4. Adding: have I moved the first stop behind the new protected point?Yes, before placing the new order.
  5. Re-entering: is the protected point still intact?Yes. No candle has closed beyond it.
  6. Re-entering: is this attempt number two at most?Yes.
  7. What is the worst case for the whole idea now?Written down, and within my limit.

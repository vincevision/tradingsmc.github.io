How to read this page

The diagrams come from three accounts and use different marks for the same things. £ (ZeusCapital) and $$$ (BSBY, FXBY) both mean resting liquidity. CH and CHoCH both mean change of character. B and BoS/BOS mean break of structure. IDM is inducement. x is a sweep. OB is an order block and FVG a fair value gap. The images came without captions, so the explanations are my reading. Ten of the twelve are drawings, not trades, so no reward-to-risk is quoted.

Part 1

Structure: strong and weak highs and lows

Structure gives the direction and the two prices that matter most: where the idea is wrong, and where price is likely to go.

Structure · Bearish to bullish

Strong and weak points: a downtrend turns up

What the diagram shows

  1. The downtrend. Three lows are broken (BoS). Each broken low is a weak low. Each high that produced a break is a strong high, with a supply zone drawn from it.
  2. “No follow through”. The fourth low breaks the one before it, but price does not continue down. It bounces and leaves a small demand zone.
  3. CHoCH. After one more dip into that demand, price closes above the last strong high. The label on the bottom low changes to strong low.
  4. The uptrend. The labels swap. Highs are now weak highs and get broken. Lows are strong lows with demand under them, and they hold.
  5. The two longs. The tall blue boxes are long positions. Each starts where price comes back to a demand zone (mitigation), after small liquidity marked £ has built up and been taken.

How to use the labels

  • Strong means the point caused a break of structure. Expect it to hold. Your stop goes behind it.
  • Weak means the point has not broken anything. Expect it to be taken. Your target goes there.
  • In an uptrend, buy near strong lows and target weak highs. In a downtrend, sell near strong highs and target weak lows.

Why “reactive, not predictive”

Nobody buys the bottom here. At the fourth low there is nothing to act on, only a missing follow through. The first long comes much later, after the CHoCH and two breaks of structure upward. The price is worse and the evidence is better.

Notice also what the old supply zones do after the CHoCH. Price still reacts at two of them, then breaks through. The trend has changed, so they are places to take profit on a long. They are not places to sell.

Rule to carry forward

Label every swing strong or weak before looking for a trade. If you cannot say which low is the strong low, you do not know where the stop goes.

Structure · Bullish to bearish

The same thing upside down: an uptrend turns down

What the diagram shows

  1. The uptrend. Three weak highs are broken. Three strong lows hold, each with demand drawn from it.
  2. “No follow through”. The last high breaks the one before it and stalls. A supply zone forms on the first lower high.
  3. CHoCH. Price closes below the last strong low. The top becomes the strong high.
  4. Mitigation. The dotted blue arrows are price returning to a supply zone. Each return makes a lower strong high, and each is followed by a BoS downward.
  5. The old demand zones. Price reacts at each one on the way down and then breaks it. The line marked £ £ is the liquidity resting under the old strong low.
  6. The two shorts. Both start at a return to supply, after a small cluster of highs and lows (£) or a rising trendline of lows (£) has formed.

What to take from it

The first zone after a CHoCH is not traded in either diagram. Both wait for a break of structure in the new direction and then trade the next return. That costs one leg of the move and removes the trades where the CHoCH was only a deep pullback.

“Strong” is a label, not a guarantee

Both diagrams are drawn after the fact, so every strong point held. On a live chart a strong low is only the low that should hold. When it breaks, that is the CHoCH, and the labels swap.

Part 2

Entry models

Structure says long or short. The entry model says when the order goes in. The four below use the same zones and differ in how much they wait for.

Entry models · The two basic choices

Risk entry and confirmation entry

What the diagram shows

  1. The swing structure. A BoS down sets a range high and a range low. The trend is down. A supply zone sits under the range high.
  2. The pullback. Price climbs back in small steps. The breaks marked C and B are internal: a small change of character, then small breaks of structure. One is marked S. The slide does not define it. I read it as a high that was matched and not yet broken.
  3. Risk entry. A sell limit at the supply zone, placed in advance. The stop is above the zone.
  4. Confirmation entry. Price rejects the zone and breaks an internal low (C). The drop leaves a smaller zone. The sell goes there on the retest, with the stop above that small zone.
  5. Target. Both aim at the range low.

Internal and swing structure

This is the point of the slide. The pullback looks bullish if you only read the small breaks. It is five internal breaks upward inside one bearish swing. Internal structure tells you when the pullback ends. It does not change the direction, which comes from the range high and range low.

Entry, stop loss, take profit

  • Entry: Risk: sell limit at the bottom edge of the supply zone. Confirmation: sell limit at the small zone left by the internal break.
  • Stop loss: Risk: above the supply zone. Confirmation: above the small zone, which on this diagram sits under the high made inside supply.
  • Take profit: The range low for both. The confirmation entry is lower and its stop is tighter, so the ratio is similar or better.

The trade-off

The risk entry is filled every time price reaches the zone, including the times it goes straight through. The confirmation entry skips those, and also misses the moves that leave without a retest. The site’s entry models page covers the same choice under the names aggressive and confirmation.

Entry model 1 · Aggressive

Aggressive entry: a limit at the order block

What the diagram shows

  1. The order block. It sits at the pullback low. That low dipped under an earlier low first (the arc), so the block comes with a sweep.
  2. BOS. The rise from the block breaks the previous high. That makes the block valid and the low strong.
  3. Liquidity builds. The pullback makes lower highs under a trendline, and a higher low marked $$$.
  4. The fill. Price takes the $$$ low and drops into the block. The order was already there.

Entry, stop loss, take profit

  • Entry: Buy limit at the top edge of the order block, placed as soon as the BOS confirms.
  • Stop loss: Below the order block, which is also below the swept low. The yellow box on the slide.
  • Take profit: The high that made the BOS.

What makes this version acceptable

  • The block caused a break of structure.
  • The block’s low swept liquidity when it formed.
  • Liquidity above the block ($$$) is taken on the way in.

With all three, an order without confirmation is reasonable. With the $$$ low still untouched, the block is more likely the target of a deeper move than the turning point. See inducement.

Entry model 2 · Confirmation

Confirmation entry: wait for the lower timeframe

What the diagram shows

  1. BOS, then an order block at the low the break came from.
  2. IDM. The first small low above the block. Price breaks it on the way down, so the early buyers are out.
  3. Inside the circle. Price is in the block. On the lower timeframe it makes a low, breaks its last small high, and leaves a small zone (the shaded square). That zone is the entry.
  4. A second chance. After the next BOS, a new order block forms at the base of the leg. A pullback under a $$$ trendline brings price into it, and the trade repeats toward the same high.

Entry, stop loss, take profit

  • Entry: Buy limit at the small lower-timeframe zone inside the order block.
  • Stop loss: Below the low made inside the block. If the buffer puts it near the block’s own low, use the block’s low.
  • Take profit: The high marked target.

Compared with the aggressive entry

Same block, same target. The stop is a fraction of the size, so the same move pays more R. The cost is that you must be watching when price arrives, and some entries will not come back to the small zone.

All eleven case studies on this site are this model.

Entry model 2 · The full checklist

The confirmation entry with every label on it

What the diagram shows

The same model as the last slide, drawn on one timeframe with each step labelled.

  1. Order block at a low that swept an earlier low (the arc), followed by a BOS.
  2. IDM is taken during the pullback.
  3. $$$ builds along the falling highs.
  4. The tap. Price reaches the order block.
  5. CH. Price breaks the last lower high. BSBY and FXBY write CH for change of character.
  6. OB. The small block at the base of that move. Price returns to it and goes to the target.

Entry, stop loss, take profit

  • Entry: Buy limit at the top edge of the small OB.
  • Stop loss: Below the low made in the large order block.
  • Take profit: The old high. The falling highs with $$$ on them are taken on the way.

Rule to carry forward

Five things in order: BOS, inducement taken, zone tapped, change of character, return to the small block. If one is missing, the model is not there yet.

Entry model 3 · Sweep, then confirm

When the first reaction low is swept

What the diagram shows

  1. The first tap worked. Price took the IDM, reached the order block, and rallied to a new high with a BOS.
  2. The low of that tap is now liquidity. Anyone who bought there has a stop under it.
  3. The second visit goes deeper. After a long pullback with $$$ along its highs, price drops under that low (x). It stays inside the original order block.
  4. CH, then OB/FVG. Price breaks the last lower high and leaves a block or a fair value gap. The entry is there.
  5. BOS upward follows, then the target.

Entry, stop loss, take profit

  • Entry: Buy limit at the OB/FVG left by the change of character.
  • Stop loss: Below the sweep low (x).
  • Take profit: The high marked target.

Why it matters

An aggressive order at the block on the second visit would have filled early and sat in drawdown through the sweep. A stop under the first tap’s low would have been hit. The confirmation entry comes after the sweep, so the sweep becomes the reason for the trade.

A zone that has already been used

A second visit to the same block is weaker than the first. Take it only with a sweep and a change of character, never with a resting limit.

Entry model 4 · Continuation

Continuation entry: joining a trend that is already running

What the diagram shows

  1. The trend. Two BOS downward. The order block is at the high the trend started from.
  2. The curve. Three lower highs. Every one has stops above it.
  3. The pullback. Price rallies through all three highs and reaches the order block. Inside the pullback it makes a small high and low, then pushes above that high into the block.
  4. The shift. Price falls through the small low.
  5. The entry. The sell is on the retest after that break.
  6. Target. The last low of the trend, a weak low.

Entry, stop loss, take profit

  • Entry: Sell limit at the retest after the small low breaks. Use the bottom edge of the zone that the drop left.
  • Stop loss: Above the high made inside the order block.
  • Take profit: The low marked target. Price is drawn going through it.

How it differs from a reversal

There is no CHoCH on the larger structure. The trend is intact and you are selling a pullback in it. The sweep that matters is the run through the lower highs, which clears the liquidity above before price turns.

Part 3

Targets and real charts

Two diagrams on where price goes after the entry, then two candle charts that put the pieces together.

Targets · Reading the path

Price delivery: from one zone to the next

What the diagram shows

  1. The way down. Each lower high leaves a supply order block. Three are stacked above price.
  2. HTF demand. The last break downward (B) carries price into a higher-timeframe demand zone.
  3. CH. Price breaks the last lower high and leaves an order block at the low.
  4. The way up. Price reaches the nearest supply block and pulls back to the new order block. It then reaches the second supply block. The arrows show a reaction expected at each one.

How to use it

  • The long is at the order block made by the CH, after the first rise. Stop below the low in HTF demand.
  • The supply blocks above are the targets, nearest first. They are a ladder for partial profits.
  • Each block can turn price. The diagram draws arrows down at two of them. Move the stop up only after price has left a new strong low.
  • Count the room before entering. If the nearest supply block is less than 2R away, the trade does not pay.

Rule to carry forward

A target is the next zone or the next liquidity in the path. Mark every one between entry and the final target before placing the order.

Targets · Trendline liquidity

The target is the liquidity that built on the way

What the diagram shows

  1. The climb. Higher lows along a curved trendline. The lows carry stops, marked $$$.
  2. The sweep. The last push takes the previous high (x) and reaches HTF supply.
  3. CH. Price breaks the last higher low.
  4. OB. The block at the top. Price returns to it, then breaks structure downward.
  5. Target. The low the whole climb started from. Every low on the trendline is taken on the way.

Entry, stop loss, take profit

  • Entry: Sell limit at the bottom edge of the OB, after the CH.
  • Stop loss: Above the sweep high, inside HTF supply.
  • Take profit: The base of the trendline.

A practical note on the target

The base of the trendline is the full target and it is far. Each trendline low before it is a place where price can pause. Taking part of the position at the first one or two is reasonable. The slide shows only the full target.

On candles · Example 1

The short from HTF supply on a real chart

What the chart shows

  1. Trendline liquidity. A long rally with rising lows.
  2. HTF supply. The rally peaks inside the zone.
  3. CH. Price closes below the last higher low.
  4. IDM. A small high is left just under the peak. One fast candle later takes it and reaches back into supply.
  5. The short. The position box starts at that spike. The stop (yellow) is above the peak.
  6. The path. Price falls, rallies about halfway back without reaching the entry, and then drops to the target.

Entry, stop loss, take profit

  • Entry: Sell limit in the zone at the top, above the IDM high.
  • Stop loss: Above the peak, inside HTF supply.
  • Take profit: The low the rally began from, where the trendline starts.

What the candles add

The diagrams draw clean legs. Here the entry is one fast candle, so a market order would have been late. A resting limit above the IDM high is the practical way to get this fill. The halfway rally in the middle is also normal, and a stop moved to break-even would have survived it on this chart.

On candles · Example 2

XAU/USD: sweep of a range into supply

What the chart shows

  1. The range. Price stalls under HTF supply and makes several highs at one level.
  2. The sweep (x). One candle spikes above them into the zone. The order block is drawn there.
  3. Displacement. A long candle down leaves a fair value gap and breaks the range low (CH).
  4. The return. Price falls further, then rallies all the way back into the order block.
  5. The short. Entry in the block, stop above it, target at the base of the first rally, where an older FVG was left.
  6. Result. Price reaches the target line.

Entry, stop loss, take profit

  • Entry: Sell limit at the bottom edge of the OB.
  • Stop loss: Above the sweep high (the top of the yellow box).
  • Take profit: The base of the rally. The intermediate low is taken on the way.

What to notice

The retrace was deep and fast. It filled the FVG and reached the block. Waiting for the block gave the fill here. An entry at the FVG would also have filled, with a wider stop. This is the same model as the site’s XAU/USD case study.

Order placement

Entry, stop loss and take profit

The rules that hold across all four models, written for longs.

Entry

A limit at the near edge of the zone.

  • Aggressive: the order block that caused the BOS.
  • Confirmation, sweep and continuation: the small block or FVG left by the change of character.
  • Only at a zone next to a strong low, in the direction of the swing structure.

Stop loss

Behind the strong point, plus a buffer.

  • Aggressive: below the order block.
  • The other three: below the lowest point of the sweep or tap.
  • If that low breaks, it was not strong and the idea is finished. Do not widen the stop.

Take profit

The nearest weak point or opposing zone.

  • The weak high that made the last BOS is the standard target.
  • Opposing order blocks before it are places for partial profit.
  • Trendline lows or highs are taken on the way to the base of the trendline.
  • Under 1:2 to the first target, skip the trade.

Repeatable strategy

Step-by-step for the next trade

Written for longs. For shorts, mirror it. A “no” at any step ends the process.

  1. Label the swing structure: which highs and lows are strong, which are weak?Uptrend means strong lows and weak highs. If the last strong point has just broken, wait for one break in the new direction.
  2. Mark the zone at the last strong low.The order block the last BOS came from. Note whether it has been tapped before.
  3. Mark the liquidity between price and the zone.IDM lows, equal lows, trendline lows. Expect them to be taken first.
  4. Choose the model before price arrives.Fresh block with a sweep at its low: aggressive is allowed. Tapped block, or any doubt: wait for confirmation.
  5. For confirmation: has price tapped the zone and broken its last lower high?Draw the small block or FVG at the base of that move.
  6. Place the limit, with the stop behind the strong point.Below the block for aggressive. Below the sweep low for the rest.
  7. Mark every zone and liquidity level up to the weak high.The nearest one must be at least 2R away.
  8. Size, place, hold, record.Risk 0.5–1%. Write down which of the four models it was, so the journal can compare them.

Concept

The full sequence: structure, sweep, shift, imbalance, order block

One long trade, shown one element at a time. Structure gives the direction. A liquidity sweep ends the pullback. A market structure shift confirms the turn. The imbalance and order block give the entry. Every case study on this site is a version of this sequence, so this page works as the short form of the whole method.

How to read this page

X marks a liquidity sweep. SSL and BSL are sell-side and buy-side liquidity. IMB is an imbalance and OB an order block. The slides carry titles only and show no pair, timeframe, price or time. The explanations are my reading of the charts. Reward-to-risk is measured from the position box.

Part 1 · Direction

Market structure: which way is the trend?

What the slide shows

  1. Three breaks of structure upward. Each time, price closes above the previous high. Two are labelled BoS and the first has a line without a label.
  2. A higher low between each break. One pullback has a long wick down. It did not close below the prior low.
  3. After the last break, a drift lower. Small candles, no break of a low.

Why it comes first

Higher highs and higher lows mean the trend is up. That decides the side: look for longs only. It also decides how to read what comes next. The drift lower is a pullback in an uptrend until a low is closed below.

Rule to carry forward

Before anything else, mark the last break of structure and the low it came from. Trade in the direction of that break.

Part 2 · Liquidity

Liquidity sweep: the pullback takes the low

What the slide shows

  1. The BoS at the left. One large bullish candle. The base it launched from is the low under the X line.
  2. A long, slow pullback. Price gives back the whole move, step by step.
  3. A spike up and full reversal. Late in the pullback price jumps and is sold straight back. The slide does not label it.
  4. The sweep (X). A wick runs below the low the BoS came from and closes back above it.

Why it matters

The low under a break of structure is where buyers from that move keep their stops. A wick through it takes those stops and fills larger buy orders. The candle closing back above is what makes it a sweep.

This low is also the one that must hold

The low that launched a BoS is the protected low. A close below it would break the bullish structure from slide 1. Here only a wick went through. At the moment of the sweep you cannot yet know which it is. That is why the next step exists.

Part 3 · Confirmation

Market structure shift: proof the sweep was a sweep

What the slide shows

  1. The line at the bottom. The old low from slide 2.
  2. The sweep wick. It goes through that line and comes back.
  3. The MSS. A large bullish candle closes above the last small high of the decline.

Why it matters

During the pullback the short-term trend was down: lower highs, lower lows. The MSS is the first close above one of those lower highs. Coming straight after a sweep, it says sellers could not hold price under the low and buyers have taken over.

Rule to carry forward

After a sweep, mark the last lower high. Do nothing until a candle closes above it. A sweep with no shift is not a signal.

Part 4 · The zone

Imbalance and order block: where to enter

What is marked

  • IMB. The MSS candle moved so fast that it left an imbalance. The arrow points at it.
  • OB. A box at the base of that candle, covering the last small candles before it.
  • SSL. The low of the sweep wick, under the order block. The sell-side liquidity there has been taken.
  • BSL. A line far above, with a dashed line just under it. Buy-side liquidity that has not been taken.

Why it matters

The imbalance shows the move had force. The order block is where that force started, so it is where unfilled buy orders are most likely to remain. The two together turn “price should go up” into a price to buy at.

The slide does not show which high the BSL line is drawn from. I assume it is a high from the uptrend on slide 1.

Rule to carry forward

When the MSS candle closes, draw the order block at its base and check there is an imbalance above it. No imbalance, no trade.

Part 5 · The order

Entry and target: three prices fixed in advance

What is marked

  • Entry. The top edge of the order block.
  • Stop. The bottom of the grey box is level with the SSL line, the low of the sweep wick.
  • Target. The BSL line.

Reading the box

The target is about three times the stop. Price has only just shifted and has not come back yet. This is a resting limit order.

The stop covers the whole order block and the wick under it. That is wider than a stop just under the block, and it is why the ratio is 1:3 and not higher.

Result

Result: an edge fill, then a straight run

What happened

  1. A push up first. After the MSS, several large candles carried price higher.
  2. The return. Price came back and sat on the top edge of the order block for several candles. Wicks dipped a short way into it.
  3. The rally. A nearly straight climb to the BSL line.
  4. The target. Three wicks went through the line. Then price fell back hard.

What to take from it

  • The fill was at the edge. An order placed deeper in the block might not have filled.
  • The stop was never approached.
  • Price turned down right at the BSL. Taking profit at the liquidity, not beyond it, was correct here.

Everything as one

Five elements, five questions

THE LOGIC IN ONE SENTENCE

In an uptrend, wait for the pullback to sweep a low and shift back up with force, then buy the origin of that shift toward the high.

Order placement

Entry, stop loss and take profit

Entry

Buy limit at the top edge of the order block.

  • The order block is the base of the candle that made the MSS, under the imbalance.
  • Place the order as soon as that candle closes.
  • Here price returned to the edge and went little further. The edge is the price that fills most often.

Stop loss

Below the sweep low (the SSL line), plus a buffer.

  • The grey box ends at the sweep wick. Put the stop a little beyond it.
  • If that low breaks, the sweep was a real break and the uptrend from slide 1 is in question.
  • Do not put it just under the order block to improve the ratio. Deep fills are common in the case studies.

Take profit

The BSL line. About 3R.

  • Untouched buy-side liquidity above a prior high.
  • Price wicked through it and reversed. Set the order a little before the line.
  • If the distance from the order block to the BSL is under twice the stop, skip the trade.

Repeatable strategy

Step-by-step for the next trade

Written for longs. For shorts, mirror it. A “no” at any step ends the process.

  1. Is structure making higher highs and higher lows?Mark the last BoS and the low it launched from.
  2. Mark the liquidity.The low under the BoS is the sell-side. The nearest untaken high above is the buy-side and the target.
  3. Has the pullback swept that low?A wick below and a close back above. A close below it ends the long idea.
  4. Has a candle closed above the last lower high?That is the MSS.
  5. Did that candle leave an imbalance?If the break was slow and overlapping, pass.
  6. Draw the order block at the base of that candle.Buy limit at its top edge. Stop under the sweep low. Target the buy-side liquidity.
  7. Does it pay at least 1:2?If not, skip it.
  8. Size, place, hold, record.Risk 0.5–1%. Exit at target or stop.

A fair test of the headline

Is it really all you need?

The five elements are the core of every trade on this site. The comparison table in the playbook shows the same sweep, shift and zone in all twelve case studies. The headline still leaves things out.

What the five elements cover

  • Direction.
  • When the pullback is finished.
  • Evidence that the turn is real.
  • The entry price.
  • Where the idea is wrong, which gives the stop.

What they do not cover

  • The target. Even this post adds a sixth thing, the BSL. See types of liquidity.
  • Which zone is the real one. A sweep and shift above the true zone is a trap. See inducement.
  • Timeframe alignment. Most case studies read direction on a higher timeframe than the entry.
  • Risk. Position size, a minimum ratio and a daily loss limit.
  • News and session. None of the five says when not to trade.

Keep this in proportion

This is one winning example with no pair, timeframe or date. It shows what the sequence looks like when it works. It says nothing about how often it works. The same five elements appear on charts that then fail, and the stop under the sweep low exists for those.

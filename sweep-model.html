The sweep model, step by step

The second way into a trade. Price does not come back to a demand or supply zone. It takes a clear low or high, turns, and shifts structure. The zone you enter from is made by the sweep itself. Six steps, in order, with a diagram that builds up one long.

When both are present

If the lows that get swept sit at a higher-timeframe demand zone, you have the zone model, which is the stronger case. Use the sweep model when the level is clear and there is no zone behind it.

Step 1 · Higher timeframe

Check the context

There is no zone in this model, so the higher timeframe has one job: tell you whether a reversal here is with the trend or against it.

What you are waiting for

  • Nothing yet. This is a check before you start.

What you do

  • Read the last higher-timeframe break of structure.
  • With the trend: the higher timeframe is bullish and you are looking for a low to be swept. This is the stronger version.
  • Against the trend: the higher timeframe is bearish and you want to buy a swept low. Only at a major level, with a nearer target and half the risk.

Stop here if

  • The higher timeframe has just broken down with force and price is still falling fast. Lows get taken one after another in that phase.
  • You cannot say which side the higher timeframe favours.
Step 2 · Entry timeframe

Mark the liquidity and the target

The level takes the place of the zone. Pick lows that many traders can see.

What you are waiting for

  • Clear lows forming: equal lows, a range low, the previous day’s low, a session low, a trendline of lows.

What you do

  • Draw a line under the lows. That is the sell-side liquidity.
  • Mark the target: the nearest clear high above, where buy-side liquidity rests.
  • Check the distance. The target should be several times the depth you expect the sweep to go.
  • Set an alert at the lows.

Stop here if

  • The low is a minor one in the middle of a move. Few stops rest there.
  • The low has already been swept once.
  • A supply zone or old high sits just above, leaving no room.

Which lows are worth it

The more obvious the level, the more stops sit under it. Equal lows and range lows are the clearest. The types of liquidity page covers each kind.

Step 3 · Entry timeframe

Wait for the sweep

Price goes under the lows. The question is whether it stays there.

What you are waiting for

  • Price trading below the line.
  • A close back above it within a candle or two, or a long lower wick.

What you do

  • Mark the lowest point of the sweep. Your stop will go under it.
  • Do not buy the wick. A sweep and a break look the same while they are happening.

Stop here if

  • Candles close below the line and keep closing there.
  • Price comes back up to the line from underneath and is rejected.
  • The move down came on high-impact news that is still being priced.

A sweep and a break

You cannot be sure which one it is at the low. That is why the entry waits for step 4. Buying the wick is guessing; the shift is what tells you it was a sweep.

Step 4 · Entry timeframe

Wait for the shift

The same rule as in the zone model, and more important here, because there is no zone behind the trade.

What you are waiting for

  • A candle close above the last lower high (MSS or CHoCH).
  • An imbalance left by the move.

What you do

  • Mark the high that was broken and the imbalance.
  • Wait for the breaking candle to close.

Stop here if

  • Only a wick went above the high.
  • The break is slow and leaves no imbalance.
  • Price makes a lower low under the sweep.

Rule

Sweep without shift is not a setup. Many lows are swept and then broken properly on the next push.

Step 5 · Entry timeframe

Mark the zone the sweep created, and place the order

In this model the zone does not exist beforehand. The sweep and the shift make it.

What you are waiting for

  • Nothing. Place the order when the shift candle has closed.

What you do

  • Draw the zone: the last down candle before the move that made the shift. Its low is the sweep low.
  • Entry: buy limit at the top edge of that zone.
  • Stop loss: below the sweep low, plus 5–10 pips and the spread.
  • Take profit: the high from step 2, a few pips before it.

Stop here if

  • The ratio is under 1:2.
  • The zone is very tall because the sweep candle was large. The stop becomes too wide for the target.
  • Price has already come back and tapped the zone.

If price does not come back that far

A strong reversal often turns in the imbalance and never reaches the zone. You can place the limit at the middle of the imbalance instead, with the same stop under the sweep low. The stop is wider, so check the ratio again. Decide which you use before the trade and keep to it.

Step 6 · After the order is placed

Size, hold and exit

From here the two models are the same.

What you are waiting for

  • Price returning to the top edge of the zone.

What you do

  • Size the position so the stop costs 0.5–1% of the account.
  • Leave the stop and target alone once filled.
  • Journal it, and note that it was a sweep-model trade so you can compare the two models later.

Stop here if

  • Cancel if price reaches the target before filling you.
  • Cancel if a candle closes below the sweep low before the fill.

The details

Sizing, the cancel rules, break-even and the review are in steps 7 to 9 of the zone model page.

The other direction

Sweeping a high: the short

Everything is mirrored.

  1. Context. Stronger when the higher timeframe is bearish.
  2. Liquidity. Equal highs, a range high, the previous day’s high. Target the nearest clear low.
  3. Sweep. Price trades above the highs and closes back below.
  4. Shift. A close below the last higher low, with an imbalance.
  5. Zone and order. The last up candle before the drop. Sell limit at its bottom edge, stop above the sweep high plus the spread, target just before the low.
  6. Size, hold, exit. As for longs.

The range example on the liquidity page is a short of this kind, and the XAU/USD case starts with the 4H sweeping an old high.

Everything as one

The sweep model on one page

  1. Context: is the higher timeframe with you, or at least not falling hard against you?With the trend: full risk. Against: half risk, nearer target.
  2. Liquidity: is there a clear low with stops under it, and a clear high above?Draw both lines. Set an alert.
  3. Sweep: has price gone under the low and closed back above?Mark the sweep low. Do not buy yet.
  4. Shift: has a candle closed above the last lower high, with an imbalance?Mark the break.
  5. Zone and order: limit at the top of the zone the sweep made, stop under the sweep low, target at the high. At least 1:2?Place all three.
  6. Size, hold, exit, record.0.5–1% risk. Cancel if the target is reached first.

Keep this in proportion

With no zone behind it, this model depends on two things you judge in real time: that the level mattered and that the shift was real. Expect more losing trades than with the zone model. The site has no numbers for either. Backtest the two separately, at least fifty setups each on one pair, and compare the results before choosing.

The confirmation model, in his frames

TrendForge.FX posted this as a seven-frame carousel, “SMC Confirmation Model”. Five of those frames are reproduced here with his captions. How to use this page

The slides are by TrendForge.FX, from his “SMC Confirmation Model” carousel. His intro and outro frames are not reproduced; the five here carry his captions in the figure notes. The marks are the same shorthand the rest of this site uses: X a sweep, Sh the shift candle at an order block’s edge, MSS a market structure shift, IMB an imbalance, OB an order block, BoS a break of structure, SSL sell-side liquidity. The slides show no pair, price or timeframe — the axis carries session hours only — so read them as a pattern, not a trade. The explanations below are this site’s reading of his charts.

Frame 1 · The map

The whole model, before the steps

What the slide shows

  1. A 30m OB across the top. The higher-timeframe supply the whole trade is built against.
  2. A fall, a base, and a rally back into it. The left half of the chart is the context: price leaves the block, makes a low, and returns.
  3. A smaller OB at the rally high, with Sh at its left edge. The order block the shift leaves behind — the entry block.
  4. IMB and MSS at the displacement candle. The shift, and the imbalance that proves the displacement was real.
  5. A blue column from the small OB down to SSL. The trade itself: entered at the block, delivered to the sell-side liquidity at the prior low.

Why it comes first

Because the order of the marks is the strategy. The sweep and the 30m block come first in time, but the entry block only exists after the shift, and the target is drawn before the entry is placed. Reading the finished chart first stops the steps below from feeling like separate rules.

Rule to carry forward

Read the frames as one trade: bias, sweep, pullback, shift, entry, target. If any piece is missing on your own chart, the model says wait — the missing piece is the trade.

Frame 2 · Bias

The sweep sets the side, not the entry

What the slide shows

  1. An X at a high, left of the 30m OB. Price takes the liquidity resting above that high — the sweep.
  2. A hard drop from the sweep. Long bearish candles straight out of it; this is what makes the 30m bearish rather than merely ranging.
  3. A base and a partial bounce. The fall pauses; the bounce that follows is the pullback the next frame is about.

Why it comes second

The caption does the work: the bias is bearish because liquidity was taken above and then sold. A 30m order block on its own is only an area; the sweep into or above it is what turns the area into a side. Nothing here is an entry — the aggressive seller who sells the X is trading the sweep, and this model does not.

Rule to carry forward

The sweep is the reason to start watching, not the reason to click. After the X, the only job is to wait for the pullback.

Frame 3 · Confirmation

The pullback, then the shift that confirms it

What the slide shows

  1. The pullback climbs into the 30m OB. The bounce from frame 2 reaches the block and mitigates it. This is the moment the impatient sell.
  2. A smaller OB forms at the rally high, Sh at its left edge. The last up-candles before the turn: the block the shift will leave behind.
  3. MSS at a close below the last higher low. The pullback’s structure fails for the first time. A wick would not count.
  4. IMB on the displacement candle. The gap the break leaves — the evidence the shift was displacement and not drift.

Why it comes third

This frame is the confirmation in the model’s name. The 30m block says where; the MSS with an IMB says when. Until both exist there is no trade, only an area and a hope — which is exactly the mistake the losing XAU/USD case study on this site makes.

Rule to carry forward

No MSS with an IMB beside it, no trade. A wick through the low is not a shift, and a slow break with no gap beside it is not a shift either.

Frame 4 · The order

Enter at the block the shift left, not at the 30m

What the slide shows

  1. The small OB is the entry block. The order sits at the block drawn at the shift, not at the 30m block above it. The 30m gave the bias; the shift gives the price.
  2. The blue column runs from that block to SSL. Entry at the top, target at the bottom: the whole reward, drawn before the order is placed.
  3. SSL is the low of the first drop. The same low the sweep in frame 2 left behind. Nothing is invented as a target; it was marked in frame 1.

Why it comes fourth

Because the entry is the least important decision in the sequence and the last one made. The side came from the sweep, the area from the 30m block, the timing from the MSS. The entry is simply the price at which the confirmed idea is bought.

Rule to carry forward

Draw the target before the entry. If the distance to the SSL is not worth the height of the entry block, the trade is skipped, not resized.

Frame 5 · Delivery

The payoff for waiting

What the slide shows

  1. Price leaves the entry block. Filled at the block, it goes immediately the right way.
  2. It falls through the column. The blue area from frame 4 is now the path, not a plan.
  3. A long candle closes through SSL. The target is not tagged, it is broken — liquidity taken, trade done.

Why it comes last

It is the frame that makes the waiting look easy, which is exactly why it should be read with suspicion. A result slide is a story about one trade. The discipline is frames 2 to 4; frame 5 is what the discipline is for, not proof that it works.

Keep it in proportion

One delivered target proves nothing. The site’s own numbers for this exact sequence live in the case studies — see XAU/USD: confirmation pays, which ran this model on a live replay and printed its R:R from the position tool.

Memorise

A liquidity sweep followed by a shift can be enough — but if that liquidity sweep came from a demand or supply zone, the shift that follows adds more probability. Seen three ways in Exhibit D: two shorts on the euro, and the same gate in the mirror for longs.

Order placement

Where the stop and the target go

His slides draw the entry and the target but not the stop. The site’s standing rule fills the gap: the stop sits beyond the structure the idea depends on, never inside the block.

Orders, in this model

Entry: sell limit at the entry block — the small OB drawn at the shift, at the edge the displacement left.
Stop loss: above the protected high the shift made, the Sh candle at the block’s left edge. If price closes back above it, the shift did not happen and the idea is dead. Add the buffer and the spread; the block’s own height is not the invalidation.
Take profit: the SSL line, the low the first drop made. It was drawn in frame 1, before any entry existed.

Two worked numbers from this site, same sequence, different charts: the line drawing on entry models measures about 2.9 times the risk from block to SSL, and the live XAU/USD replay in confirmation pays printed entry 3,733.89, stop 3,743.22 (0.25%) and target 3,694.42 (1.057%) — R:R 4.23 in the position tool’s own words. The geometry pays when the shift is real; it is the shift that has to be real.

The point of it

The rule this model enforces

Note 12 says it in one line: if there is inducement, always wait for confirmation. These five frames are what waiting looks like, drawn.

  • The X is the trap. Selling the sweep means selling while the buys that chased the high are still above you, unswept. That is inducement over your head.
  • The pullback is the test. If the 30m block is defended, the rally into it fails. If it is not, no MSS prints and there is no trade — information, not loss.
  • The MSS with IMB is the confirmation. Only then does the model allow an order, and only at the block the shift left.
  • The two case studies are the control group. The losing XAU/USD short entered with inducement above and no shift: stopped. Its winning twin waited for exactly this sequence: paid. Same pair, same week, same rules, opposite order of operations.

What the model costs

The entry is later and worse-priced than the aggressive seller’s, and when price drops away after the sweep without retracing into the block, there is no fill at all. Those missed moves are the price of the confirmation, and they are supposed to hurt less than the stops they replace.

Repeatable

The sequence, in six steps

  1. Mark the higher-timeframe block and the liquidity under it. The 30m OB and the SSL below the recent low. The target exists before the trade does.
  2. Wait for the sweep (X) of the liquidity above. A high taken and sold. Until then the block is only an area.
  3. Let the pullback reach the block. No orders during the rally. The rally into the block is the mitigation, not the entry.
  4. Wait for the shift: MSS on a close, with an IMB beside it. Draw the entry block at the shift candle — the Sh at its left edge.
  5. Place the sell limit at that block, stop above the protected high. Beyond the structure, never inside the block.
  6. Target the SSL and journal the frame. Screenshot at the entry with all five marks visible. A setup you cannot screenshot is a setup you imagined.

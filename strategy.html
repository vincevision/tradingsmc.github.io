Two models

This page is the zone model: price returns to a demand or supply zone, sweeps and shifts there. When price takes a clear low or high with no zone behind it and then shifts, use the sweep model.

How to use this page

Go through the steps in order and do not skip ahead. A “no” at any step means no trade on that pair for now. The diagrams are drawn for this page and are not real trades. The black dot is where price is at that step. The rules come from the twelve case studies; the playbook has the full rule tables and the comparison of the cases.

Smaller timeframes give smaller stops and more trades, and make spread and news matter more. If you have a job during the day, the swing or intraday row is easier to follow correctly. Top-down analysis shows four timeframes on one trade.

Step 1 · Higher timeframe

Find the direction

Start on the higher timeframe and decide one thing: long or short.

What you are waiting for

  • A break of structure: a candle close above the last high (for longs).
  • Price starting to pull back after that break.

What you do

  • Write one word: long or short.
  • Mark the high that was just made. It is a weak high and the likely target.
  • Mark the low the break came from. It is the strong low.

Stop here if

  • The higher timeframe is moving sideways with no recent break.
  • The last break was down and you are looking for a long.
  • You cannot decide in a minute. Move to another pair.

Rule

Trade only in the direction of the last higher-timeframe break. Everything after this step is about timing.

Step 2 · Higher timeframe

Mark the zone and the target

Decide where you are willing to buy and where you will get out, before price gets there.

What you are waiting for

  • Price falling into the lower half of the last rally (discount).
  • Price approaching the demand zone the break came from.

What you do

  • Draw the demand zone: the last down candle before the move that broke structure, low to open.
  • Mark 50% of the rally. Only buy below it.
  • Mark the target: the weak high, or a supply zone if one sits in front of it.
  • Set an alert at the top edge of the zone and close the chart.

Stop here if

  • The zone has already been tapped and has reacted. It is used.
  • A candle has closed below the zone.
  • A supply zone or old high sits so close above the zone that 1:2 is not possible.

Rule

Nothing is bought when the alert goes off. The alert only tells you to open the lower timeframe.

Step 3 · Entry timeframe

Wait for the sweep

Price is at the zone. Now wait for it to take the stops resting above or inside it.

What you are waiting for

  • Lows forming just above the zone or at its edge: equal lows, a range low, a trendline of lows.
  • A drop through those lows into the zone.
  • The candle closing back above the swept low, or turning quickly.

What you do

  • Mark the lows as liquidity while they form.
  • When they are taken, mark the lowest point of the sweep. Your stop will go under it.
  • Do nothing else yet.

Stop here if

  • Price bounces from above the zone without taking any low. That bounce is likely inducement.
  • Candles close below the zone. It was a break, not a sweep.
  • High-impact news for the pair is minutes away.

Why wait for it

A sweep shows that the sellers’ stops and the early buyers’ stops have been taken. All twelve case studies had one before the entry. Without it, the low you are buying above is still a target.

Step 4 · Entry timeframe

Wait for the shift

The sweep is a reason to pay attention. The shift is the reason to trade.

What you are waiting for

  • A candle close above the last lower high of the pullback (MSS or CHoCH).
  • The move leaving an imbalance: strong candles whose wicks do not overlap.

What you do

  • Mark the high that was broken.
  • Mark the imbalance.
  • Wait for the breaking candle to close before drawing anything else.

Stop here if

  • Only a wick went above the high. That is not a shift.
  • The break is slow, with overlapping candles and no imbalance.
  • Price makes a new low under the sweep instead.

Rule

No shift, no trade. If price leaves the zone without one, you have missed nothing you could have traded by these rules.

Step 5 · Entry timeframe

Mark the entry zone

The shift came from somewhere. That base is where the order goes.

What you are waiting for

  • Nothing. The zone exists as soon as the shift candle has closed.

What you do

  • Draw the small demand zone: the last down candle before the move that made the shift.
  • Check that it sits inside or on the higher-timeframe zone.
  • Mark any small low that forms between price and the zone. Expect it to be taken before the fill.

Stop here if

  • The small zone is above the higher-timeframe zone, with untouched lows beneath it. See inducement.
  • Price has already come back and tapped the small zone before you could place the order.
Step 6 · Entry timeframe

Place the order

Three prices, set at the same time. Then check the ratio.

What you are waiting for

  • Nothing. Place it as soon as the zone is drawn.

What you do

  • Entry: buy limit at the top edge of the small zone.
  • Stop loss: below the sweep low, plus 5–10 pips and the spread.
  • Take profit: the target from step 2, a few pips before the level.
  • Divide the distance to the target by the distance to the stop.

Stop here if

  • The ratio is under 1:2. Do not move the stop closer to fix it.
  • The stop would have to sit inside the zone to make the numbers work.
  • A zone or liquidity level sits between entry and target at less than 2R.

For shorts

Everything is mirrored. The sell limit goes at the bottom edge of the supply zone, the stop above the sweep high plus spread, and the target at the weak low or the top of a demand zone.

Step 7 · Before the fill

Size the position

The stop distance is fixed by the chart. The only thing you choose is how much money that distance is worth.

The sum

  1. Money at risk = account × 0.5% to 1%.
  2. Stop in pips = entry minus stop.
  3. Lots = money at risk ÷ (stop in pips × pip value per lot).
  4. Round down to the size your broker allows.

Pip value per standard lot: about $10 on pairs quoted in USD such as GBP/USD. On JPY pairs it is ¥1,000, which is about $6.40 with USD/JPY near 157. On gold, lots = money at risk ÷ (stop in dollars × 100).

Worked example

  • Account $1,000, risk 1% = $10.
  • GBP/USD, stop 25 pips.
  • 10 ÷ (25 × 10) = 0.04 lots.
  • Target at 3R pays about $30. A loss costs about $10.

The USD/JPY swing case has the same sum for a 109-pip stop.

Rule

Size from the stop, never the other way round. If the smallest size your broker allows risks more than 1%, skip the trade.

Step 8 · After the order is placed

Wait for the fill, then leave it alone

Most of the work now is not interfering.

What you are waiting for

  • Price returning to the top edge of the entry zone.
  • In the case studies this took from under an hour to about a day on intraday charts, and days on the 4H.

What you do

  • Leave the limit order where it is.
  • Once filled, leave the stop and target where they are.
  • If you want a rule for moving the stop: only after price has closed beyond a new high and left a new higher low, move it under that low.

Stop here if

  • Cancel the order if price reaches the target before filling you.
  • Cancel if a candle closes below the sweep low before the fill.
  • Cancel before high-impact news if the order has not filled.

About break-even

Moving the stop to entry early feels safe and often costs the trade. In the GBP/JPY 5m case price dipped halfway into the zone after the fill before reaching the target. A break-even stop would have closed it for nothing.

Step 9 · The end

Exit, record, review

The trade ends at the target or the stop. The strategy ends when it is written down.

Exit

  • Target hit: done. Do not re-enter in the same direction into the opposing zone.
  • Stop hit: done. The idea was wrong or early. Do not re-enter until a new sweep and shift appear.
  • One loss on a pair is enough for the day.

Record

  • Screenshots of each timeframe, before and after.
  • Entry, stop, target, size, result in R.
  • Which steps were clean and which were forced.
  • How the fill went: edge touch, deep, or missed.

Review

  • Every 20 trades, count wins, losses and average R.
  • Look for the step that fails most often.
  • Change one rule at a time, then take another 20.

What the numbers need to be

At 1:2 you break even at about 33% wins, at 1:3 about 25%, before costs. The method does not need to win most of the time. It needs the losses to stay at 1R, and that depends on steps 6 to 8.

Everything as one

The whole strategy on one page

  1. Direction: has the higher timeframe just broken structure?Write long or short.
  2. Zone and target: is price pulling back to a fresh zone in discount, with room to the target?Draw both. Set an alert.
  3. Sweep: has price taken a low at the zone?Mark the sweep low.
  4. Shift: has a candle closed above the last lower high, with an imbalance?Mark the break.
  5. Entry zone: where did that move start?Draw the small zone.
  6. Order: limit at the edge, stop under the sweep, target from step 2. Is it at least 1:2?Place all three.
  7. Size: what position makes the stop cost 0.5–1%?Round down.
  8. Fill and hold: has price come back to the edge?Then hands off. Cancel if the target is reached first.

Keep this in proportion

These steps describe how the twelve trades on this site were taken. All twelve are winners chosen as examples, so they cannot tell you how often the steps work. Run the steps in replay on at least fifty setups on one pair, losses included, before trading them with real money.

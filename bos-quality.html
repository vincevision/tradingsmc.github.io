Concept

Good vs bad break of structure

Two uptrends can print the same break of structure and leave very different lows behind. If the pullback swept the previous low before price broke higher, the new low is protected. If it did not, the lows underneath are still untouched, and price is likely to come back for them. The second kind is where zone entries get stopped out.

How to read this page

X marks a liquidity sweep. BoS is a break of structure. BSL is buy-side liquidity. The slide captions are quoted. The two candle examples show no pair, timeframe or price scale, so the detail in them is my reading. Reward-to-risk is measured from the position boxes.

1 · The idea

Good BoS: the pullback sweeps the last low first

What the drawing shows

  1. A normal uptrend. A high, a pullback low, then a break above the high (the first BoS).
  2. A deeper second pullback. It goes under the previous pullback low. That is the X.
  3. The break. From that sweep low price rallies and breaks the latest high (the second BoS).

Why the new low is protected

Buyers from the first leg had stops under the first pullback low. The sweep took them. There is no clean low left under the new one, so price has less reason to come back down. The new low also launched a break of structure, which makes it the level the uptrend now depends on.

A sweep, not a close

A line drawing cannot show wicks. In the candle example below, price dips under the earlier low and turns straight back up. If price closes below the previous low and stays there, that is structure breaking down, not a sweep.

2 · The contrast

Bad BoS: a higher low with stops still under it

What the drawing shows

  1. The same uptrend. High, pullback low, break.
  2. A shallow second pullback. It stops above the previous low. A tidy higher low.
  3. The break. Price breaks the latest high all the same.

Why the low is unprotected

Nothing was taken. Stops are still under the first low, and new stops are now under the second. That is two layers of sell-side liquidity stacked under price. The post says such a low has “high potential to be swept in the future”.

“Bad” does not mean the break is fake. Price did make a higher high. It means the low is a poor place to buy from and a poor place to hide a stop behind.

Rule to carry forward

After every break of structure, look at the low it came from and ask one question: did that low take out the low before it? If yes, it is protected. If no, mark it as liquidity.

3 · On a chart

Example: the good break holds

What happened

  1. An uptrend with a BoS. Then a pullback.
  2. The sweep (X). The pullback dips under an earlier low. A large bullish candle follows at once.
  3. The second BoS. Price breaks the previous high and keeps climbing to a new one. That high is marked BSL.
  4. The zone. A box from the sweep low up to the base of the rally.
  5. The return. Price falls back and dips into the upper part of the zone.
  6. The result. It turns there and rallies through the BSL.

The position box

  • Entry at the top edge of the zone.
  • Stop at the bottom of the zone, which is the sweep low.
  • Target at the BSL, about 2.6 times the stop.

The pullback into the zone never came near the sweep low. There was nothing left under it to take.

4 · On a chart

Example: the bad break fails

What happened

  1. An uptrend with a BoS. A pullback leaves a low. The line under the chart is drawn from it.
  2. A higher low. The next pullback stops above that line. The order block (OB) is drawn here.
  3. The second BoS. Price breaks higher and runs to a new high, marked BSL.
  4. The return. Price falls back and taps the top of the order block. The long fills.
  5. A bounce. Price climbs roughly halfway to the target.
  6. The failure. It rolls over, goes through the order block, through the earlier low and through the stop. The last wick ends well below.

The position box

  • Entry at the top edge of the order block.
  • Stop under the order block. As far as I can read the slide, it sits just beneath the earlier low.
  • Target at the BSL, about 3 times the stop. Not reached. The trade lost 1R.

On the way up, this chart looked the same as the good one: two breaks, a zone, a return to the zone. The difference was underneath. An untouched low sat just below the order block, and price went and took it.

The bounce is the dangerous part

The trade was in profit by roughly 1.4R, measured from the box, before it failed. A trader who moved the stop to break-even there lost nothing. A trader who held for the full target lost 1R. Neither changes the lesson: the entry was at a zone with liquidity under it.

Telling them apart

Protected or unprotected?

The check has to be made before the order is placed. The first row comes from the slides. The others are my additions, drawn from the case studies.

CheckProtected low (good BoS)Unprotected low (bad BoS)
Did the low take out the low before it?Yes. There is an XNo. It is a higher low
What is under the zone?Only the sweep low itselfOne or more clean, untouched lows
How did price leave the low?Fast, usually one large candle with an imbalanceOften a steady climb
Is it at a higher-timeframe zone?Often. The sweep runs into HTF demandOften mid-leg, above any HTF zone
What to doBuy the zone at the lowMark the low as liquidity. Wait for it to be swept, or pass

A bad BoS can become a good setup

In the losing example, price ended by sweeping the unprotected low. That sweep is step one of the sequence on this site. If price then shifts structure back up with an imbalance, the zone that shift leaves is the one to buy. The slide does not show what happened next, so this is a rule to test, not a result.

Not a guarantee in either direction

Protected lows are broken when the trend ends. Unprotected lows sometimes hold for a long time in a strong trend, and waiting for the sweep means missing those moves. The post says “high potential”, not certainty.

Order placement

Entry, stop loss and take profit

For the long after a good break, as drawn on slide 3.

Entry

Buy limit at the top edge of the zone at the protected low.

  • The zone runs from the sweep low up to the base of the candle that launched the break.
  • The example filled in the upper part of the zone.
  • Only place the order once the break of structure has closed.

Stop loss

Below the sweep low, plus a buffer.

  • The grey box on slide 3 ends at the sweep low. Go a little beyond the wick.
  • If that low breaks, the protected low has failed and the uptrend is in question.
  • If an untouched low sits under your stop, the stop is not safe. That is the losing example.

Take profit

The BSL: the high made after the break.

  • About 2.6R in the example.
  • Set the order a little before the line.
  • Skip the trade if the high is closer than twice the stop.

Repeatable strategy

Step-by-step for the next trade

Written for longs. For shorts, mirror it: a good break down comes after a high is swept.

  1. Find the last break of structure in the trend direction.Mark the low it launched from.
  2. Did that low sweep the low before it?Yes: protected. Go to step 4. No: unprotected. Go to step 3.
  3. Unprotected: mark it as liquidity and do not buy the zone.Set an alert under the low. If it is swept and price shifts back up, treat that as a new setup.
  4. Protected: draw the zone from the sweep low to the base of the launch candle.Check that nothing clean and untouched lies beneath it.
  5. Mark the high made after the break.That is the BSL and the target.
  6. Buy limit at the top edge of the zone, stop below the sweep low.At least 1:2 to the target.
  7. Size, place, hold, record.Risk 0.5–1%. Note in the journal whether the low was protected.

Why the losing example matters

This is the first losing trade on the site. One good and one bad example prove nothing about frequency. Their use is to give you something to count: when you backtest, record for every zone entry whether the low was protected, and compare the two win rates yourself.

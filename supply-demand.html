Concept

Imbalance, supply and demand

Liquidity explains why price moves. Structure gives the direction. Supply and demand zones give the price for the order, and the imbalance is how you tell a real zone from a rectangle drawn on any candle. This page covers how each one forms, which ones to trade, and where the entry, stop and target go.

Part 1

What an imbalance is

An imbalance is a stretch of price that was crossed by one candle only. It needs three candles to define.

Candles 1 and 3 do not overlap. The space between the high of candle 1 and the low of candle 3 is the imbalance.

The definition

  1. Bullish. Candle 2 is a strong up candle. The low of candle 3 is above the high of candle 1. The gap between those two prices is the imbalance.
  2. Bearish. Candle 2 is a strong down candle. The high of candle 3 is below the low of candle 1.
  3. If the wicks of candles 1 and 3 overlap, there is no imbalance, however large candle 2 looks.

What it means

Inside the gap, price traded in one direction only. Buyers took every offer and moved on before sellers could trade back. It is a record of urgency. This move, where price leaves quickly and leaves a gap, is called displacement.

Rule to carry forward

Mark an imbalance only when the move that made it also broke structure. Small gaps appear on every low-timeframe chart and most mean nothing.

Part 1 · In practice

Three uses of an imbalance

1

Proof

A break of structure that leaves an imbalance was made with force. A break that crawls through the level with overlapping candles is weaker. In the case studies the shift almost always comes with an IMB.

2

Magnet

Price often comes back to trade inside the gap before continuing. That is why you can wait for a pullback after a strong move, and why an unfilled gap beyond your target is a reason to hold.

3

Location

The zone the displacement started from sits just beyond the imbalance. The gap leads price back to the zone. An order at the zone is filled after the gap has been filled.

How far it fills

What price doesWhat it tells youWhat to do
Never returnsStrong move. The order at the zone is not filled.Let it go. Do not chase with a market order.
Fills part of the gap and turnsStrong. Common when the trend is running.An order at the zone is missed. An order at the middle of the gap is the more aggressive choice, with the stop still beyond the zone.
Fills the whole gap and reaches the zoneThe standard case on this site.The limit at the edge of the zone fills.
Closes through the gap and the zoneThe move has failed.The stop is hit. The idea is finished.

Entering at the gap or at the zone

Entering at the imbalance gets more fills and has a wider stop. Entering at the zone has a tighter stop and misses some trades. Pick one and keep to it for a full sample. Mixing them trade by trade makes the journal useless.

Part 2

Demand: where buying started

A demand zone is the last place sellers were in control before buyers moved price far enough to break structure.

The last down candle before the move that broke structure. The zone runs from its low to its open and is extended to the right.

How to draw it

  1. Find the break. A close above a previous high, made by strong candles that leave an imbalance.
  2. Walk back to the start of that move. The last down candle before it is the zone.
  3. Mark two prices. The low of that candle, including the wick, is the far edge. The open of that candle is the near edge. If the candle is very small, use its high as the near edge.
  4. If price paused for a few small candles before leaving, draw the zone around the whole pause.
  5. Extend it to the right until price comes back.

Why price reacts there

The usual explanation is that large buyers could not fill their whole order before price left, and buy again when it returns. Nobody can verify that from a chart. What you can verify is the behaviour: price left this level fast and broke structure. Treat the zone as a level to test, not as a promise.

Fresh and used

  • Fresh: price has not returned since the zone formed. This is the one to trade.
  • Mitigated: price has returned once and reacted. A second visit is weaker. Trade it only with a sweep and a shift on the lower timeframe. See sweep, then confirm.
  • Broken: a candle has closed below the far edge. Delete it.

Part 3

Supply: where selling started

The mirror image. A supply zone is the last up candle before a move that broke structure downward.

The last up candle before the move that broke structure downward. The zone runs from its high to its open.

How to draw it

  1. Find the break. A close below a previous low, with an imbalance.
  2. Walk back to the start of that move. The last up candle before it is the zone.
  3. Mark two prices. The high of that candle, including the wick, is the far edge. The open is the near edge.

The edges for a short

For supply, the near edge is the bottom of the zone. That is where the sell limit goes. The stop goes above the top. It is easy to get this backwards when you have been looking at longs. The XAU/USD and GBP/USD shorts both sell the bottom edge.

Spread on shorts

Charts show the bid price. A short is closed at the ask, which is higher by the spread. Add the spread to the stop above a supply zone, or a wick that never shows on the chart can close the trade.

Selection

Which zones are worth trading

Any chart has dozens of last-down candles. Six questions cut them down. The first three are required.

QuestionWhy it mattersIf the answer is no
1. Did the move from the zone break structure?Without a break, nothing shows the zone had any effect.Not a zone. Do not draw it.
2. Did it leave an imbalance?Shows price left with force.Skip it.
3. Is it fresh?A zone that has been tested has already done its work.Only with lower-timeframe confirmation.
4. Did the zone’s extreme sweep liquidity?The stops under an old low were taken when the zone formed, so there is less reason for price to dig under it again.Expect a deeper move. Look for the zone below that did sweep.
5. Is it on the right side of the range?Demand in discount, supply in premium. See the next section.Poor reward-to-risk. Usually skip.
6. Does it agree with the higher timeframe?A 15m demand zone inside a 4H demand zone has two reasons to hold.Counter-trend. Smaller target or skip.

Liquidity in front of the zone

If small equal lows sit just above a demand zone, the zone is better, because price has a reason to come down to it. If a clean zone sits above an untouched origin zone with liquidity under it, the upper zone is likely the trap. That is covered in inducement.

Location

Premium and discount

Split the current swing in half. Buy in the lower half and sell in the upper half.

Two demand zones in the same leg. Only the one below the midpoint leaves room to the high.

How to use it

  1. Take the swing from the last strong low to the high it made (for longs).
  2. Mark 50%. Below it is discount, above it is premium.
  3. Buy only from demand in discount. A zone in premium is close to the high, so the target is near and the stop is the same size. The numbers do not work.
  4. For shorts, sell only from supply in premium.

This is an arithmetic rule more than a market theory. It makes sure the distance to the target is larger than the distance to the stop before you look at anything else.

Everything as one

Liquidity, structure, imbalance and zone in one trade

The same order of events as every case study on this site.

Equal lows are swept. One leg breaks structure and leaves an imbalance. The demand zone is the base of that leg. Price returns through the gap to the zone.

1

Sweep

Price takes the equal lows (X). The low of the sweep becomes the far edge of the zone.

2

Displacement

One leg closes above the last lower high (MSS) and leaves an imbalance. Now the zone is valid.

3

Return

Price fills the imbalance and reaches the near edge of the demand zone. The limit fills. The target is the weak high.

Order placement

Entry, stop loss and take profit

For a long from demand. Reverse every edge for a short from supply.

Entry

Buy limit at the near (top) edge of the demand zone.

  • Place it when the candle that broke structure has closed.
  • In seven of the twelve case studies price only touched the edge. An order deeper in the zone would have been missed.
  • For supply: sell limit at the bottom edge.

Stop loss

Below the far edge of the zone, plus a buffer.

  • The far edge is the wick low. If that low was a sweep, the stop is under the sweep.
  • A close below the zone means it has failed. Do not move the stop down.
  • For supply: above the wick high, plus spread.

Take profit

The first opposing zone or untouched liquidity.

  • The weak high that the displacement made is the standard target.
  • If a fresh supply zone sits before it, close there, or close part.
  • Set the order a little before the level.
  • Less than 1:2, skip the trade.

Repeatable strategy

Step-by-step for the next trade

Written for longs. For shorts, mirror it. A “no” at any step ends the process.

  1. Is the higher timeframe bullish, and is price in the discount half of its swing?If price is in premium, wait for it to come down.
  2. Has a move just closed above a previous high?That is the break. No break, no zone.
  3. Did that move leave an imbalance?Check that the wicks of candles 1 and 3 do not overlap.
  4. Mark the last down candle before the move.Low to open. Extend it right.
  5. Is the zone fresh, and did its low sweep an earlier low?Fresh is required. The sweep makes it the better zone.
  6. Is there liquidity between price and the zone?Good. Expect it to be taken before the fill.
  7. Buy limit at the top edge, stop under the wick low.Or wait for a lower-timeframe shift inside the zone and use the smaller zone it leaves.
  8. Is the first opposing zone or high at least 2R away?If not, skip it.
  9. Size, place, hold, record.Risk 0.5–1%. Record whether price touched the edge, went deep, or never came back.

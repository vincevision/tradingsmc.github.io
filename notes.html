Trading notes: the routine in order

The whole method as ten short notes. Mark the trend. Check that the break had a sweep before it. Mark the protected low and the zone that caused the break. Wait for price to return, sweep and shift. Then place the order. It is written for longs; the table at the end mirrors it for shorts.

The idea in one sentence

The same three events happen twice: sweep, break, zone on the higher timeframe to give you the place, then sweep, shift, zone on the lower timeframe to give you the entry.

Note 1 · Higher timeframe

Mark the trend

Open the higher timeframe and decide whether it is making higher highs and higher lows, or lower lows and lower highs.

Do this

  • Find the last break of structure: a candle close above the previous high (uptrend) or below the previous low (downtrend).
  • Write one word: long or short. You only trade in that direction.
  • Mark the high that the break made. It is the weak high.

Stop here if

  • Price is moving sideways and has not broken a high or low recently.
  • The last two breaks point in opposite directions.

Why

Trading with the higher timeframe means the larger orders are on your side. Every case study on this site starts here.

Note 2 · Higher timeframe

Check the break: was there a sweep first?

In a continuing uptrend, the pullback before the break must have gone below a previous low and taken the stops there. Only then did price break the high.

Do this

  • Look at the pullback that came before the break of the high.
  • Find the low it took: a previous swing low, equal lows, or the low of a range.
  • Confirm price went below that low and then turned up. That is the sweep.
  • If you find it, the break is a good one. Carry on.

Stop here if

  • The high broke without the pullback taking any low. Treat the low as unprotected and wait: price may return to sweep it. That sweep can become your setup.
  • The break was a wick only, with no candle close beyond the high.

Why

A sweep collects the sell stops resting under the low. Those stops become the buy orders that fuel the break. When a high breaks with no sweep before it, the stops under the low are still there, and price often comes back for them before the trend continues. See a good break of structure.

Note 3 · Higher timeframe

Mark the protected low

The low of the pullback that swept and then broke the high is now the protected low. The trend is intact while price stays above it.

Do this

  • Draw a line at the lowest point of the sweep.
  • Label it “protected low”.
  • Remember what it means: a candle close below it cancels the long idea.

Stop here if

  • A candle has already closed below this low. The trend has changed; go back to note 1.

Why

This low did its job: it took liquidity and produced a break. Price has no reason to return below it while the trend holds. That makes it the level your stop loss hides behind, and the level that tells you when you are wrong.

Note 4 · Higher timeframe

Mark the demand that caused the break

The zone is the last down candle before the move that broke the high. It sits at or just above the protected low.

Do this

  • Draw the zone from that candle’s low to its high.
  • Check it left an imbalance: a fast move away with a gap between candles.
  • Check it is fresh: price has not come back to it since.
  • Check it is in discount: in the lower half of the move from the protected low to the new high.

Stop here if

  • The zone has already been tapped.
  • There is no clear candle, or the move away was slow and overlapping.
  • The zone is in the upper half of the move (premium).

Why

This is where the buying that broke the high began. Orders that were not filled on the way up are still waiting there. A zone that did not break structure has not shown that strength. See supply and demand.

Note 5 · Higher timeframe

Mark the target

Decide where the trade ends before you look for an entry.

Do this

  • Mark the weak high from note 1. Price is expected to break it.
  • If a supply zone sits in front of that high, the target is the lower edge of that zone instead.
  • Measure from the demand zone to the target. You need room for at least 1:2.

Stop here if

  • The target is too close for 1:2.

Why

The target comes from the chart, not from the reward you would like. If the room is not there, the trade is not there.

Note 6 · Waiting

Wait for price to return

Set an alert at the top of the demand zone and close the chart.

Do this

  • Set the alert.
  • Do nothing until it rings. No trades in the middle of the move.

Stop here if

  • Price reaches the target before it returns to the zone. Delete the zone and start again.

Why

Most losing trades are taken between the zones out of boredom. The alert does the watching for you.

Note 7 · Lower timeframe

Inside the zone, wait for a sweep

When the alert rings, drop to the lower timeframe. Price should take one more small low inside or just into the zone.

Do this

  • Watch price arrive in the zone.
  • Wait for it to go below a lower-timeframe low and turn back up.
  • Mark the low of that sweep. Your stop will go below it.

Stop here if

  • A candle closes below the higher-timeframe zone. The zone has failed.
  • Price drops through the zone without slowing.

Why

This is the same event as note 2 on a smaller scale. It shows the selling is being absorbed at your zone, and it gives you a tight, logical place for the stop.

Note 8 · Lower timeframe

Wait for the shift

After the sweep, a lower-timeframe candle must close above the last lower high. That is the shift (also called MSS or CHoCH).

Do this

  • Mark the last lower high before the sweep.
  • Wait for a candle to close above it.
  • Check the move left an imbalance.

Stop here if

  • Price only wicks above the high and closes back below.
  • The shift is slow and leaves no imbalance.

Why

The shift is the confirmation. Before it, price touching the zone is only a touch. After it, the lower timeframe has turned in the direction of the higher one.

Note 9 · Lower timeframe

Mark the small zone and place the order

The entry zone is the last down candle before the shift move. This is the same rule as note 4, one timeframe down.

Do this

  • Draw the small demand zone.
  • Buy limit at its top edge.
  • Stop loss below the sweep low from note 7, plus 5–10 pips and the spread.
  • Take profit at the target from note 5.
  • Check the reward is at least 1:2. Risk 0.5–1% of the account.

Stop here if

  • Reward is below 1:2.
  • Price reaches the target before filling the order. Cancel it.

Why

Entering on the return to the small zone gives a better price and a smaller stop than buying the shift candle. If price leaves without filling you, you have lost nothing.

Note 10 · After the order

Hold, then record

Once the order is placed, the work is done.

Do this

  • Leave the stop and target where they are.
  • When the trade closes, save a screenshot and write the result in R.
  • Review every 20 trades, not every trade.

Stop here if

  • You feel like moving the stop to break-even early. The plan already has a stop.

Why

One trade proves nothing. Twenty trades taken the same way show whether the routine works for you.

How strict is “must”?

The sweep before the break is a filter you choose to apply, not a law of the market. Highs do break without a sweep and price sometimes keeps going. The filter is worth keeping because it removes the breaks that are most likely to be pulled back, and because it gives you a clear protected low for the stop.

  • Keep it as a fixed rule while you backtest. A rule you bend cannot be measured.
  • Count the setups you skipped because of it. If many of them would have won, review the rule after 50 samples, not after one.
  • When a low was not swept, mark it as liquidity. A later sweep of that low followed by a shift is a setup for the sweep model.

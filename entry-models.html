Entry models

Aggressive entry and confirmation entry

Two ways to get into the same idea. Both sell a pullback into supply after structure has turned bearish. The difference is how much proof you ask for before the order goes in, and what that proof costs you.

  • Both drawn as shorts
  • Schematics, not live charts
  • Mirror for longs

From the post

“Aggressive Entry: Taking positions early based on established market context and protected swing points without waiting for minor structural confirmations. Confirmation Entry: Waiting for a clear internal shift, a change of character (CHoCH), or a mitigation of premium and discount zones before executing. No guesswork. Just pure rules, patience, and structure.”

How to read this page

These two slides are line drawings of idealised price, so every swing is clean. Real charts are messier. X marks a liquidity sweep. $$$ marks resting liquidity along a trendline. EQH is equal highs. CHoCH (change of character) is the first break against the current internal trend; the earlier posts label the same event MSS. Reward-to-risk figures are measured from the drawn boxes and are illustrative only.

Model 1

Aggressive entry (with context)

You sell the zone when price touches it. There is no lower-timeframe signal first. What makes that reasonable is the context built up before price returns.

Reading the drawing, left to right

  1. A rally with a trendline of lows ($$$). Price makes higher highs (the first BoS) and its lows line up on a dotted trendline. Stops from buyers collect beneath that line.
  2. The final high sweeps the previous one (X). Price pokes above the prior high, takes the buy-side liquidity there and turns. This high becomes the protected high.
  3. A drop breaks structure. Price falls through the trendline and through the low the rally began from (the lower BoS line). The move is fast enough to leave an imbalance (IMB).
  4. Supply is drawn at the origin. The zone runs from the protected high down to where the drop began, with the imbalance directly beneath it.
  5. Structure breaks down again. Price bounces, then makes another lower low (the second lower BoS). The downtrend is now established, not a one-off.
  6. Equal highs form under the zone (EQH). The bounces stall at the same price twice. Sellers put stops above that double top, right in front of supply.
  7. Price returns. It runs the equal highs, fills the imbalance and enters the supply zone. The short is entered at the zone's bottom edge.
  8. Price falls to the last low. The target line is drawn across it.

The context that justifies entering without confirmation

  • The zone's high swept liquidity before the drop.
  • The move away from the zone broke structure and left an imbalance.
  • Structure has broken in the same direction a second time.
  • The zone is fresh. This is the first return.
  • Liquidity (equal highs) sits in front of the zone, so price has a reason to reach it, and those stops are taken as you are filled.

Orders

Entry: sell limit at the bottom edge of supply.
Stop loss: just above the protected high. On the drawing the grey box ends a fraction above the line.
Take profit: the most recent low. On the drawing this is about 3.8 times the risk.

The target line is labelled “BSL” on this slide. It sits under a low, which is sell-side liquidity, and the second slide labels the equivalent level “SSL”. I am treating the label as a slip.

Model 2

Confirmation entry

Price reaches the zone and you do nothing yet. You wait for the internal structure of the pullback to break, then sell the first retracement after that break.

Reading the drawing, left to right

  1. A drop from supply breaks structure. The large supply zone at the top is the origin of the fall through the BoS line. The low of that fall is marked SSL: sell-side liquidity, and the eventual target.
  2. The pullback climbs in a staircase. Higher highs and higher lows, each previous high broken (the short dotted lines). Internally, the trend is up.
  3. Its lows sit on a trendline ($$$). More sell-side liquidity building under the pullback.
  4. Price taps the supply zone and sweeps a high (X). The last push takes the previous internal high and reaches into the zone. The zone has now been mitigated. The aggressive trader is already short from the zone's edge.
  5. CHoCH. Price turns and breaks below the most recent higher low. This is the first time the staircase has failed. The drop leaves an imbalance.
  6. A new, smaller supply forms. It runs from the high made inside the big zone, now the protected high, down to the start of the CHoCH move.
  7. Price retraces into the small supply. The short is entered at its bottom edge.
  8. Price falls to the SSL. Through the trendline lows on the way.

What the confirmation adds

  • Proof that the higher-timeframe zone is being defended. If price had gone straight through it, no CHoCH would have printed and there would be no trade.
  • A protected high that formed at the zone, giving a precise invalidation.
  • A second, refined zone to enter from.

Orders

Entry: sell limit at the bottom edge of the small supply left by the CHoCH.
Stop loss: above the protected high. On the drawing the grey box ends on the line, so add a buffer and the spread.
Take profit: the SSL, the low that the pullback started from. On the drawing this is about 2.9 times the risk.

The cost

The entry is lower than the aggressive trader's and comes later. And if price drops away after the CHoCH without retracing, there is no fill at all.

How the two fit together

Look at the confirmation drawing from the CHoCH onward: a high that swept liquidity, a break of structure with an imbalance, a fresh zone, and a limit order at its edge. That is the aggressive model again, one level down. This is my observation, not a claim made in the post, and it explains why the two never conflict. Confirmation means waiting for the aggressive setup to appear on a smaller scale inside the larger zone.

Which one to use

Choosing between them

The post defines the two models and does not say when to prefer one. The guidance below is mine.

Lean aggressive when

  • The trade is with the higher-timeframe trend.
  • The zone is fresh, was born from a sweep and broke structure with an imbalance.
  • Liquidity sits in front of the zone (equal highs or lows, or a trendline).
  • The zone is narrow enough that a stop beyond it still pays at least 1:2.
  • You cannot watch the chart when price is likely to arrive.

Lean confirmation when

  • The trade is against the higher-timeframe trend, or the trend is unclear.
  • The zone is wide, so a stop beyond it is too large.
  • The zone is old or has been touched before.
  • Price is arriving fast, with large candles and no hesitation.
  • Nothing has been swept on the way into the zone.

Checklist: aggressive entry (short)

  1. Did the high sweep a prior high before turning?Mark it as the protected high.
  2. Did the drop from it break a swing low and leave an imbalance?Draw supply from the protected high down to the start of the drop.
  3. Has structure broken down again since?A second lower low shows the trend is in place.
  4. Is the zone untouched, with liquidity resting below it?Equal highs under the zone are ideal.
  5. Does it pay at least 1:2 to the last low?Sell limit at the bottom edge, stop above the protected high, target the low. Place all three together.

Checklist: confirmation entry (short)

  1. Is price inside a supply zone that caused a break of structure?Set the alert at its bottom edge and wait for it to fire.
  2. Map the pullback's staircase.Mark each higher low. The most recent one is the CHoCH level.
  3. Did price sweep an internal high as it tapped the zone?The new high becomes the protected high.
  4. Did a candle close below the most recent higher low with displacement?That is the CHoCH. It should leave an imbalance.
  5. Does it pay at least 1:2 to the SSL?Sell limit at the bottom edge of the small supply, stop above the protected high, target the low. Cancel if the protected high breaks or price reaches halfway to target unfilled.

For longs, mirror both lists: demand for supply, lows for highs, protected low for protected high.

Keep this in proportion

Neither model is the better one in general. In principle, aggressive entries lose more often and pay more when they win, while confirmation entries lose less often, pay less and miss some moves. Which comes out ahead depends on the market and on how you execute. Backtest both on the same set of zones and compare total R, not win rate.

Concept

Inducement: a liquidity trap

After a sweep and a structure shift, price often pauses and leaves a small, tidy zone close to current price. It has a break of structure, a pullback and sometimes an imbalance. It looks like the entry. Its real job is to collect buyers and their stops, so that price has something to take on its way to the zone the move actually came from.

How to read this page

The slides show one candle chart at three moments, plus one line drawing of the same idea. No pair, timeframe or price scale is given. X marks a liquidity sweep. $$$ marks resting liquidity. BSL is buy-side liquidity. Where I give a reward-to-risk figure it is measured from the drawn boxes.

Step 1 · The setup

One move, two zones

What the chart shows

  1. A range, then a sweep (X). Price drifts sideways. A wick runs under the earlier low of the range and is rejected.
  2. MSS on one large candle. A single bullish candle closes above the range high. This is the displacement.
  3. The lower zone. Drawn at the base of that candle, where the move began. Price has not been back to it.
  4. A short pause, then continuation. Two small candles pull back a little. The next candle breaks to a new high.
  5. The upper zone. Drawn around that pause. The line under it is marked $$$: the low of the pause, where stops will sit.

The question the slide is asking

Both boxes are demand zones by the usual definition. The upper one is closer, it formed after the shift and it led to a new high. The lower one is further away and needs a deeper pullback. Which do you buy?

The label gives it away. The upper zone has liquidity marked directly beneath it. The lower one has nothing beneath it except the sweep that has already happened.

Rule to carry forward

When two zones are stacked, ask which one is the origin of the displacement that broke structure. That is the real zone. A zone formed after the break, on the way up, is a candidate for inducement.

Step 2 · The trap in detail

Why the trap looks like a clean entry

This drawing shows two trades with the same pattern. The first loses. The second wins. Read it left to right.

The first trade: at the inducement zone

  1. Price leaves the HTF order block and breaks structure (BoS). The pullback low that led to the break is boxed and labelled Inducement, with $$$ under it.
  2. Price returns to that zone.
  3. Inside it, a small low is swept (X) and structure shifts up (MSS). That is a complete confirmation pattern.
  4. A long is taken. The first position box: entry at the top of the zone, stop just under the $$$ low.
  5. Price falls straight through. The $$$ low and the stop are taken.

The second trade: at the HTF order block

  1. Price carries on down into the HTF OB.
  2. The same pattern prints again. A low is swept (X), then an MSS.
  3. A long is taken from the LTF order block that the shift leaves behind, with the stop under the sweep low.
  4. Price rallies to the earlier high. About three times the risk on the drawing.

Why this matters

This is the uncomfortable part. The first trade was not careless. It waited for a sweep and a shift, which is exactly what the confirmation model asks for. It still lost, because of where it happened: above the higher-timeframe zone, with a clean low underneath it and the real zone still untouched below that.

Rule to carry forward

A confirmation pattern is only as good as its location. Sweep plus shift inside the higher-timeframe zone is a signal. The same pattern above the zone, with liquidity resting beneath it, is bait.

The slide text says “IBM”. From the other posts I read it as IMB, imbalance.

Step 3 · The trap closes

Traders enter early and are stopped

What happened

  1. Price made its new high and turned. A run of bearish candles brings it back to the upper zone.
  2. The early long. The position box shows the trade most people take: buy the top of the upper zone, stop just under the $$$ low, target the BSL line at the top.
  3. Price stalls inside the zone. A few small candles. It looks like the zone is holding.
  4. One candle drops through the $$$ line. Its wick goes below the stop. The early longs are out.
  5. An imbalance lies below (IMB). The gap left by the displacement candle sits between the two zones and has not been filled.

What gave it away beforehand

  • An unfilled imbalance directly beneath the upper zone.
  • An untouched zone at the origin of the move, beneath the imbalance.
  • A single obvious low under the upper zone for stops to gather at.
  • A shallow pullback. The upper zone is roughly at the middle of the leg; the lower zone is in the discount half.

Rule to carry forward

Before buying a zone, look underneath it. If there is an unfilled imbalance and an untouched origin zone below, expect price to go there, and expect the stops under your zone to be taken on the way.

Step 4 · The real entry

Only then does price reach the real zone

What happened

  1. Price kept falling after the stops were taken. It filled the imbalance.
  2. A wick touched the top of the lower zone. Just the edge, then a close back above.
  3. Price turned and rallied. Back up through the upper zone, to the earlier high, then on to the BSL line and through it.

The position box still on the slide is the early trade from slide 3, shown for comparison. Its target was reached in the end, but only after its stop had been hit.

What to take from it

  • The early traders had the direction and the target right. They lost on location alone.
  • The fill at the real zone was a wick. A limit order at the top edge filled. Waiting to watch a reaction would have meant chasing.
  • The trap supplied the fuel. Stops from the early longs are sell orders. They gave larger buyers something to fill against at a better price.

Rule to carry forward

Put the limit order at the origin zone and treat everything above it as liquidity. The better entry comes after the trap, not before it.

Telling them apart

Inducement or the real zone?

The slides show what inducement looks like after the fact. The table below turns that into checks you can run beforehand. The first two rows come from the slides. The rest are my reading.

CheckInducementReal zone
What is underneath it?A clean low with stops ($$$), and often an unfilled imbalanceThe sweep low. The liquidity there has already been taken
How did it form?A pause or small pullback after structure had already brokenThe base of the displacement that broke structure
Where is it in the leg?Shallow, around or above the midpointDeep, in the discount half
Where is it relative to the HTF zone?Above it. Price has left the HTF zone and not come backInside it
Is another untouched zone below?YesNo. It is the last zone before the sweep low
How obvious is it?Very. It is the first thing everyone seesLess. It needs a deeper pullback that feels like the move is failing

The first zone sometimes holds

None of these checks is certain. In a strong trend price can turn at the shallow zone and never come back for the deep one. If you always wait for the origin zone you will miss those moves. That is the price of avoiding the trap, and it is why the post says “often” and “usually”, not “always”.

Order placement

Entry, stop loss and take profit

For the trade at the real zone. The candle slides do not draw this position, so the placements follow the line drawing on slide 2 and the case studies.

Entry

Buy limit at the top edge of the origin zone.

  • The origin zone is the base of the displacement candle that made the MSS.
  • On the candle chart only a wick reached it. A limit at the edge filled.
  • Stricter version, from the line drawing: wait for a sweep and MSS inside the zone and buy the LTF order block it leaves.

Stop loss

Below the origin zone, under the sweep low.

  • The sweep low (X) is where the whole move began. If it breaks, the idea is finished.
  • In the line drawing the stop sits at the low of the sweep inside the HTF OB.
  • Never under the inducement low. That is the level price is being drawn to.

Take profit

The high above, or the BSL beyond it.

  • The line drawing targets the high made before the pullback, at about 1:3.
  • The candle chart marks a BSL line higher up, which price also reached.
  • The entry is lower than the early trader's and the target is the same, so the deeper zone can pay more per unit of risk.

Repeatable strategy

Step-by-step for the next trade

Written for longs. For shorts, mirror it.

  1. Find the sweep and the shift.A low taken and rejected, then a displacement candle closing above the prior high.
  2. Mark the origin zone.The base of the displacement candle, above the sweep low. Place the buy limit at its top edge.
  3. Mark every zone and low that forms above it afterwards.Pauses, small pullbacks, first higher lows. Label each one as liquidity, not as an entry.
  4. Check what lies between price and the origin zone.An unfilled imbalance or an untaken low means price has a reason to come down.
  5. If price reacts at an upper zone, do nothing.Even if it prints a sweep and a shift there. Note the new low it leaves.
  6. If price runs to target without you, accept it.Cancel the order. Record it as a missed trade.
  7. When price reaches the origin zone, the order fills.Stop under the sweep low, target at the high above. At least 1:2, risk 0.5–1%.

Compare this with the entry models. The confirmation entry asks for a sweep and a shift before entering. This page adds the condition that makes it work: the sweep and shift have to happen at the zone, not above it.

Keep this in proportion

Every example on this site was chosen because the deep zone was reached and held. To know how often that happens, you need the other outcomes too: the times the shallow zone held and the trade was missed, and the times the origin zone was reached and failed. Log all three when you backtest.

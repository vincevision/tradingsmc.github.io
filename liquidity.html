Concept

Types of liquidity: trendlines, ranges and sessions

Liquidity is where stop orders and breakout orders collect. They collect where many traders look at the same thing: a line across the highs, the edge of a range, the high of the last session. Price often visits those places first and makes its lasting move afterwards. This page covers three such places and how to trade around them.

  • Buy-side: stops above highs

1

Trendline liquidity above a downtrend

Three lower highs line up. Anyone can join them with a line, and many do. They sell each touch and put the stop just above the line. Every touch adds more stops, so the liquidity above the line grows the longer the line holds.

  • What is above the line: buy stops from sellers, plus buy orders from traders waiting for a trendline break.
  • What price did: it rallied through the line and through every high behind it in one leg.
  • What came just before: a sharp drop to a new low under the earlier lows. The slide does not label it, but that drop took the sell-side first. The rally started from there.

Look at the last candles

After the break, price falls back a long way and the chart ends. The slide says price “moves up”, and it did go up through the line. Whether it kept going is not shown. Treat a trendline as a place price is drawn to, and take profit there. Do not assume the break continues.

2

Trendline liquidity under an uptrend

The mirror image. Rising lows are joined by a line. Buyers buy each touch and place stops under it. Those stops are sell orders, and they sit in a neat diagonal row.

  • What is below the line: sell stops from buyers, plus sell orders from breakout traders.
  • What price did: large bearish candles cut through the line and kept falling, past the low the line started from.
  • What came just before: one long wick above the recent highs that closed back down. Again unlabelled, and again it is a sweep of the opposite side before the move.

What both trendline slides share

In both, price first takes liquidity on one side with a wick or a fast drop, then travels to the trendline liquidity on the other side. The trendline is the destination. The sweep on the opposite side is the place to look for the entry.

3

Range liquidity

Price moves sideways between a high and a low. Traders who sell the top put stops above the high. Traders who buy the bottom put stops below the low. Breakout traders place orders at both. So a range has liquidity on each side.

  • The take: one large bullish candle closes above the range high. It looks like a breakout.
  • The failure: the next candles are bearish and bring price straight back inside the range.
  • The move: price falls through the whole range and through the low on the other side.

The breakout candle here closed above the high, not just a wick. That matters. A close beyond the level is not proof of a real break. What settles it is what the next few candles do.

Rule from this slide

Do not trade the first break of a range. Wait to see whether price holds outside it or comes back in. If it comes back in, the opposite side of the range becomes the target.

4

Session highs and lows

Each trading session is a block of time with its own high and low. Those levels are easy to see and easy to mark, so stops collect beyond them like any other high or low.

SessionWhat it did on the slideWhat it left
AsiaFell from the top of the chartAsia high far above, Asia low below
LondonMoved sideways. Its low stayed above the Asia lowLondon high and London low, both inside the Asia range
New YorkDropped first, through the London low and the Asia low. Then rallied through the London high and the Asia highNew York low and New York high

New York took the lows of both earlier sessions first. That drop was the visit to liquidity. The rally that followed was the larger move, and its targets were the London high and then the Asia high.

The slide also shows price falling back after the New York high. That high is now liquidity for the next session.

Session hours

The slide gives no clock times. As a rough guide in UTC: Asia about 00:00–09:00, London about 07:00–16:00, New York about 12:00–21:00. For Nairobi, add three hours. These shift by an hour with daylight saving in London and New York, and brokers define them differently. Check the session indicator on your own platform.

The other half

What price does after it takes the liquidity

Marking liquidity tells you where price is likely to go. It does not tell you what happens when it arrives. There are two outcomes, and the candles after the take show which one you have.

CheckSweep (trade the reversal)Real break (stand aside or trade with it)
Where do the next candles close?Back inside the level within a few candlesOutside it, candle after candle
Does structure shift back?Yes. A displacement candle breaks the last swing the other way (MSS)No. Pullbacks stop at or beyond the old level
What is left behind?A fresh zone with an imbalance beside it, pointing back into the rangeA fresh zone pointing in the direction of the break
Does the higher timeframe agree?The take ran into an HTF zone, or against the HTF trendThe take is in the direction of the HTF trend with room to run

The first three rows can be seen on the slides. The fourth is my addition and comes from the case studies, where each sweep happened at a higher-timeframe zone.

“Often” is not “always”

Every slide says price “often” takes the liquidity first. Real breakouts happen too, and from the level alone the two look identical. That is why the entry on this site comes after the shift and not at the level. A sell limit resting at a range high is a bet that the break fails, placed before there is any evidence.

Order placement

Entry, stop loss and take profit

For a short after buy-side liquidity is taken, as on the range slide. For a long after sell-side is taken, mirror every line. The slides draw no positions, so these placements follow the playbook used in the case studies.

Entry

Sell limit at the bottom edge of the supply zone left by the shift.

  • First price must take the high and come back under it.
  • Then a displacement candle must close below the last swing low (MSS), leaving an imbalance.
  • The supply zone is the last up candle, or small base, before that displacement. Rest the order at its near edge.

Stop loss

Above the sweep high, plus spread and a small buffer.

  • The sweep high is where the liquidity was taken. If price goes back above it, the break was real.
  • Not at the old range high or on the trendline. Those are the levels everyone else uses, which is why they were swept.
  • If that stop makes the trade smaller than 1:2 to the target, skip it.

Take profit

The untouched liquidity on the other side.

  • Range: the opposite side of the range.
  • Sessions: the nearest untaken session low, then the next one.
  • Trendline: the lows the line is drawn through. Take profit at the first one if a higher-timeframe demand zone sits in between.

Two uses for every level you mark

Liquidity that has just been taken and rejected gives the entry area. Liquidity that is still untouched in the direction of the trade gives the target. Before placing an order, name both. If you cannot name the liquidity that was taken, there is no entry. If you cannot name the liquidity ahead, there is no target.

Repeatable strategy

Step-by-step for each day

A marking routine to run before the session you trade. It feeds the daily routine in the playbook.

  1. Mark the previous sessions' highs and lows.Asia high and low before London. Asia and London highs and lows before New York. Add yesterday's high and low.
  2. Mark any range.Three or more turns between the same high and low. Draw both sides.
  3. Mark any trendline with three clean touches.Label it as liquidity. Do not buy or sell the touches.
  4. Note which levels have already been taken.A taken level is finished. Only untouched levels are targets.
  5. Check the higher-timeframe bias and zones.The strongest sweep takes liquidity on the side the bias is pushing away from, at an HTF zone. Example: a low taken inside HTF demand in an uptrend.
  6. Wait for price to take a marked level.Set an alert at each level. Do nothing until one triggers.
  7. Watch the next candles.Back inside and a shift with an imbalance: plan the trade. Holding outside: leave it alone.
  8. Place the limit order at the zone the shift left.Stop beyond the sweep extreme. Target the nearest untouched level on the other side. At least 1:2, risk 0.5–1%.
  9. Log it either way.Which type of liquidity, sweep or real break, and what price did afterwards.

Keep this in proportion

The slides are examples picked because the liquidity was taken and price then moved. They do not say how often that happens. When you backtest, count three things for each marked level: taken and reversed, taken and continued, and never reached.

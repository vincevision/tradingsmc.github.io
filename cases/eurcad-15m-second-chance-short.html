The missed short and the second chance

This session started like every other case on the site: a liquidity sweep, a higher-timeframe supply, and a clear short toward the demand below. And the entry was missed. What follows is the part most breakdowns never show — the price leaving without you, the decision not to chase, the second sweep and shift after the supply was tapped, and a re-entry at the ratcheted supply that still paid the original target. The missed trade is the case study.

How to read this page

Six frames from one EUR/CAD session on the 15m, in the order the session unfolded. Three frames show the setup everyone recognises; three show what happens after the entry is missed. The marks — X for sweep, bos for break of structure, the supply boxes, the protected swing — are the ones drawn on the charts. Prices are quoted to five decimal places as displayed; the arrows on the last frame sit at 1.56541.77 in the chart's own notation, which is the broker's price line at the bottom of the move.

Everything the site teaches is present in this first frame. A higher-timeframe supply sits at 1.57800–1.57860. Price rallies into it, and on the way it takes out the high marked X — a liquidity sweep, the fuel, not the signal. Underneath, the swing low at 1.57673 is labelled as the protected swing: the line that, if a candle closes above it later, would cancel the whole idea.

At this point there is no trade. There is a location and a condition to wait for: a reaction from the supply, then a break of structure down on this timeframe. The sweep alone is not an entry — see the sweep model for why a sweep without a shift is just a wick.

The next frame widens the map. Below the HTF supply there is a second complex, drawn at 1.57560–1.57670 and labelled liquidity sweep & supply, with its own X near 1.57590 — the high swept on the way up through it. This is the structure that matters for the second half of the story: when price reacts from the upper supply, this lower complex is what it has to break through, and the liquidity inside it is what the move feeds on.

Stacked supplies in premium with swept highs inside them are a short bias in waiting. Both boxes were drawn before any reaction — the analysis precedes the trade, never the other way round.

The reaction came. Price refused the supply, broke structure down — the bos lines on this frame — and the plan is written on the chart itself: short targeting lower demand. The target is the low of the first drop, near 1.57120, and the demand zone sitting under it. That is the next liquidity point in the direction of the shift, exactly the rule from the origin-and-target note: after a shift, target the next pool, not some distant level on a higher timeframe.

On this frame the setup is complete: HTF supply respected, structure shifted, target named. It is also the frame where the entry was missed.

The first pill is the trade that didn't happen: price reacted from the HTF supply and there was no fill — no limit resting at the edge, no confirmation caught in time. The reaction left without an entry. This is the moment that separates process from gambling, and the chart records the decision: nothing was chased.

Then the second chance formed the only way a second chance ever forms. Price tapped the HTF supply again, swept liquidity on that tap, and shifted down — the second pill names it exactly: liquidity sweep and shift after HTF supply tapped. A fresh sweep plus a fresh shift is a fresh setup, not the old one running late. That distinction is what makes the re-entry legitimate instead of a chase with extra steps.

The rule this frame teaches

A missed entry is not a reason to enter. It is a reason to wait. The market owes you nothing for the move you missed; it only offers the next setup, which arrives on its own terms — sweep, shift, zone. If those three appear again, the trade is new. If they don't, the day is over for this pair.

The shift down after the second tap left its own footprint: a fresh supply zone at 1.57457–1.57557, marked reentry on the chart. Notice what happened structurally — the zone ratcheted down. The first chance would have been sold from the HTF supply near 1.57800; the second chance sells from 1.57557 at worst. Lower entry, same target: the reward-to-risk compresses, and that has to be re-measured before the order goes in, not assumed from the first setup.

The order: sell into the re-entry box, near its lower edge where the shift's own displacement began. Stop above the box and above the sweep high of the leg that made it. Target unchanged — the lower demand, the next liquidity point in the direction of the shift.

The re-entry delivered. Price fell from the 1.57457–1.57557 supply through the lower complex and into the demand below, printing RESULT at 1.56915 with the session lows tagged at 1.56867, 1.56845 and 1.56843. The target from frame 3 — the lower demand — is the same target that paid frame 5. The plan was right the first time; only the entry had to be re-earned.

Worth stating plainly: the missed first short would have paid more. The second chance paid less from a lower entry. Taking less is not failure — taking a chase into the open would have been.

Review

What this case proves

Done right

  • Location first: the HTF supply and the stacked lower complex were marked before any reaction.
  • Sweeps read as fuel, not signals — both X marks preceded shifts.
  • The protected swing (1.57673) named the invalidation early.
  • After the miss: no chase. Waited for a genuine second setup — sweep, shift, new zone.
  • The re-entry re-measured risk from the new, lower entry.
  • The target never changed: the next liquidity point, the demand below.

The lesson in one line

A missed entry is information, not injury. The market that just left without you is the same market that will build a fresh zone on its way — if the shift is real. Your job after a miss is identical to your job before one: wait for sweep, shift, zone, and measure. The only difference is that the entry is lower and the R is smaller, and you have to be honest about both.

Keep this in proportion

One session, one pair, one outcome — and the losing versions of second chances (where the re-entry zone gets run through) are not on these frames. This is an illustration of the recovery process, not evidence of an edge. The evidence is your own log: misses recorded, chases resisted, re-entries measured.

Repeatable steps

How to trade the second chance

  1. Mark the HTF supply or demand and everything stacked inside it.Here: the HTF supply 1.57800–1.57860 and the lower complex 1.57560–1.57670, each with its swept high (X).
  2. Name the protected swing before the reaction.The swing that, if closed beyond, cancels the idea. Here: 1.57673.
  3. Wait for sweep, shift, zone — and if you miss the fill, do nothing.No market order into an extension. A missed entry costs nothing; a chase costs the stop.
  4. Watch for the zone to be tapped again.A second tap of the same HTF supply is the setup's second life. A tap plus a new sweep plus a new shift is a new setup.
  5. Trade the zone the new shift leaves behind.It will have ratcheted in the trade's direction — here, down to 1.57457–1.57557. Enter near the edge where displacement began.
  6. Re-measure everything from the new entry.Stop above the new box and its sweep high. Target unchanged: the next liquidity point in the shift's direction. If the R no longer pays, stand aside.

When to skip

The second chance is not always there

  • If the second tap of the supply produces no sweep and no shift, there is no setup — only a range grinding at your zone. Stand aside.
  • If a candle closes beyond the protected swing (1.57673 here), the whole read is cancelled. Later weakness is a different trade.
  • If the ratcheted zone leaves a reward-to-risk under your floor (1:2 on this site), skip it. The first chance's R does not carry over.
  • If the demand below has already been tapped on the way down, the target is spent — the next pool is farther away and the trade no longer fits the shift.

The gate, both invoices

Same metal, same week, same lines. Yesterday: entry with the inducement unswept, stop inside the toll, −1R before the drop. Today: sweep, shift, then entry behind paid liquidity, +4.23R to the pool. The gate is not a suggestion; it is the price of admission, and this pair of pages is the receipt book.

With the inducement swept and the supply mitigated, nothing rests in front of the move — the same clean air the paying case study had one session earlier. The delivery follows the dashed line like a drawn plan: 3,7250, 3,7150, 3,7050, the target line at 3,694.42 crossed around 10:00, and a printed low near 3,6870 before the first pause. Pools behave like magnets, not walls; the print through is the market confirming the level was liquidity, not support.

Review

What this case proves

Done right

  • One word from the 1h before anything else: short, under a ratcheted protected high.
  • The target was named on the chart that sees it largest — the 1h pool at 3,694.42.
  • The evening rally was treated as a pullback, not a reversal: no longs, no anticipation.
  • Entry waited for both marks: the sweep of ≈ 3,7400 and the bos at ≈ 3,7310.
  • The stop sat behind paid liquidity; the R was measured before the run, at 4.23.
  • The open ending is reported as open — the print through is shown, the exit decision is left to the rules.

The lesson in one line

Confirmation is not delay — it is the price of the stop's location. Waiting for the sweep moved the stop from inside the toll to behind it, and that single change is the difference between −1R and +4.23R on the same metal, one day apart.

Keep this in proportion

One replay session with the tool's maths displayed; the print through is shown but the ticket's close is not. Waiting for confirmation also costs trades — some sweeps never come and some zones leave without you, and those are logged as skips, not as failures of patience. Repeatable steps

Three rungs, one ticket

  1. Take the direction from the hour chart.Last break, protected high, one word. Here: short, under ≈ 3,7915.
  2. Name the target on that same chart.The next untouched pool in the direction of travel: 3,694.42. If no pool, no trade.
  3. Let the 5m label the fuel.Sweeps, inducements, the day's lows and highs. Fuel is information, not entry.
  4. On the 15m, wait for the pullback to reach your supply.The evening rally into ≈ 3,7350–3,7450. Alerts, not anticipation.
  5. Require the two marks.The sweep of the rally's high into the zone (X ≈ 3,7400), then structure away (bos ≈ 3,7310). One mark is not confirmation.
  6. Measure before placing.Stop behind the swept high (3,743.22), target at the pool (3,694.42), ratio on the ticket (4.23). Under 1:2, stand aside.
  7. Write the exit rule before the target prints.Pool reached: scale, move, or hold for the print — decided by rules, logged either way.

Memorise

A liquidity sweep followed by a shift can be enough — but if that liquidity sweep came from a demand or supply zone, the shift that follows adds more probability. Seen three ways in Exhibit D: two shorts on the euro, and the same gate in the mirror for longs.

When to skip

The gate still cuts both ways

  • If the rally taps the supply without sweeping its high, stand aside — yesterday's page is what happens next.
  • If the sweep comes but no bos follows, the toll was paid and the move failed: the zone is suspect, not confirmed.
  • If the protected high (≈ 3,7915 on the 1h) closes at any point, every short below it is cancelled, filled or not.
  • If the pool below has already been taken before your entry, the magnet is spent; re-measure or stand aside.
  • If the stop behind the swept high leaves under 1:2 to the pool, the setup is too far from its target — skip it, log it, move on.

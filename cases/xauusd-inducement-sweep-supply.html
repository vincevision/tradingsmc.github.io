The inducement pays first

Every short on this site waits for liquidity to be taken before the zone is sold. This session draws that waiting line explicitly: an INDUCEMENT line at ≈ 3,787.5, resting just under the lowest supply band of a three-step staircase. Until that line is swept, the band above it is not a setup — it is a magnet with a toll booth in front of it. The toll got paid in one violent V-spike, the band got tapped, and the delivery ran to the sell-side pool at 3,743.48 and through it to 3,737.38.

How to read this page

Four frames from one gold replay: three on the 15-minute and a closing zoom on the 5-minute. The marks are the trader's — SUPPLY and LIQUIDITY SWEEP pills, X on each swept high, bos on each break, the red Protected high and INDUCEMENT lines, the red $$$ pool line, and the white plan arrow. This page is the worked example the Inducement concept page describes in theory: the liquidity sitting between price and your zone, taken before the zone does its work.

The night is a bearish staircase. From above 3,820, each rally runs into a supply box — ≈ 3,808–3,816, then ≈ 3,798–3,804, then ≈ 3,792–3,798 — and each time the rally first sweeps something on its way up: X at ≈ 3,802, X at ≈ 3,800, X at ≈ 3,790, flagged with LIQUIDITY SWEEP pills. Every sweep is fuel spent; every bos underneath is the market confirming it will not pay more. By 01:00 the lowest untouched supply is the wide band at ≈ 3,788–3,792, and the board around it is fully labelled.

Two red lines frame the band. Above, the Protected high at 3,798.24 — a close over it cancels everything below. Below, at ≈ 3,787.5, the INDUCEMENT: the high of the 04:30 bounce, the buy-side resting between price and the zone. That line is the toll booth. A tap of the band that never takes it out would leave stops and early sellers in front of the move; a tap that sweeps it first cleans the path. The white arrow on the frame is the plan in one stroke: up into the band, then down.

Before the setup can trigger, the market writes the target. The 07:30–08:00 crash drops to ≈ 3,742–3,744 and leaves a fresh sell-side pool there — the red $$$ line. Per the origin-and-target rule, that pool is exactly what a shift from the band above should aim at: the next untouched liquidity in the direction of travel, drawn before any order.

Then the trigger, in one move. From the crash low a V-spike lifts straight up: through the bounce highs, sweeping the inducement at 3,787.5, and wicking into the supply band at ≈ 3,790 — all inside a few 15m candles around 08:00–08:45. The toll is paid and the zone is tapped in the same stroke, and the protected high at 3,798.24 is never closed above. A sell limit resting at the band's bottom edge (≈ 3,788–3,790) fills on the way through. This is the violent version of the sequence from the full sequence: sweep, shift, zone — compressed into a spike instead of a grind.

Why the spike is not a reason to chase

A V-spike into supply looks like the move leaving without you. It is not: the limit was resting where the plan said, at the near edge, before the spike began. If you had no limit and watched the spike tap the band, the correct read is still patience — the band stays valid until the protected high closes, and gold came back to it. Chasing a wick that already swept is buying the toll receipt.

What follows the tap is the reason the toll matters. With the inducement swept and the band mitigated, there is no buy-side left in front of the move — nothing for the drop to feed on before the pool. The delivery is clean: a steady bearish run from ≈ 3,790 through 3,760 and 3,750 into the $$$ line at 3,743.48, reached around 11:00–11:30 with a wick a touch beyond. The shaded sweep window on the frame (red box over the band, blue column down the delivery) is the whole trade in one rectangle.

Notice what the run did not do: it did not stall at the 04:30 bounce lows, because their liquidity was already taken on the way up — that is the inducement doing its job from the other side. Liquidity in front of the entry is liquidity paid out of your reward; see the "liquidity in front of the entry" note on the playbook patterns table.

The closing zoom on the 5-minute changes nothing about the read and everything about the texture: the spike's wick into the band, the dashed delivery path, and the moment the $$$ line gives way around 11:15, printing a low at 3,737.38 before the rebound. A pool target is a magnet, not a wall — price usually tags through it, which is why the site's rule is to exit a few pips before a liquidity level when you are taking it, and to let the print confirm when you are holding for the break.

Zooming also shows the inducement's exact job on the smaller chart: the 08:45–09:00 candles take 3,787.53 and only then stall inside the band. On the 5m the toll and the tap are two separate events; on the 15m they merge into one candle. Either way the order of operations is the same, and it is the order this site teaches.

Review

What this case proves

Done right

  • The staircase was mapped supply by supply, with every sweep labelled before the next one printed.
  • The inducement was drawn as a line, not a feeling — ≈ 3,787.5, the bounce high under the band.
  • The protected high (3,798.24) outranked the trade: it never closed, so the short stayed valid through the spike.
  • The target was the crash's $$$ pool, named before the entry and honoured by the delivery.
  • The limit rested at the near edge before the spike; nothing was chased after it.
  • The 5m zoom was used to read the tape, not to move the levels.

The lesson in one line

The liquidity between price and your zone belongs to the setup, not to you. Draw it, name it, and let the market pay it first — a zone tapped with its inducement still resting in front is a zone that will likely sweep you before it moves.

Keep this in proportion

One replay session on gold, a market that spikes through levels retail charts treat as walls. The V-spike fill is the good version; the same limit through a spike that closes above the protected high is a stopped trade, and the frames do not show those. Illustration of the inducement gate, not evidence of an edge.

Repeatable steps

Selling a band with a toll booth in front

  1. Map the staircase, not just the zone.Every supply above yours and every sweep into it. Here: three boxes from 3,820 down, each with its X.
  2. Keep only the untouched band and name its protected high.≈ 3,788–3,792 with 3,798.24 above. A close over it ends the read.
  3. Draw the inducement: the nearest buy-side resting under your supply.The bounce high at ≈ 3,787.5. This line is a gate, not a target and not a stop.
  4. Let the market print your target before you need it.The 07:30 crash left the $$$ pool at 3,743.48. If no pool exists below, the R probably is not there either.
  5. Rest the sell limit at the band's bottom edge and wait.≈ 3,788–3,790, placed before the move that fills it. No limit, no chase: a spike that taps without you is information for the next setup.
  6. Require the toll before you trust the tap.The sweep of 3,787.5 and the wick into the band came together here. A tap that leaves the inducement untouched is a tap to be suspicious of.
  7. Manage at the pool by your written rule.Take a few points before it, or hold for the print through (3,737.38 here) — decided before the tape arrives, per scaling & re-entry.

When to skip

The gate cuts both ways

  • If the band is tapped without sweeping the inducement, stand aside or expect a second, deeper tap — the liquidity in front has not been paid.
  • If the spike closes above the protected high (3,798.24), the short is cancelled mid-flight; the band becomes someone else's demand test.
  • If the crash's pool had already been taken before your entry, the target is spent and the trade has no magnet left.
  • If the stop above the protected high leaves less than 1:2 to the pool, skip — wide bands on gold make thin R easily.
  • If the staircase above is still building fresh supplies un-swept, your band is not the lowest untouched one yet; wait for the staircase to reach you.

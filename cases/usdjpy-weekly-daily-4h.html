How to read this page

These are your screenshots, with your own lines: bos, MSS, X for a sweep, and grey boxes for zones. You sent no notes, so the reasoning is my reading of what you drew. Prices are read from the price axis and are approximate, except the three order prices, which are printed on the chart. The charts are in TradingView replay mode on the FXCM feed, so this is a backtest. Reward-to-risk is calculated from the order prices on the last screenshot.

Step 1 · Weekly bias

Weekly: the 2024 high is broken

What the chart shows

  1. The old high. July 2024, just under 162. Price fell from it to about 140 and spent almost two years below it.
  2. BoS. In mid-2026 weekly candles close above that high and reach about 164.
  3. The pullback. Price drops back fast to about 153, then starts to bounce. It is at 156.85 on this screenshot.

Why it matters

A weekly break of a two-year high sets the direction: longs. The fall afterwards is large in pips, but on the weekly it is a pullback after a break. The weekly does not say where the pullback ends, so the next chart has to.

Rule to carry forward

Take the direction from the highest timeframe that has just broken structure. Then look for a zone on the next timeframe down. Do not buy only because the weekly is bullish.

Step 2 · Daily zone

Daily: back to the demand that started the rally

What the chart shows

  1. The sweep (X). A December 2025 low near 154.3 is taken in late January. Price drops to about 152.1.
  2. MSS. Price then closes above the January high near 159.45. That is the daily shift up.
  3. The demand zone. Drawn at the February low the shift came from, about 152.7 to 153.9.
  4. The return. After the top near 164, price falls about 1,100 pips and reaches the zone in September. The low is about 152.9, inside it.

Why it matters

This is the location. The zone came after a sweep, it produced a break of structure, and price had not returned to it since February. That is a fresh zone by the rules on the supply and demand page.

One detail: the January sweep low near 152.1 sits below the zone. If the zone had failed, that low was the next level down.

Rule to carry forward

A daily zone is a place to start watching. Nothing is bought here yet.

Step 3 · Daily shift

Daily: a break down, a supply zone, then a shift up

What the chart shows

  1. BoS down. The early August low near 155.2 is broken in September. For a while the daily is bearish.
  2. Supply. The high the break came from, about 159.6 to 160.4, is marked as a supply zone.
  3. Reaction at demand. Price spends several days inside the February demand zone without closing below it.
  4. MSS up. Price closes above the last lower high near 156.75, with a wick to about 158.

Why it matters

Two things are settled here. The daily demand held and the daily has turned back up, so the weekly direction and the daily now agree. And the trade has a ceiling: a fresh daily supply zone at 159.6. A long from below should be closed there.

Mark the opposing zone before the entry

The supply zone was drawn weeks before the trade. The target was known first, and the entry had to pay enough to reach it.

Step 4 · The zone to trade from

Daily: the shift leaves a new demand zone

What the chart shows

  1. The rally from demand. A run of up candles from about 153 to 158.
  2. The new zone. About 155.3 to 156.3, at the base of the push that broke the MSS line.
  3. Price now. Above the zone at 156.85, with a long upper wick.

Why it matters

Price is too far from the February zone to buy there with a sensible stop. The new zone is the place to wait for a pullback. It is closer to the target, so the stop has to be small, and that needs the 4H.

Step 5 · 4H structure

4H: the shift, seen up close

What the chart shows

  1. The drop. From the supply zone near 160 to the daily demand near 153 in about a week.
  2. A pause on the way. A low near 155.3 (marked X) and a bounce to 156.75. That bounce high is the level the MSS line is drawn from.
  3. The base. Several days of lows around 153 inside the daily demand zone.
  4. MSS. Price climbs back and closes above 156.75, reaching about 158.

Why it matters

The 4H agrees with the daily. The demand zone at 155.3 to 156.3 is now the area to watch, and the plan is to wait for price to come back to it.

Step 6 · The wait

4H: a month of range, then price reaches the zone

What the chart shows

  1. The range. From about 20 September to 21 October price moves between roughly 156.4 and 159. It does not reach the demand zone and does not reach the supply zone.
  2. The drop. Around 21–22 October price falls out of the range and wicks to about 156.1, into the top of the zone.
  3. The level to beat. A small MSS line at about 157.35, the last lower high of the drop.

Why it matters

About a month passed between the shift and this return. There was no trade in that time, because price was in the middle with the zone below and the supply above. The lows of the range, just above the zone, were building up stops the whole time.

Rule to carry forward

No trade in the middle of a range. Wait for price to reach the zone.

Step 7 · Sweep and shift

4H: the range low is swept, then the shift

What the chart shows

  1. The liquidity. The range low of about 29–30 September, near 156.38. Three weeks of trading sat above it.
  2. The sweep (X). A wick to about 156.07 takes that low and reaches the top of the demand zone. The candles close back above the line.
  3. MSS. Price closes above 157.35.
  4. The entry zone. A small 4H demand zone at about 156.65 to 157.15, the base of the move that made the shift.

Why it matters

This is the confirmation. The sweep happened at the zone, not above it, and the shift followed straight after. The small zone gives an entry with a stop of about 110 pips. An entry at the larger zone would have needed a stop of about 200.

Rule to carry forward

At the zone, wait for a low to be swept and for a close above the last lower high. Then use the small zone that move leaves.

Step 8 · The order

4H: entry, stop and target

What the chart shows

  • Entry: buy limit at 157.156, the top edge of the small 4H demand zone.
  • Stop loss: 156.069, under the sweep wick. That is about 109 pips.
  • Take profit: 159.599, the bottom edge of the daily supply zone. That is about 244 pips.
  • Reward to risk: about 1:2.25.

Why each level is where it is

The entry is the near edge of the zone, where the other trades on this site were filled. The stop is beyond the sweep low: if price goes back under 156.07, the sweep was a real break and the idea is wrong. The target is the first opposing zone, marked on the daily before the trade existed.

The buffer is thin

The sweep wick is at about 156.07 and the stop at 156.069. That leaves about a pip. Spread alone can be more than that. A buffer of 5 to 10 pips puts the stop near 156.00 and the ratio at about 1:2.1.

Step 9 · Result

4H: filled at the edge, target reached

What the chart shows

  1. The fill. Price comes back to the zone a few days after the shift. The wick goes about 10 pips past the entry and turns.
  2. The hold. Roughly a week of sideways trading between 157.3 and 158.8. Price never came near the stop.
  3. The target. A strong push reaches 159.587 and continues into the supply zone, to about 160.05.

What to take from it

  • The fill only just reached the zone. A limit in the middle of it would have been missed.
  • The trade took about two weeks from fill to target. This is a swing trade and it has to be sized and left alone like one.
  • Price went straight into the supply zone after the target. Closing at the bottom edge was the plan, and what the zone does next is a separate trade.

The dates after 27 October are hidden by the toolbar. I estimate the shift at about 26 October, the fill at about 30 October and the target at about 10–11 November, from the spacing of the candles.

Order placement

Entry, stop loss and take profit

Entry

Buy limit at 157.144, the top edge of the small 4H demand zone.

  • The zone is the base of the move that closed above 157.35.
  • Place the order when that 4H candle has closed.
  • The fill went about 10 pips past the edge. A deeper order would have missed.

Stop loss

156.057, under the sweep wick. About 109 pips.

  • Under 156.07 the sweep has become a break, and the reason for the trade is gone.
  • The buffer here is about a pip. Use 5 to 10 pips plus the spread.
  • The wider choice is under the 155.3–156.3 zone. That is about 190 pips and brings the ratio to about 1:1.3, which is too low.

Take profit

159.587, the bottom edge of the daily supply zone. About 244 pips.

  • The first opposing zone above the entry, and a fresh one.
  • The range highs near 159 sit just under it. They are taken on the way.
  • The order is a pip or two under the zone edge, so the zone does not have to be entered.

Position size with a 109-pip stop

On USD/JPY one standard lot moves ¥1,000 per pip, about $6.36 at 157.14. A 109-pip stop therefore costs about $693 per lot. To risk 1% of a $1,000 account ($10) the size is 10 ÷ 693 ≈ 0.014, so 0.01 lots, which risks about $6.90. On a $10,000 account, 1% is about 0.14 lots. A swing stop means a small position. Do not shrink the stop to fit a bigger one.

Review

What was done well, and what to tighten

Done well

  • Direction came from the weekly, and the daily had to agree before anything was planned.
  • The target zone was marked on the daily weeks before the entry.
  • A month of range was sat out. No order was placed in the middle.
  • The entry waited for a sweep at the zone and a shift after it.
  • The stop is behind the sweep, where the idea is wrong, and the target is the first opposing zone.

To tighten

  • Stop buffer. About a pip under the wick. Add 5 to 10 pips plus spread.
  • Ratio. About 1:2.25 is the lowest on this site, and about 1:2.1 with a proper buffer. It passes the 1:2 rule with little to spare. At 1:2.25 you need to win about 31% of the time to break even, before costs.
  • The daily had broken down. Between early and mid-September the daily was bearish under the 155.2 break. The trade was only valid after the daily MSS. Keep that as a written condition.
  • Swap. A two-week hold pays or earns overnight interest. Check the rate on your broker and include it in the journal.
  • Replay. Draw the zones before stepping the chart forward, and record the trades that lose as well. One win says the method can work once.

Repeatable strategy

Step-by-step for the next trade

Written for longs. For shorts, mirror it. A “no” at any step ends the process.

  1. Weekly: has price just closed beyond a major high?Write “long”. If the weekly is inside a range, use the daily for direction and halve the size.
  2. Daily: is price pulling back into a fresh demand zone that produced a break?Mark it. Mark the nearest supply zone above as the target.
  3. Daily: has price reacted and closed above its last lower high?Draw the new demand zone at the base of that move.
  4. Is price back at that zone?If it is ranging above it, wait. Set an alert at the top edge.
  5. 4H: has a low been swept into the zone, with a close back above?Mark the sweep low.
  6. 4H: has a candle closed above the last lower high?Draw the small zone at the base of that move.
  7. Buy limit at the top of the small zone, stop under the sweep low plus 5–10 pips.Target the bottom edge of the daily supply.
  8. Does it pay at least 1:2 with the buffer included?If not, skip it.
  9. Size for the wide stop, place, hold, record.Risk 0.5–1%. Expect to hold for days or weeks. Screenshot the weekly, daily and 4H.

Filters

When this setup should be skipped

Do not take it if

  • A daily candle closes below the daily demand zone.
  • The daily has not closed back above its last lower high.
  • Price is in the middle of the range, between the demand and the supply.
  • The 4H breaks up without first sweeping a low at the zone.
  • The daily supply is closer than 1:2 from the entry.
  • A Bank of Japan or Federal Reserve decision, or US jobs or inflation data, is due before the order is likely to fill.
  • The position needed for the stop is below your broker’s minimum size at the risk you allow.

This trade had all of these

  • A weekly break of structure upward.
  • A fresh daily demand zone that held.
  • A daily shift up and a marked supply zone above.
  • A swept range low at the zone.
  • A 4H shift and a small zone to enter from.
  • A target about 2.25R away.

Keep this in proportion

USD/JPY at these levels has a history of sudden moves of several hundred pips in a day, and the fall from 164 on these charts is one of them. A stop order can be filled well beyond its price in a move like that. Keep the risk per trade small enough that a slipped stop is survivable.

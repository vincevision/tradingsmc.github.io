What Happens After the Sweep

A long on Bitcoin, and a lesson in waiting. Price swept a low, shifted structure and rallied hard. It looked like the move had left without anyone who had not chased it. Nine hours later it came all the way back to the order block, filled the resting order and then ran to the highs.

  • Long
  • 15m only
  • Target: buy-side liquidity
  • TP hit · ≈ 1:3.5

Step 1 · Sweep, shift and the temptation

After the sweep, the move looks obvious

What happened

  1. Price sold off through a staircase of lows. The dashed lines on the left mark earlier lows, each one broken in turn. The olive box is not labelled; it looks like an earlier demand zone that price drove straight through.
  2. The final low was a sweep (X). After the large bearish candle, price bounced, then a wick reached slightly lower and was rejected. That wick's low becomes the protected low.
  3. MSS. One large bullish candle closed above the high of the bounce. The 15m downtrend is over. The candle left an imbalance (IMB) at its base.
  4. The order block is drawn wide. The grey OB box runs from just under the imbalance all the way down to the protected low. It covers the whole base that the shift launched from.
  5. A violent pullback stopped short ($$). After a first rally, one big bearish candle fell most of the way back and turned just above the order block. Its low is marked as liquidity.
  6. BoS. The next candles reversed that entire drop and broke to a new high.

Why this is the dangerous moment

At the end of this slide everything agrees: sweep, shift, break of structure, strong candles. The order block was never touched, so a trader with a limit order there has no position, and price is far above. This is where the post says “you want to enter now”.

Two things on the chart argue for waiting. The order block is still fresh. And there is now a clean low marked $$ sitting directly above it, with the stops of everyone who bought that dip resting underneath.

Rule to carry forward

A pullback that turns just short of your order block has not cancelled the setup. It has added liquidity in front of it. Leave the order where it is.

Common mistake

Buying the breakout at the highs with a stop under the $$ low. That stop sits exactly where price is most likely to go next.

Step 2 · The plan

Wait for price to return to the zone

What is marked

  • BSL. The rally topped and price began to range. The line across that top marks the buy-side liquidity above it. This is the target, and it did not exist when the shift happened. It was set once the high had formed.
  • $$. The low of the earlier spike down, still untouched, just above the order block.
  • OB and protected low. Unchanged from slide 1.
  • The position. Entry at the top edge of the order block. Stop a little below the protected low. Target on the BSL line.

Why the plan is built this way

The order block is tall, so the stop cannot hide just beneath a thin zone as it did on gold. It goes under the protected low, which is also the bottom of the block. One level does both jobs: if it breaks, the order block has failed and the shift has failed.

Even with that wider stop, the distance from the top of the block to the BSL high is about 3.5 times the risk. The rally that tempted people to chase is the same rally that created the reward.

Rule to carry forward

Waiting with a plan means four things are written down before price returns: the entry price, the stop, the target, and what would cancel the order. If any one is missing, you are just watching.

Step 3 · Fill, management and outcome

Price taps the zone, then reaches the target

What happened

  1. Price fell out of the range fast. Several large bearish candles in a row, starting around 09:00 on the chart.
  2. The $$ low was taken. Every stop under the earlier dip was triggered on the way down.
  3. The order filled, and price kept going. Wicks reached more than halfway through the order block. The trade was in drawdown almost immediately.
  4. The protected low held. Price never reached it. Two candles left long lower wicks inside the block, then buyers took over.
  5. Target reached and exceeded. Price rallied straight back to the BSL high, went through it, and continued up to the dotted line above.

What to take from it

  • The wait was about nine hours. The shift came around midnight on the chart and the fill a little after 09:00.
  • This fill was deep. In the other three cases price only touched the near edge of the order block. Here it went well inside. A stop placed under the top half of the block would have been hit before the reversal.
  • The entry was into strong selling. The candles arriving at the order block were large and bearish. A limit order takes that trade without hesitation. A trader waiting to “see a reaction” would have entered much higher, or not at all.
  • A strict reading would call the drop a bearish shift. The $$ low was the higher low behind the last break up, and price traded below it. The post treats it as liquidity. What kept the long valid was the protected low underneath. That is the line to watch.
  • Price went beyond the target. Taking profit at the BSL left some of the move behind. That is the cost of a fixed target, and the plan was still met in full.

Rule to carry forward

Size the trade so that price trading through most of the order block does not worry you. The stop belongs under the protected low, and the position should be small enough that you can leave it there.

The logic in one sentence

After a sweep and a shift, leave a buy limit at the order block with the stop under the protected low, and let price come back for the liquidity in front of it, however long that takes.

Order placement

Entry, stop loss and take profit

Entry

Buy limit at the top edge of the order block.

  • Placed after the MSS and left in place through the near miss and the rally.
  • The imbalance ends at the top of the block, so that edge is the first price a full pullback reaches.
  • Price went much deeper here. Entering at the top edge still worked because the stop was under the whole block.
  • A lower entry, say mid-block, would have filled too and paid more per unit of risk. It would have missed the three earlier cases. Choose one rule and keep it.

Stop loss

Below the protected low, plus a buffer.

  • The protected low is the sweep low (X) and also the bottom of the order block.
  • If it breaks, the liquidity sweep has been undone and the bullish shift is void.
  • The grey box extends slightly past the line. That gap is the buffer.
  • Do not tighten it to the middle of the block. This trade would have been stopped out before the reversal.

Take profit

The BSL line: the high made after the shift. About 3.5R.

  • No opposing supply zone is marked. The target is the stops above the most recent high.
  • The target was set after that high formed, not at the moment of the shift.
  • Price ran through it here. In GBP/JPY it only touched. Setting the order a few pips before the level covers both.
  • If you want to stay in for more, close most at the BSL and trail the rest. Decide before entry.

Managing the position

The post shows a single entry and a single exit. The points below are my suggested handling, not something marked on the slides.

  • While waiting: cancel only if the protected low breaks. A new high without you is not a reason to move the order up.
  • Re-measure when the target forms. Once price prints the high you will aim at, check the trade still pays at least 1:2 from the order block.
  • Once filled: expect drawdown inside the block. Do nothing unless the stop is hit.

Position size on Bitcoin

Fix the money at risk first, then let the stop distance decide the size.

Size in BTC = money at risk ÷ stop distance in dollars

Example: $10,000 account, 1% risk = $100. A stop $400 away from entry gives 100 ÷ 400 = 0.25 BTC. At 1:3.5 the target pays about $350.

Check what 0.25 BTC is worth at the current price: it can be more than the account, which means leverage. The risk is still $100 only if the stop actually executes at its price. Bitcoin trades through weekends when liquidity is thin and stops can slip, and perpetual contracts charge funding while you wait in a position.

Repeatable strategy

Step-by-step for the next trade

Written for longs. For shorts, mirror it. A “no” at any step ends the process.

  1. Check the higher timeframe first.This post does not show one. Before using the 15m, know whether the 1H and 4H are trending, and whether a higher-timeframe low has just been taken.
  2. Was a low swept and rejected?A wick below the previous low that closes back above. Mark that wick's low as the protected low.
  3. Did a candle close above the bounce high with displacement?This is the MSS. It should leave an imbalance at its base.
  4. Draw the order block.From under the imbalance down to the protected low. Place the buy limit at its top edge.
  5. Do not chase.If price rallies without filling you, leave the order. If a pullback turns just short of the block, mark its low as liquidity and still leave the order.
  6. Set the target once a high has formed.The most recent swing high above is the buy-side liquidity. Place the take profit a few pips before it.
  7. Does it pay at least 1:2?Entry at the top of the block, stop under the protected low, target at the high. If not, cancel.
  8. Size for the full stop.0.5–1% of the account at risk, measured to below the protected low. Assume price will trade deep into the block.
  9. Hold, then record it.Exit at target or stop. Log how long you waited and how deep the fill went.

Filters

When this setup should be skipped

Do not take it if

  • The protected low has been broken. The setup is finished.
  • The order block has already been tapped once and price is returning a second time.
  • The shift came without a sweep before it.
  • The shift candle left no imbalance.
  • The higher timeframe is in a clean downtrend with no low taken. A 15m shift alone is thin evidence.
  • The nearest high is closer than 1:2 from the order block.
  • The order block is so tall that a stop under it is larger than you are willing to hold.

This trade had all of these

  • A sweep of the low, rejected by a wick.
  • A 15m MSS on a displacement candle with an imbalance.
  • A later BoS confirming the shift.
  • An untouched order block resting on a clear protected low.
  • A liquidity low sitting just in front of the block.
  • A swing high above, about 3.5R away.

Keep this in proportion

“Price often comes back first” is true often, not always. Sometimes it does not come back and the trade is missed. Sometimes it comes back and goes straight through the protected low. The post shows the time waiting was rewarded. Count all three outcomes in your backtest, including the missed moves, before deciding how much this patience is worth.

Frame 1 · Context

The 1h keeps the ceiling

What the frame shows

  1. A staircase out of the top. From above 3,660 on the 19th, price falls in legs with no meaningful retracement — the 1h is bearish before any mark is drawn.
  2. One wide supply, ≈ 3,584–3,626. The box is drawn at the origin of the last drop and stretched right: every rally this session has to prove itself against it.
  3. The first rally dies at the floor. Under the cursor, price tags ≈ 3,586.93 — the zone’s lower edge — and stalls. Nothing is shorted here: a tag is not a trigger.

Why it comes first

Because the 1h box decides the side and the area, and nothing else. The 30m will supply the timing, but only inside this ceiling. A rally that closes above 3,626 is a new chart, not a better discount.

Rule to carry forward

Mark the higher-timeframe zone once and leave it alone. Its job is to forbid the wrong side and to catch the pullbacks; its floor is where the lower timeframe goes to work.

Frame 2 · Fuel and shift

The 30m sweeps, then shifts

What the frame shows

  1. The 19th prints the pool. Lows near 3,520–3,525 with wicks to 3,519.25: sell-side liquidity, marked with a white line before any entry exists.
  2. The pullback’s high is swept (X). On the 20th, 03:00–06:00, the rally tags the zone’s floor and takes ≈ 3,590 — the buys that chased the bounce are cleared above it.
  3. MSS at ≈ 3,572. Across 04:30–07:30 price closes below the pullback’s last higher low, leaving the bracket and the blue-tagged level. The shift is the gate; until it prints there is no trade, only a ceiling and a hope.

Why it comes second

The sweep alone would have offered a sell at ≈ 3,590 with the pullback’s own highs still unswept above the entry — the exact invoice of the stopped case. The MSS turns the zone from an area into a side, and the 3,519.25 line turns the side into a measured trade.

Rule to carry forward

Sweep first, shift second, order third — never in another order. The X is the reason to watch; the MSS is the reason to work; the entry is only the price.

Frame 3 · The order

The tap writes the ticket

What the frame shows

  1. The post-shift drop stalls. Near 3,552–3,555 the MSS leg exhausts; the retracement that follows is the entry window, not a reversal.
  2. The red 30m OB, ≈ 3,584–3,596. The block the shift left behind, sitting exactly on the 1h zone’s floor: two timeframes agreeing on one price band.
  3. The tap, 20th 08:00–09:00. Price trades into the block and the sell limit fills at 3,584.00 — the block’s lower edge, not its middle.
  4. The column to 3,533.78. Entry at the top, target at the bottom: the reward drawn before the order, the SSL cluster above the 3,519.25 low.

Why it comes third

Because the entry is the last decision and the least important one. The side came from the 1h, the timing from the MSS, the target from the 19th’s low; the tap only names the price. Selling the first tag in frame 1 would have meant carrying two days of pullback; selling the X in frame 2 would have meant selling into the retracement that became the entry.

Orders, as tagged

Entry: 3,584.00 — sell limit at the 30m OB’s lower edge, on the 1h floor.
Stop: 3,595.95 — above the protected high the sweep made, beyond the structure the shift depends on. Risk 11.95, 0.33%.
Target: 3,533.78 — the sell-side cluster below, booked above the 3,519.25 low. Reward 50.22, 1.40%. About 4.2 R.

Frame 4 · Delivery

Paid, and printed through

What the frame shows

  1. The tap fails immediately. One candle inside the block and price is back under it — the defence the shift promised.
  2. Through the column. 3,560, 3,545, then the target 3,533.78 crossed around the 20th, 13:30–15:00.
  3. A print to 3,523.46. The move overshoots the booked target into the 23rd, stopping short of the 3,519.25 low. Nothing was moved to catch it.

Why the overshoot matters less than the book

The print through is luck; the 4.2 R to the booked target is the strategy. Moving the target to 3,519.25 because it was on the chart would have turned a measured trade into a wish — and the next tap that fails would have been held twice as long for half the reason.

Keep it in proportion

Three winning gold replays in a row — this one, confirmation pays and the 15m retest — describe a style, not an edge. The stopped case sits beside them on purpose. Backtest the gate on fifty setups before the size goes up.

Review

What earned the print, and what to watch

  • The gate was waited for, twice. The first tag at the 1h floor produced no order, and neither did the X. Only the MSS opened the ticket window. That is note 12 in three frames.
  • The target existed before the entry. The 3,519.25 line was drawn on the 19th; the booked 3,533.78 sits above it. Nothing was moved during the trade, including during the overshoot.
  • The stop sat beyond structure, not inside the block. 3,595.95 is above the protected high; a stop at the block’s top edge would have been swept by the tap’s own wick.
  • Watch: no imbalance is labelled. The MSS bracket is on the chart but no IMB arrow beside it. The shift was taken on the close alone — by the site’s own definition that is a weaker shift. On a chart where the gap is missing and the tap is deep, stand aside.
  • Watch: the entry sat on a confluence. The 30m OB and the 1h floor agreeing made this tap cleaner than most. A 30m block floating in the middle of the 1h zone is a different, worse trade with the same marks.

Repeatable

The sequence, as this trade ran it

  1. Draw the higher-timeframe zone once. The 1h supply at the origin of the last drop; stretch it right and leave it alone.
  2. Mark the pool below. The low the first drop prints is the target’s address — here 3,519.25, booked later at 3,533.78.
  3. Wait for the sweep into the zone’s floor. The X on the pullback’s high. No sweep, no watch.
  4. Wait for the MSS on a close. Below the pullback’s last higher low, with an imbalance if the chart offers one. A wick is not a shift.
  5. Sell the tap of the block the shift left. Limit at its lower edge; stop above the protected high; target at the booked pool.
  6. Journal the four frames. Context, sweep-and-shift, ticket, delivery. A trade without the first two frames is a trade without a reason.

The same six lines as the routine on key points: the higher timeframe supplies the marks, the shift supplies the timing, the shift’s timeframe supplies the target.

Memorise

A liquidity sweep followed by a shift can be enough — but if that liquidity sweep came from a demand or supply zone, the shift that follows adds more probability. Seen three ways in Exhibit D: two shorts on the euro, and the same gate in the mirror for longs.

Discipline

When this trade does not exist

  • No sweep into the zone. A pullback that tags the floor without taking a high first leaves its buys above your entry — inducement, per the stopped case.
  • No MSS before the tap. A tap of the zone with the pullback’s structure intact is an aggressive entry, a different model with a different failure rate.
  • The tap goes through the block. A close above the protected high cancels the shift; the order comes off, or never goes on.
  • The pool is too close. Under 1:2 from the block’s edge to the booked target, the gate passed but the trade does not pay for its own stops.

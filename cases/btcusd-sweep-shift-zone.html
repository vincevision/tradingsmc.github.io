Sweep, Shift and Zone

A long on Bitcoin that spells out the order of events behind every trade on this site. First liquidity is swept. Then structure shifts. Only then is there a zone worth buying. Here the sequence runs on the 4H for bias and again on the 1H for the entry.

How to read this page

X marks a liquidity sweep. EQL is equal lows. BSL is buy-side liquidity. The slides have no price axis, so levels are described by structure. Reward-to-risk is measured from the position boxes. A dotted horizontal line on each chart is unlabelled and I have not built anything on it.

The model

The sequence, in three words

  1. Sweep. Price makes a low just above the HTF demand, bounces, then drops into the zone and takes that low (X). Buyers who entered on the first touch are stopped out here.
  2. Shift. Price rallies and closes above the high of the bounce (MSS). The lower timeframe has turned.
  3. Zone. The base of the leg that made the shift becomes the LTF demand. Price pulls back into it, and that is the entry.

Why the order matters

The caption describes the trader who gets the zone right and the order wrong. They buy when price first reaches HTF demand. That is before the sweep. Price drops a little more to take the low, their stop goes with it, and the move starts without them.

The same zone traded in the right order gives a different trade: the entry comes after the low is taken and the shift is confirmed, from a smaller zone with a stop under a low that has already been swept.

Rule to carry forward

Reaching the zone is step zero. The trade needs three events after that, in this order: a low is swept, structure shifts, price returns to the zone the shift left behind.

Step 1 · Bias

4H: the sequence gives the bias

What happened

  1. An earlier sweep and rally. On the left, a long wick takes a low (the first X) inside the olive demand zone and price rallies hard out of it.
  2. The rally tops out (BSL). The line across that high marks buy-side liquidity. It is the obvious draw above.
  3. A sharp drop, then a range. One large bearish candle gives back most of the rally. Price then goes sideways for two days, leaving lows at a similar level.
  4. The range lows are swept (second X). A push down runs under those lows and is bought back within the same candle.
  5. MSS. The final candle closes above the most recent swing high. The 4H has completed sweep and shift.

Why this matters

The 4H now says two things: sell-side liquidity under the range has been taken, and structure has turned up. With the BSL high still untouched above, the bias is long and the target is known before the entry timeframe is opened.

Rule to carry forward

Take a long bias from the 4H only when both halves are present: a low swept and a close above the last swing high. Mark the nearest untouched high above as the draw.

Step 2 · Confirmation

1H: equal lows swept into demand, then the shift

What happened

  1. A wide demand zone sits below. The box labelled Demand is the base of the first rally, extended to the right.
  2. Price made equal lows above it (EQL). Two lows roughly a day apart stopped at almost the same price, just over the zone. Each bounce encouraged buyers, and their stops sit under that double bottom.
  3. The equal lows were swept into the zone. A drop ran through both lows and its wick dipped into the top of the demand before price turned.
  4. MSS. The rally that followed closed above the previous swing high.

Why this matters

This is the cover drawing on a real chart. Price hovered above the zone and built liquidity. It then took that liquidity and tapped the zone in the same move. Anyone who bought either of the equal lows was stopped at the worst possible moment.

Rule to carry forward

Equal lows resting just above a demand zone are a target, not support. Wait for them to be run into the zone and for a 1H close above the last swing high before planning the entry.

Step 3 · Zone and order placement

1H: the zone the shift left behind

What is marked

  • X and the line beneath it. The sweep of the lows, and a line drawn from the sweep's own low. The post does not label that line. It is the level the stop sits under.
  • MSS. The break of the previous high.
  • IMB. The imbalance left by the rally, above the zone.
  • Demand. A narrow zone at the base of the leg that made the shift. This is the third word of the title.
  • BSL. After the shift price ran up and made a high. The line across it is the target.
  • A short line under the pullback. Price has come off the high and paused. The low of that pause is marked with an unlabelled line. It reads as liquidity sitting between price and the zone.

Why the entry is here

Entry is a buy limit at the top edge of the 1H demand. The stop goes below the sweep low, a little under the bottom of the zone. From entry to the BSL line is about 3.4 times that risk.

At the moment of this slide, price is far above the zone and has already made a new high. The plan is to wait for it to come back.

Rule to carry forward

The zone to trade is the one created by the shift, not the larger zone price was in when the shift happened. It is smaller, it gives a tighter stop, and it sits above a low that has already been swept.

Step 4 · Fill, management and outcome

Result

What happened

  1. Price ranged at the pullback low. Several hours of small candles along the unlabelled line.
  2. One wick took that low and tagged the zone. It reached just inside the top of the demand and closed well above. The buy limit filled on that wick.
  3. A bounce, then a second dip. Price rallied, then drifted back down for several hours. It made a higher low and did not return to the zone.
  4. Expansion. A run of large bullish candles carried price to the BSL line and through it.

What to take from it

  • The wait was about a day. On a 1H chart the shift and the fill were roughly 26 hours apart. The move to target took around 13 more.
  • The fill was a wick at the edge. A deeper order would not have filled.
  • Liquidity above the zone was taken first. The same behaviour as in the GBP/JPY, AUD/USD and first Bitcoin cases.
  • The second dip tested patience. For hours after the fill the trade gave back most of its early profit. Nothing structural had changed: the zone and the sweep low were intact.

Rule to carry forward

After the fill, the only levels that matter are the stop and the target. A slow drift back toward entry is not a signal.

THE LOGIC IN ONE SENTENCE

Do not buy the zone when price reaches it. Buy the zone that is left after the low has been swept and structure has shifted.

Order placement

Entry, stop loss and take profit

Entry

Buy limit at the top edge of the 1H demand zone.

  • The zone is the base of the leg that made the 1H MSS, under the imbalance.
  • Place it once the shift is confirmed and leave it.
  • Only a wick reached the edge. A mid-zone order would have been missed.

Stop loss

Below the sweep low, plus a buffer.

  • The sweep low is the wick that took the equal lows. On the entry slide a line is drawn from it and the grey box ends just beneath.
  • It sits a little under the bottom of the demand zone, so the stop covers the zone and the low together.
  • If that low breaks, the sweep has been undone and the shift is void.

Take profit

The BSL line: the high above. About 3.4R.

  • On the 1H slides the line is drawn across the high price made after the shift.
  • The 4H slide also marks BSL at a high, so the bias chart and the entry chart point at the same area.
  • Price went through the level. Set the order a little before it regardless.

Managing the position

The post shows a single entry and a single exit. The points below are my suggested handling, not something marked on the slides.

  • While waiting: cancel if the sweep low breaks, or if price takes the BSL without filling you.
  • Once filled: hold through the retest. Move the stop only after a new 1H high and a confirmed higher low.
  • A day is a long time on Bitcoin. Check for scheduled news and for weekend gaps in liquidity while the order rests.

Position size on Bitcoin

Fix the money at risk first, then let the stop distance decide the size.

Size in BTC = money at risk ÷ stop distance in dollars

Example: $10,000 account, 1% risk = $100. A stop $800 away from entry gives 100 ÷ 800 = 0.125 BTC. At 1:3.4 the target pays about $340.

A 1H zone usually means a wider stop in dollars than a 15m one, so the size is smaller for the same risk. Check the position's full value against your account: it may involve leverage, and stops can slip in thin markets.

Repeatable strategy

Step-by-step for the next trade

Written for longs. For shorts, mirror it. A “no” at any step ends the process.

  1. 4H: has a low been swept and has price closed above the last swing high?Both. Then mark the nearest untouched high above as the draw.
  2. 1H: mark the demand zone below and the lows resting above it.Equal lows or a clean swing low just over the zone are the liquidity.
  3. Sweep: have those lows been taken, into or onto the zone?Not yet means no trade yet, even if price is sitting on the zone.
  4. Shift: has a 1H candle closed above the last swing high?It should leave an imbalance.
  5. Zone: mark the demand at the base of the shift.Under the imbalance, above the sweep low. It must be untouched.
  6. Does it pay at least 1:2 to the high above?Entry at the zone's top edge, stop under the sweep low, target just before the BSL.
  7. Place the orders and wait.Size for 0.5–1% risk. On the 1H the wait can be a day or more.
  8. Hold, then record it.Exit at target or stop. Note whether the sweep, the shift and the zone came in that order.

Filters

When this setup should be skipped

Do not take it if

  • The steps are out of order: a shift with no sweep before it, or a zone with no shift.
  • The 4H has swept a low and not yet closed above a swing high.
  • The lows above the 1H zone are still intact.
  • The 1H demand has already been tapped since the shift.
  • The sweep low breaks.
  • The high above is closer than 1:2 from the zone.

This trade had all of these

  • 4H sweep of the range lows and a 4H MSS.
  • 1H equal lows swept into demand.
  • 1H MSS with an imbalance.
  • An untouched 1H demand at the base of the shift.
  • A pullback low above the zone, taken on the fill.
  • A clear high about 3.4R away.

Keep this in proportion

“The only sequence you need to know” is the post's headline, not a guarantee. The sequence describes how these winning trades were built. It also appears before trades that fail: the low is swept, structure shifts, the zone is tapped, and price goes through it anyway. The sequence tells you when a trade is worth taking. Position size and the stop are what keep the failures small.

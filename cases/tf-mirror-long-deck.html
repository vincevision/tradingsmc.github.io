How to read this page

Slide by slide: the H4 names the fuel (the X sweep at the wick low) and stalls; the M15 names the location (supply above, demand below) and the confirmation (tap, MSS, BoS); the entry slide names the address (OB and blue zone) and the trigger (the SSS sweep before the final push). Read it against case 26: same deck grammar, mirrored direction — which is the point of the mirror on this site.

Step 1 · Higher-timeframe structure

H4: the sweep that ends the fall

No zones on this slide, and none needed: its job is to name the fuel and stall the tape.

The H4 falls into its 20th–22nd low, rallies through a BoS at the 24th’s high, and then fails to continue: the 25th’s push prints a lower high, and the fall that follows runs long enough to sweep the X — sell-side liquidity resting at the 24th’s pullback low — before a doji stalls everything on the 26th.

Read against the house sequence, that wick is the prior sweep: liquidity collected at a low, after a failed higher high, with the tape refusing to continue down. It does not by itself buy anything — a sweep in no-man’s-land is the “enough” half of the memorised rule at best. What turns it into a long is the next slide, where the same hours carry a zone.

Step 2 · Location and confirmation

M15: two taps and the shift

The slide that makes this a trade: the demand gets tapped, and the tap gets answered.

One rung down, the same hours carry two boxes. The rose supply spans the top of the card; its left edge is tapped at ≈ 09:00 and the fall that leaves runs to the 12:00 wick, sweeping the X at the prior low — the M15’s own copy of the H4’s fuel sweep. Then the green demand, sitting mid-card where the 13:30 rally’s origin candles live, and the 19:30 drop’s wick into it: the tap.

What follows is the sentence this site memorises, in long form: the bounce off the tapped demand prints MSS, the 26th’s push prints BoS above the 23:30 high, and the shift therefore carries the demand’s defenders’ weight — a sweep taken from a zone, answered by a shift. Contrast the two taps on this one slide: the supply’s tap at 09:00 was answered by a fall, the demand’s tap at 19:30 by a rally. Zones vote; the shift is how the vote gets counted.

Memorise

A liquidity sweep followed by a shift can be enough — but if that liquidity sweep came from a demand or supply zone, the shift that follows adds more probability. The 19:30 wick took the demand’s own floor; the MSS and BoS that follow are that rule, mirrored for longs.

Step 3 · The entry

M15: the entry setup

Everything the order needs, drawn before the order exists: a swept low, a block, a zone, and a target already on the card.

After the BoS push, the deck does not chase. The pullback into 01:30–03:00 sweeps its own mini low — the dashed SSS — collecting the last sell-side fuel under the rally, and the final push off that sweep leaves two addresses behind: the green OB, origin candles of the push, and the blue zone standing from just under the supply’s floor down through the OB’s right end — the corridor the entry waits in.

The slide’s last candles do the waiting’s job: a pullback down into the blue zone and the OB’s top. That is the confirmation entry in the house’s own words — bought at the block the shift left, after the sweep and the shift, never at the push that made them. And the target needs no new ink: the rose supply across the top, the very box whose 09:00 tap started the fall. Origin to origin, the fall’s supply is the rally’s target.

The maths the deck leaves out

Stop, target, ratio

Entry
The pullback into the blue zone at the OB’s top — the slide’s last candles. A limit at the OB’s upper edge is the house rendering of the same idea.
Stop (house rule)
Below the OB’s low and below the SSS-swept wick. The deck draws none; the rule is structural — a body close under the swept low means the final push failed and the BoS that justified it is void.
Target
The rose supply’s floor first, its body beyond: the zone that started the M15 fall. Book at the floor the way case 28 booked at the teal’s edge — let the zone’s defenders pay on their way in.
Ratio, read off the card
From the OB’s top to the supply’s floor is several times the OB-and-wick risk on the slide’s geometry — comfortably past 1:2, which is the gate; measure it on your own feed before sizing, since the deck carries no prices.
Cancel rules
Off if a body closes below the SSS-swept low before the fill; off if the supply’s floor is reached by someone else first; after the fill, only the stop speaks.

Do it again

Repeatable steps

  1. Let the higher rung name the fuel and stall: a sweep at a wick low plus a refusing tape is context, not a trade.
  2. On the entry rung, want two boxes in view — the zone behind the tap and the zone across the way as target. One box is a hope; two is a map.
  3. Grade taps by their answers: the supply’s tap fell, the demand’s tap rallied. The shift is the vote count, not the tap.
  4. After the BoS, wait for the pullback’s own sweep (the SSS) before trusting the final push — fuel collected last is fuel spent against you least.
  5. Enter at the block the push leaves, in the corridor above it; never at the push’s high.
  6. Stop below the swept low that started the final push; target at the far zone’s floor, booked at the edge.
  7. Read mirrored decks the same way with the scale turned: case 26 sold this exact grammar; this case buys it.

Discipline

When this trade does not exist

  • Tap without shift. A wick into the demand with no MSS and no BoS after it is a raid, not a trigger — the 09:00 supply tap on slide 2 is what an unanswered tap looks like.
  • No sweep before the push. If the pullback never sweeps its mini low (no SSS), the final push runs on unspent fuel above its own stops; wait or stand aside.
  • Chasing the push. Entry at the push’s high instead of the OB’s top inverts the ratio and puts the stop a whole push away.
  • Target already spent. If an earlier leg already tapped the supply’s floor and bounced, the floor book sits on occupied ground — take a nearer book at ratio or skip.
  • Body below the swept low. A close under the SSS wick before or after the fill voids the shift; delete the order, do not move the stop.

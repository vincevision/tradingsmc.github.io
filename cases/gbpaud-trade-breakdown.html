How to read this page

X marks a liquidity sweep. IDM is inducement: the first pullback low after a shift. BSL is buy-side liquidity. The slides carry only timeframe titles, so the step-by-step reasoning here is my reading of the charts. There is no price axis; reward-to-risk is measured from the position boxes.

Step 1 · Map the higher timeframe

1H: a demand zone that broke structure

What happened

  1. A rally broke structure (BoS). At the lower left, price closes above a prior high and then runs up strongly.
  2. Demand is drawn at the base. The zone covers the small candles just before the impulse. That is where the buying that broke structure began.
  3. Price topped and came all the way back. Two highs, then a gap lower between sessions, then a steady decline. Almost the entire rally was retraced.
  4. The zone was tapped. One wick dipped well into the demand zone and was rejected. Price then sat just above the zone for several hours.

Why this zone is worth trading

  • It caused a break of structure.
  • This is the first return to it.
  • It is at the very bottom of the leg, so deep in discount.

The pullback into it is long and steep, though. On the 1H there is no sign yet that the selling has stopped. That is the 15m's job.

Rule to carry forward

Start on the 1H. Find the zone that produced the last break of structure, check it is fresh and in discount, and wait for price to reach it. Then change timeframe.

Step 2 · Sweep and shift

15m: tap, range, sweep, shift

What happened

  1. First tap of the 1H zone. A hard drop takes price into the demand and it bounces sharply. The high of that bounce is the level the MSS line is drawn from.
  2. A nine-hour range. Price drifts sideways and slightly down above the zone. Lows form along the bottom of the range.
  3. The range low is swept (X). A wick runs under the lowest of those lows, reaches the top of the 1H zone and closes back up.
  4. MSS. The very next move is one large bullish candle that closes above the bounce high. Nine hours of range is cleared in fifteen minutes.

Why this matters

The first tap at 10:30 looked like the entry and was not. The bounce it produced went nowhere for nine hours and price came back for the lows. The trade only became valid once those lows were taken and the bounce high was broken with displacement.

The two events arrived back to back: sweep, then shift on the next push. That is as clean as the sequence gets.

Rule to carry forward

After the first reaction from a 1H zone, mark the bounce high and the lows that form afterwards. The signal is a sweep of those lows followed by a 15m close above the bounce high.

Step 3 · Mark the zone

15m: the demand the shift left behind

What is marked

  • 15m Demand. A box at the base of the displacement candle, covering the last small candles before it. It sits just above the 1H zone.
  • IMB. The displacement candle left an imbalance directly above the 15m demand.
  • The sweep low. The X wick sits a little below the bottom of the 15m demand box.

Why this is the zone

It is the origin of the candle that broke structure, it formed immediately after the sweep, and nothing lies beneath it except the low that has already been taken. By the checks on the inducement page, this is the real zone.

Rule to carry forward

As soon as the MSS candle closes, draw the demand at its base and note the imbalance above. That box is where the limit order goes.

Step 4 · Plan the order

15m: inducement above, liquidity target overhead

What is marked

  • BSL. A line across the high on the far left, where the sell-off into the 1H zone began. Buy-side liquidity rests above it. This is the target.
  • IDM. After the shift, price paused and made a small low before pushing higher. That low is the inducement. Early buyers have stops under it.
  • IMB. The imbalance sits between the inducement and the 15m demand.
  • The position. Entry at the top edge of the 15m demand. Stop under the sweep low. Target on the BSL line.

Why the plan is built this way

For price to reach the entry it must first take the inducement low and fill the imbalance. So the fill comes after the early longs are out. The stop sits under the low that started the whole move, slightly beyond the bottom of the zone. From entry to the BSL is about 2.9 times that risk.

Rule to carry forward

The target for a long out of a pullback is the high the pullback started from. Mark it before the entry. If it is less than twice the risk away, there is no trade.

Step 5 · Fill, management and outcome

Result

What happened

  1. Price pulled back from the post-shift high.
  2. The inducement low was taken and the imbalance filled.
  3. Price touched the top of the 15m demand. It did not travel into the zone. The limit order filled at the edge.
  4. A steady climb. Price rallied, paused once below the target, then pushed on.
  5. Target reached. Price hit the BSL line and wicked through it.

What to take from it

  • This was the quickest of the cases. About three hours from shift to fill and about three more to target.
  • The fill was a touch. Again, only the near edge of the zone was reached.
  • The inducement was taken first. The same pattern as AUD/USD, on a shorter clock.
  • A calendar marker sits on the time axis close to when the target was reached. The post does not comment on it. It is a reminder to check what is scheduled while a trade is open.

Rule to carry forward

The sequence does not have a fixed duration. The steps and their order are the plan. The clock is not.

THE LOGIC IN ONE SENTENCE

At a 1H demand that broke structure, wait for the 15m to sweep and shift, buy the zone that shift leaves, and target the high the pullback came from.

Order placement

Entry, stop loss and take profit

Entry

Buy limit at the top edge of the 15m demand.

  • The zone is the base of the displacement candle, under the imbalance.
  • Price only touched the edge. A deeper order would not have filled.
  • Buys fill on the ask. GBP/AUD has a wide spread, so add it to the limit price.

Stop loss

Below the sweep low (X), plus a buffer.

  • The sweep low sits slightly under the bottom of the 15m demand. The grey box ends at about that wick.
  • If it breaks, the sweep is undone and the shift is void.
  • It is not under the 1H zone. That would be roughly twice as wide on this chart.
  • Crosses like GBP/AUD move quickly. Leave a few pips beyond the wick.

Take profit

The BSL line: the high before the drop. About 2.9R.

  • It is the 15m high the sell-off into the 1H zone started from.
  • No opposing supply zone is marked. The target is liquidity.
  • Price wicked through the level. Set the order a few pips before it regardless.

Managing the position

The post shows a single entry and a single exit. The points below are my suggested handling, not something marked on the slides.

  • While waiting: cancel if a 15m candle closes below the 15m demand, or if price reaches the BSL without filling you.
  • Once filled: leave the stop until price clears the post-shift high and prints a higher low.
  • News: check the calendar for GBP and AUD releases before leaving an order or a position overnight.

Position size

Fix the money at risk first, then let the stop distance decide the lot size.

Lots = money at risk ÷ (stop in pips × pip value per lot)

On GBP/AUD one standard lot moves 10 Australian dollars per pip. In US dollars that is 10 × the AUD/USD rate, for example $6.50 at 0.6500.

Example: $10,000 account, 1% risk = $100. Stop of 25 pips at $6.50 per pip per lot gives 100 ÷ (25 × 6.50) ≈ 0.62 lots. At 1:2.9 the target pays about $290.

Repeatable strategy

Step-by-step for the next trade

Written for longs. For shorts, mirror it. A “no” at any step ends the process.

  1. 1H: find the demand that caused the last break of structure.Fresh, and in the lower half of the leg. Set an alert at its top edge.
  2. Price taps the zone. Do not buy the first bounce.On the 15m, mark the high of that bounce. It is the MSS level.
  3. Mark the lows that form after the bounce.They are the liquidity. Wait for them to be swept, at or into the 1H zone.
  4. Did a 15m candle close above the bounce high with displacement?That is the MSS. It should leave an imbalance.
  5. Draw the 15m demand at the base of that candle.Buy limit at its top edge. Stop under the sweep low.
  6. Mark the target: the high the pullback started from.Does it pay at least 1:2? Set the take profit a few pips before it.
  7. Mark the inducement.The first small low after the shift. Expect it to be taken before the fill. Do not buy there.
  8. Size, place, hold, record.Risk 0.5–1%. Exit at target or stop. Screenshot the 1H and the 15m.

Filters

When this setup should be skipped

Do not take it if

  • The 1H zone did not cause a break of structure, or has been tapped before.
  • A 1H candle closes below the 1H demand.
  • The 15m breaks the bounce high without first sweeping the range lows.
  • The break is a slow grind with no imbalance.
  • The 15m demand has already been touched since the shift.
  • The high above is closer than 1:2.
  • High-impact GBP or AUD news is due while the order rests.

This trade had all of these

  • A fresh 1H demand that caused a BoS, deep in discount.
  • A first tap, then a range that built lows above the zone.
  • A sweep of the range low at the zone.
  • A 15m MSS on a single displacement candle with an imbalance.
  • An inducement low taken before the fill.
  • A clear high about 2.9R away.

Keep this in proportion

The 1H pullback into this zone retraced almost the whole rally, including a gap lower. Deep, fast pullbacks like that often continue through the zone. This one held, and the 15m sweep and shift were the evidence that it would. Without them, the same 1H picture could equally be a reversal in progress.

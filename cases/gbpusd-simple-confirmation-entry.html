Case study · GBP/USD

Simple Confirmation Entry

A short on GBP/USD. The 1H breaks structure down and bounces. On the 15m that bounce looks like a new uptrend, and it runs into the supply zone the drop came from. Highs are taken inside the zone, buyers chase the breakout, and one candle later structure shifts down. The entry is the new supply zone that shift leaves. The target is the low.

How to read this page

X marks a liquidity sweep. $$$ marks resting liquidity. The slide captions and the post text are quoted. The rest is my reading of the charts. There is no price axis, so I give no pip distances. Times are read from the time axis and are approximate. Reward-to-risk is measured from the position box.

Step 1 · Higher-timeframe bias

1H: structure breaks down, then a bounce

What happened

  1. A high was swept (X). Before the drop, a wick ran above an earlier 1H high and failed. The line across the top marks the high that was left.
  2. BoS down. One large bearish candle, around 21:00, closed below the prior low.
  3. Two lows. Price made a low, bounced, and made a higher low around 02:00. The dashed line with $$$ joins them. Stops are resting under both.
  4. The bounce. A large bullish candle from the second low, then a slower climb. Price stays below the high.

Why this matters

The 1H is bearish. The last structural event is a break down that came straight after a sweep of a high. The bounce is a pullback inside a bearish leg until price closes above that high.

The rising lows with $$$ are trendline liquidity. They give a short somewhere to aim.

Rule to carry forward

After a 1H break of structure down, treat the next rally as a pullback. Look for a short where the drop began, not for a long.

Step 2 · Sweep and shift

15m: the rally reaches supply and fails

What happened

  1. Supply is drawn at the origin of the drop. The box covers the last candles before the 21:00 sell-off that made the 1H BoS.
  2. The 15m turned bullish. After the lows, price broke a small high (the dotted BoS) and rallied from about 02:00. Each pullback held. On this chart alone it looks like an uptrend.
  3. First sweep (X), about 04:00. A high of the rally is taken and price dips.
  4. Second sweep (X), about 05:30. Price pushes above the next high and a wick reaches well into the supply zone. It does not close there.
  5. MSS, about 06:00. One large bearish candle closes below the last low of the rally.

Why this matters

The post describes it from the buyer's side: “Most traders saw a breakout and enter before the candle closed.” Those buyers bought inside 1H-bearish supply, at a high that had just been taken. Their stops sat under the last low. The MSS candle ran straight through them.

The sweep happened inside the supply zone. The inducement page makes the same point for longs: the sweep and shift have to happen at the zone.

Rule to carry forward

When a rally reaches supply, mark its last higher low. A sweep of a high inside the zone, followed by a 15m close below that low, is the signal. A wick into the zone on its own is not.

Step 3 · Plan the trade

15m: the new supply, the stop and the target

What is marked

  • The new supply. A smaller box under the old zone. It covers the candles just before the bearish candle that made the MSS.
  • $$$ under the new supply. A short dashed line just below its bottom edge. I read it as the highs price is expected to make there before it reaches the zone.
  • The dotted line above. The high of the sweep wick.
  • Target. A line at the 02:00 low, the upper end of the $$$ trendline.
  • The position box. Entry at the bottom edge of the new supply. Stop just above its top edge. Target at the low.

Reading the box

The target is about 3.8 times the stop. The stop is above the new supply zone. It is below the sweep high, which is the dotted line. That is the tight version of the stop and it is what makes the ratio this large.

The target is the nearer of the two lows. The lowest low, from about 22:00, is further down and is not used.

Rule to carry forward

After the MSS candle closes, draw the supply at the candles it launched from. Sell limit at its bottom edge, stop above it, target the first clean low below.

Step 4 · Result

Result: filled within the hour, target an hour later

What happened

  1. A small bounce. After the MSS candle price climbed back for two or three candles.
  2. The fill, a little before 07:00. The highs under the zone were taken and a wick went about a third of the way into the new supply.
  3. The drop. Price fell, bounced once for a couple of candles, then one very large bearish candle around 08:00 reached the target.
  4. The target was only just passed. The wick ends slightly below the line.

What to take from it

  • The wait from shift to fill was under an hour. From fill to target was a little over an hour.
  • The wick into the zone stopped short of the top edge, so the tight stop held.
  • A US flag sits on the time axis at 10:00. The trade was finished before it.

THE LOGIC IN ONE SENTENCE

In a 1H downtrend, let the rally reach supply and take its highs, wait for the 15m to shift down, and sell the new supply toward the low.

The aggressive entry would also have worked here

A sell limit at the bottom of the old supply with a stop above it would have filled on the 05:30 wick and reached the same target. It needs a wider stop and gives no evidence beforehand. The post's point is about the buyer who chased, not about that seller.

Order placement

Entry, stop loss and take profit

Entry

Sell limit at the bottom edge of the new 15m supply.

  • The zone is the group of candles just before the bearish candle that made the MSS.
  • The slides mark $$$ just under the zone. Do not sell there. Let those highs be taken.
  • A wick went about a third of the way into the zone. An order at the edge filled.
  • Sells fill on the bid, so no spread needs adding to the limit price.

Stop loss

Just above the new supply, plus spread and a buffer.

  • The grey box ends slightly above the top of the new supply, below the sweep high.
  • If price closes back above the zone, the shift has failed.
  • Buy stops trigger on the ask. Add the spread to the stop price.
  • The safer alternative is above the sweep high, the dotted line. It is wider and brings the ratio down.

Take profit

The Target line: the 02:00 low. About 3.8R.

  • It is the low the rally into supply started from.
  • It is the nearer of the two lows on the $$$ line. The lower one is left alone.
  • Price passed the line by very little. Set the order a few pips above it.

Managing the position

The post shows a single entry and a single exit. The points below are my suggested handling, not something marked on the slides.

  • While waiting: cancel if price reaches the target without filling you, or if a 15m candle closes above the new supply.
  • Once filled: leave the stop alone through the first bounce. That bounce is on the chart, about halfway down.
  • News: the axis shows a US event at 10:00. If the trade is still open shortly before a release like that, decide beforehand whether you close or hold.

Position size

Fix the money at risk first, then let the stop distance decide the lot size.

Lots = money at risk ÷ (stop in pips × pip value per lot)

On GBP/USD one standard lot moves $10 per pip.

Example: $10,000 account, 1% risk = $100. Stop of 10 pips gives 100 ÷ (10 × 10) = 1.0 lot. At 1:3.8 the target pays about $380. The 10 pips is an illustration. The slides show no prices.

Repeatable strategy

Step-by-step for the next trade

Written for shorts. For longs, mirror it. A “no” at any step ends the process.

  1. 1H: has structure broken down?A close below the prior low, ideally after a high was swept. Bias is short.
  2. 15m: draw the supply where that drop began.The last candles before the sell-off. Set an alert at its bottom edge.
  3. Mark the lows under the bounce.The low the rally starts from is the target.
  4. Price rallies into the supply. Do not buy the rally and do not sell yet.Mark each high it makes and its last higher low.
  5. Is a high swept at or inside the supply?A push above the high that fails to close there.
  6. Does a 15m candle close below the last higher low?That is the MSS. It should be a large, fast candle.
  7. Draw the new supply at the candles before that candle.Sell limit at its bottom edge. Stop above it. Target the low.
  8. Does it pay at least 1:2?If not, skip it.
  9. Size, place, hold, record.Risk 0.5–1%. Exit at target or stop. Screenshot the 1H and the 15m.

Filters

When this setup should be skipped

Do not take it if

  • The 1H has not broken structure down, or has closed back above the high.
  • The rally has not reached the supply zone.
  • A 15m candle closes above the supply zone.
  • The 15m breaks down without first sweeping a high.
  • The break is a slow drift, not a displacement candle.
  • Price has already reached the low before the order can fill.
  • The low is closer than 1:2.
  • High-impact GBP or USD news is due while the order rests.

This trade had all of these

  • A 1H break of structure down after a swept high.
  • A 15m supply at the origin of that drop.
  • Two highs swept, the second inside the supply.
  • A 15m MSS on one large candle.
  • A new supply tapped within the hour.
  • A clear low about 3.8R away.

Keep this in proportion

This trade was fast and clean, and the tight stop was never tested. That makes it a good illustration and a poor guide to how often it works. A stop just above a small 15m zone is hit more often than one above the sweep high. The 3.8R comes from accepting that. Backtest both stops before choosing.

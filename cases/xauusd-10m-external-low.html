This page also keeps the argument the frames invite: two targets were on the map, and which one you book is a decision, not an accident.

Step 1 · Liquidity

The pool and the raid

Two touches make a pool; one wick makes a raid; only a close makes a shift.

The 10m spends the morning falling from above 3,582 into the 3,548–3,550 shelf, then rallies into the 18:30 high. That high and the shoulder beside it print equal highs at ≈ 3,566.5 — the red X line — and above equal highs on a retracement in a falling market rest the stops of every short that joined late and the breakout buys of everyone impatient.

At ≈ 21:30 the market takes them: a spike wick to ≈ 3,575, one candle, closed back below the X. A raid, not a reversal of anything — the buys above the pool are now trapped, and the fuel for the next leg down has been collected. Note the signature the house repeats: the sweep is a wick; the story after it must be bodies.

The bodies arrive within the hour: the fall off the spike closes below the last higher low at ≈ 3,545.5 — the MSS bracket — and then below ≈ 3,543.30, wicking to ≈ 3,536. Structure down, displacement, shift stamped. The alert line parked at 3,537.26 marks where the pause sits: the market has done everything except come back.

Step 2 · Origin

The supply the shift left

Every displacement has an address. The trade lives at that address, nowhere else.

The leg from ≈ 3,560 down to the pause did not start at the spike — it started at the small rally near 22:00–22:30, the last up-candles before the fall went vertical. Those candles are the grey box, ≈ 3,556–3,561: the order block the shift left behind. It sits in premium of the leg it caused, it has never been tapped, and it faces a map with room below — which is the next frame’s whole subject.

Refinement comes with the ticket: inside the grey, the red box keeps the origin bodies — 3,556.12 to 3,562.02, the two axis tags on frame 4. Grey is where you look; red is where the order lives; above red is where the idea dies.

Memorise

A liquidity sweep followed by a shift can be enough — but if that liquidity sweep came from a demand or supply zone, the shift that follows adds more probability. Here the fuel sweep (3,575 over the X) came from no-man’s-land; the trigger sweep — the tap wick into the red box — comes at the zone. Light door first, heavy door second: the gate is full.

Step 3 · The target question

The map below: two magnets

Zoom out before you zoom in. The question is never “how far?” — it is “whose money is down there, and which of it am I taking?”

The wide view names two things between the supply and the abyss. First the teal demand ≈ 3,520–3,527: the origin of the 22nd’s violent rally, a zone with defenders, standing directly in the column’s path. Second, beneath it, the external low of the 22nd at 3,503.00938 — the sell-side pool of the entire two-day range, the level every late long’s stop is hanging under.

Those are two different targets with two different philosophies. The teal band’s top is the zone-edge book: take what the opposing zone’s defenders will pay before they defend, and leave while they still agree. The external low is the pool book: the move exists to raid that pool, so book above it and accept that the teal band must be crossed — in body — to get paid. The house checklist defaults to the zone edge. This trade’s column reached for the pool. Frame 5 settles what happened; the next section settles what it cost and what it paid.

Step 4 · The order

The ticket and its argument

Measured before the tap, or not measured at all.

The axis tags do the talking: 3,562.02 in red (box top, tap-wick high, invalidation), 3,556.12 in white (box floor, the limit’s address), 3,503.11 in blue (the pool book, a hair above 3,503.00938). From the floor to the pool: 53 handles of reward against 5.9 of box — and against the true stop above 3,562.02, roughly 6 to 8 with buffer and spread. Every ratio the house demands is cleared several times over, before the tap exists.

Now the argument, priced honestly. The conservative book — teal top at ≈ 3,527 — pays ≈ 29 handles from the floor without asking any defender for permission: that is the checklist default, and it is never wrong to take it. The pool book pays ≈ 53, but its last 27 handles run through the teal band, which means the trade only completes if the demand fails to defend. Two books, one trade: the frames show the pool book paid in full — and the review section below keeps the receipt for why the teal cross, not the hope, was what carried it.

Step 5 · Delivery

The tap, the hold, the run

Three things had to stay true: the tap stays a wick, the stop stays untouched, and the teal cross stays a body.

The tap. At 22:00–22:30 the return rally wicks into the red box at ≈ 3,560–3,562 — inside it, above its floor, below its top — and the paper sell fills at 3,560.10. One wick; the candles that follow are grey and falling. The trigger sweep at the zone: the heavy door, stamped.

The hold. Between 03:00 and 04:30 the market bounces to ≈ 3,536–3,540, mid-column, inside the trade’s own reward. This is the moment most tickets die by hand — moved stops, early exits, fear of giving it back. The stop above 3,562.02 is 20+ handles above the bounce’s high; nothing about the bounce touches the idea. The ticket stays.

The run. The second fall crosses the teal band in body — the demand’s defenders paid the toll instead of collecting it — and the last candle spikes through the external low: 3,503.11 booked, 3,503.00938 tagged, wicks to ≈ 3,495–3,500, price 3,500.55 at capture with the position still open after 02:39. Pool book paid in full; zone-edge book paid on the way; the dashed line from tap to low is the whole method in one stroke.

The maths

Entry, SL & TP — and the noise caveat

Entry 3,560.10
Paper sell filled at the tap wick inside the red box. A limit at the floor 3,556.12 was the other honest address; the tap filled the marketable version of the same idea.
Stop above 3,562.02
The box top and the tap-wick high in one level: a body close above it means the tap failed and the supply is occupied. Risk from the fill ≈ 2 handles raw, ≈ 3 with buffer and spread.
The noise caveat
A ≈ 3-handle stop on 10m gold lives inside spread and noise. It survived here because the tap was a single clean wick with immediate rejection — the thesis died the moment it was touched. A two-candle grind into the box would have made this stop noise food; when the tap is a grind, the stop belongs above the box plus the grind’s wick, and the size halves.
Target A — zone edge
Teal top ≈ 3,527: ≈ 29–33 handles from entry/floor, no defender’s permission needed. The checklist default; paid on the way.
Target B — the pool
3,503.11, booked above the external low 3,503.00938: ≈ 57 handles from the fill. Needs the teal cross in body; got it; paid in full and printed to 3,500.55.
Cancel rules
Off on a close above 3,562.02 before the fill; off if the external low is swept by someone else first; after the fill, the bounce inside the column is not information — only the stop is.

Do it again

Repeatable steps

  1. Mark the pool the retracement builds (equal highs/lows) and wait for the raid — a wick through it, closed back.
  2. Wait for bodies: the MSS close beyond the last opposite point, with displacement. Wicks propose; closes dispose.
  3. Draw the block the shift left and refine it to bodies; grey to look, red to order, above red to die.
  4. Zoom out and name every magnet below: zone edges in the path and external pools beneath them. Choose your book before the tap, in writing.
  5. Measure both books against the invalidation stop; if the pool book needs a zone to fail, say so out loud before you take it.
  6. Wait for one tap. A wick, not a grind; a grind widens the stop and halves the size.
  7. Hold through bounces inside the column that never touch the stop. The stop is the only information the position sends you.

Discipline

When this trade does not exist

  • The tap grinds. Two or three candles chewing inside the box is occupation, not a tap; the tight invalidation stop becomes noise food and the gate closes.
  • A close above 3,562.02 before the fill. The supply is occupied and the shift is cancelled; the order comes off or never goes on.
  • The teal defends. If the first cross of the demand fails with an MSS up through it, the pool book’s last 27 handles are a fight — keep the zone-edge book or stand aside.
  • The pool is pre-swept. If the external low has already been taken by an earlier leg, the column’s reason is gone; a target without a pool is a hope with coordinates.
  • Ratio under 1:2 from your chosen book. Whichever book you take, it must pay for its own stops from the box, measured before the tap.

Step 1 · Structure and zones

The fall, the two sweeps and the second shift

What the chart shows

  1. bos at about 3,872.9, X under it. The falling leg from 3,899 closes below that low, and the X candle, about 3,872.3, is the final low of the break.
  2. First MSS above about 3,882.4. The bounce closes over the last lower high. The fall is over for now.
  3. The rally to about 3,890.5 and the $$$ line. The high that rally leaves behind is resting buy-side liquidity, labelled before price ever comes back to it.
  4. The double bottom near 3,872.5, underlined in red, X on the first low. Two near-equal lows: sell-side liquidity sitting under the pullback.
  5. The wick to about 3,870.9. That liquidity is taken. A stop hunt under the pullback, one candle, and it is done.
  6. Second MSS above about 3,878.4. The rally out of the sweep closes over the last lower high. This is the shift the entry waits for.
  7. The red box, 3,869.37 to 3,878.52. The base of that second shift: the 5m demand, both edges labelled to the cent. The two grey boxes below and left of it are the older bases under the double bottom, the floor the red zone stands on.
  8. The purple box, 3,878.52 to 3,892.14. The range the rally has to travel, with its top labelled just above the $$$ line: the first objective.
  9. The grey box above about 3,892.6 and the sketch at the right. Higher supply, and the plan in one line: up into it taking the $$$, then rejection.

Why it matters

Everything a limit order needs is on the chart while price is still away from the zone: the direction (second MSS up), the level that proves the idea wrong (under the red box), the place to buy (the top edge of the red box), the first target (the $$$ high and the purple top) and the place the idea ends (the supply above). Nothing here is decided in a hurry at the edge.

Rule to carry forward

Draw the zone, the stop level and the target while price is still away from the zone. A plan drawn in a hurry at the edge is a chase, not a plan.

Step 2 · Delivery and outcome

The zone holds once and the rally runs through everything

What the chart shows

  1. The tag. A wick to about 3,875.5, a third of the way into the red zone. A buy limit at the top edge, 3,878.52, fills on the way in.
  2. The pullback to about 3,881.5. It holds above the zone top. That is the first evidence the zone is doing its job, and the last chance to enter at market if the limit missed.
  3. Displacement. Large blue candles through the $$$ line near 3,891.3 and through the purple top at 3,892.14. The liquidity labelled in screenshot 1 is taken exactly as labelled.
  4. The close above the grey supply, price at 3,900.48. The sketched rejection never prints. The supply above is broken on its first test, in one session.

Why it matters

Three separate lessons in one run. A zone only has to hold once: one tag is the whole test. Liquidity labelled before the rally gets taken: the $$$ line did its job as a target. And a plan that ends at a supply must be remade the moment that supply closes beyond: the sketch said “up then down”, the chart said “up and closed above”, and the second half of the sketch died there. The long keeps its profit; the short from that supply never gets to exist.

Rule to carry forward

When price closes through the zone your target sat in front of, the trade is done and the next idea starts fresh. Never short the break of your own target.

What is different from the other cases

Everything here is one timeframe. No 1H or 4H is shown, so nothing on these two screenshots says whether the long was with or against the bigger flow. In replay that is acceptable practice; on a live account the higher timeframes decide whether a 5m demand is the best trade on the board or a counter-trend scalp. See Top-down analysis.

The first target is too close

1:1.3 to the $$$ high is under this site’s 1:2 floor, and the purple top at 3,892.14 is only 1:1.4. Naming that liquidity before the rally is excellent work; sizing the trade to it while a supply zone sits behind it is the one mistake on these charts. Either the target is the far side of the supply, near 1:2.1, or the setup is a skip. Measure both distances before the order, not after the tag.

The tighter stop, and why it is not the rule

A stop under the sweep wick, near 3,870.40, risks about 8.1 instead of 9.6 and lifts the reward-to-risk on both targets. It also dies the moment the double bottom is swept a second time, which is a normal thing for a double bottom to do. The rule stays: the stop goes beyond the structure that invalidates the idea, which here is the zone bottom.

Review

What worked, and what to fix

Done well

  • Both sweeps marked with an X, and the double bottom underlined, before the sweep happened.
  • The demand drawn only after the second MSS, not on the first bounce.
  • Zone edges labelled to the cent, so the limit and the stop are numbers, not feelings.
  • The $$$ target labelled before the rally, then taken exactly there.
  • A sketched end to the plan: rejection at the supply above.

To fix or answer

  • No order ticket or position line in either screenshot. If the long was never placed, the cost is the whole move, and the pullback at 3,881.5 was the second chance at market.
  • The reward-to-risk as marked, to the first target, is under 1:2. The supply behind it is the real target.
  • The sketched rejection failed: price closed above the supply. The “then down” half of the plan had to be deleted on that close.
  • The purple box is my guess (the range to the first objective). Tell me what you meant by it and I will correct the page.

The question to ask this chart

Where was the 1H? A 5m demand inside 1H supply is a counter-trend scalp with a ceiling; the same 5m demand inside 1H demand is the best trade on the board. One extra screenshot of the 1H before the session answers it, and it costs nothing.

Repeatable steps

Run this the same way every session

  1. Let the fall break its low, and mark that low and the sweep of it.The bos line and the X under the final low. Before this there is nothing to trade.
  2. Wait for the first MSS.The bounce that closes over the last lower high. Do not buy the bounce before it prints.
  3. Mark the high the first rally leaves.That $$$ line is a target for the long, never a place to sell from.
  4. Wait for the pullback liquidity to be swept.Double bottoms, equal lows, trendline lows: whatever rests under the pullback gets taken first.
  5. On the sweep, wait for the second MSS, then draw the demand at its base.Exact edges. The base of the displacement, not the middle of the pullback.
  6. Place the three numbers as one order.Limit at the near edge, stop below the zone plus buffer, target at the labelled liquidity and then the zone behind it. Require 1:2 to that zone target or skip.
  7. Let the tag fill, or miss it.No chasing. The pullback that holds above the zone top is the last market entry.
  8. When the target zone closes through, close the plan with it.Read the chart again from the new structure. The old sketch is dead paper.

When to skip

The same setup, refused

Do not take it if

  • The pullback lows were never swept before the shift.
  • The zone has already been tagged once in the session.
  • The first liquidity is under 1:2 away and no zone sits behind it.
  • The higher timeframe is selling into the same supply you are targeting.
  • High-impact news for the currency lands inside the trade.

This setup had

  • Two sweeps marked before the entry, not after.
  • Two shifts, the second one at the zone.
  • A fresh zone with both edges labelled to the cent.
  • A named liquidity target and a zone behind it for 1:2 or better.
  • A pullback that held above the zone top before the run.

Keep this in proportion

One replay session on gold, one zone, and no visible order. It shows the marking routine in good order: sweeps, shifts, exact zone, named target. It does not show that the routine makes money. Keep the log running to fifty setups with the losses and the skips counted, and add the 1H screenshot next time so the context is on the page too.

Case study · GBP/JPY

Strong Structure Starts With a Sweep

A long on GBP/JPY built on one idea: a break of structure can be trusted when liquidity was taken first. The higher timeframe ran the low, the 15m then broke structure upward, and the entry came on the return to the order block. The target was the buy-side liquidity resting above an old high.

  • Target: buy-side liquidity

How to read this page

X marks a liquidity sweep. $$ marks resting liquidity that has not been taken yet. BSL is buy-side liquidity: stops above a high. The slides have no price axis, so levels are described by structure. Reward-to-risk is measured from the position boxes. The post calls the first chart “higher timeframe” without naming it; the candle spacing suggests 1H. A dotted horizontal line appears on every slide with no label, so I have not built anything on it.

Step 1 · Context and liquidity

Higher timeframe: break down, then sweep the low

What happened

  1. Structure was bearish. Two BoS labels mark two lower lows. Anyone reading structure alone is looking for shorts.
  2. The highs line up on a trendline ($$). Each lower high sits on the dashed line. Sellers trail their stops above it and breakout buyers wait there. That is buy-side liquidity building up overhead.
  3. The second low was swept (X). One candle drove far below the last BoS low and was rejected. Nearly all of that move is wick.
  4. Price recovered the whole breakdown. The candles that follow are bullish and carry price back up into the middle of the range. The slide ends with another long lower wick, which matters later.

Why this matters

  • The sell-side has been used. Stops under the low are gone. There is less reason for price to go back down there.
  • The buy-side is untouched. The trendline highs and the old high above them are still loaded. Price tends to travel from liquidity that has been taken toward liquidity that has not.
  • The sweep is a warning to sellers, not yet a buy signal. Higher-timeframe structure is still bearish on this slide. The case for a long has to be proven on the lower timeframe.

Rule to carry forward

Before trusting any break of structure, ask what was swept just before it. For a long, a higher-timeframe low must have been run and rejected, with untouched liquidity above to aim at.

How this differs from the other two cases

In USD/JPY and XAU/USD the higher timeframe had already shifted in the trade's direction. Here it had not. This is a reversal taken off a sweep, against two higher-timeframe breaks. That makes the 15m confirmation in the next step more important, not less.

Step 2 · Confirmation

15m: after the sweep, structure breaks up

What happened

  1. One large candle broke the higher-timeframe low. The BoS line on the left is that low. Price fell straight through it.
  2. The sweep had two parts (X). Price bounced, then dropped again and went slightly below the first spike's low before turning. What looked like a single wick on the higher timeframe is a double low on the 15m, with the second low running the first.
  3. MSS. The rally closed above the high of the bounce between the two lows. That is the lower high that led to the final low, so breaking it ends the 15m downtrend.
  4. An order block was left behind. The OB box marks the last bearish candles before the leg that broke structure.
  5. A wick low sits above the OB ($$). During the range at the top, one candle spiked down and recovered. Its low is marked as liquidity. Buyers who entered early have stops under it.

Why this matters

This is the sequence in the post's title. Liquidity was taken on the higher timeframe, and only then did the 15m break upward. A 15m break without that sweep would just be a bounce inside a downtrend.

The $$ mark is worth noticing before the trade is on. Unswept liquidity between price and the order block tells you two things: price has a reason to come down to the OB, and it will probably hunt those stops first.

Rule to carry forward

After a higher-timeframe sweep, wait for a 15m close above the lower high that produced the final low. Then mark the order block and any lows sitting between it and current price. Those lows are the path to your entry.

Step 3 · Refinement and order placement

15m: wait for the return to the order block

What is marked

  • BoS after the MSS. Price made one more higher high before pulling back. The shift was followed through, which is a second confirmation.
  • That high touched the trendline ($$). Price reached the descending line of highs and turned down without breaking it. The liquidity above the line is still there.
  • IMB. An imbalance left by the rally sits directly on top of the order block.
  • OB. The same block as slide 2, drawn from the origin of the leg that broke structure.
  • $$ below price. The wick low from slide 2. Price is now heading toward it.
  • BSL. A horizontal line from an older high, off the left of the chart. Buy-side liquidity rests above it. The top of the blue box is on this line.

Why the entry is here

Entry is a buy limit at the top edge of the order block. To get there price has to take the $$ low and fill the imbalance, so the fill comes after early longs have been stopped out, not before.

The stop is drawn well below the bottom of the OB box. It clears the wicks of the swing low that the breaking leg started from. From entry, the distance up to the BSL line is about 3.8 times that risk.

Rule to carry forward

Do not buy the pullback while liquidity still sits between price and your order block. Leave the limit order at the OB and let price come through those lows to reach it.

Step 4 · Management and outcome

Result

What happened

  1. The $$ low was taken. Price fell from the trendline and ran the wick low. The first dashed line marks it.
  2. Price stalled and built new lows. For several hours it ranged just above the order block, printing a cluster of lows at nearly the same price. The second dashed line marks them. More stops, even closer to the OB.
  3. One wick swept those lows and tapped the OB. Around 02:00 on the chart a single candle dipped through the cluster, touched the top edge of the order block and closed back above. The buy limit filled on that wick.
  4. Expansion. Price rallied almost without pause, through the trendline liquidity.
  5. Target reached. A wick touched the BSL line and price pulled back from it immediately.

What to take from it

  • The wait was long. The shift happened around 13:30 on the chart and the fill came around 02:00 the next day. Roughly twelve hours with a resting order. The move to target then took a little over an hour.
  • Two stop hunts came before the entry. Anyone who bought the first dip, or the range lows, was taken out just before the move. The order block sat below both.
  • The fill was shallow, for the third case in a row. Price only reached the near edge of the OB.
  • The target was hit by a wick. Price tagged the BSL line and reversed. A take profit placed a few pips beyond the line would not have filled.

Rule to carry forward

When the target is a liquidity level, place the take profit a few pips before it. Price needs only to touch the stops there. It owes you nothing beyond that.

The logic in one sentence

After a higher-timeframe low is swept and the 15m breaks up, buy the order block once the lows above it have been cleared, and take profit just before the untouched highs.

Order placement

Entry, stop loss and take profit

Entry

Buy limit at the top edge of the 15m order block.

  • Place it once the MSS is confirmed. The later BoS adds confidence and does not move the order.
  • The imbalance ends at the top of the OB, so that edge is the first price a full pullback reaches.
  • Only a wick reached it. A deeper order would not have filled.
  • Buys fill on the ask. GBP/JPY spreads are wider than the majors, so add the spread to the limit price.

Stop loss

Below the swing low under the order block, plus a buffer.

  • On the chart the grey box ends well below the bottom of the OB box. My reading is that it clears the wicks of the low the breaking leg started from.
  • That low is the 15m higher low. If price closes below it, the shift has failed.
  • It is not placed under the sweep low at the very bottom. That would be safer and would cut the reward-to-risk to well under 1:2.
  • GBP/JPY moves fast and wicks deep. Give the stop a few pips of room beyond the wick.

Take profit

The BSL line: the old high above. About 3.8R.

  • There is no opposing supply zone marked on this chart. The target is liquidity: the stops above an older high.
  • The trendline highs ($$) lie on the path and are taken on the way up.
  • The chart's target is on the line and a wick just reached it. In practice set the order a few pips below the line.
  • Sell-side was swept, buy-side was not. The trade runs from one to the other.

Managing the position

The post shows a single entry and a single exit. The points below are my suggested handling, not something marked on the slides.

  • While waiting: cancel the limit if a 15m candle closes below the order block, or if price takes the BSL without filling you.
  • Overnight orders: a twelve-hour wait can run across sessions and the daily rollover, when spreads widen. Decide beforehand whether you leave the order on or re-place it in the next session.
  • Once filled: leave the stop alone until price breaks the trendline. An optional partial belongs at the trendline highs.

Position size

Fix the money at risk first, then let the stop distance decide the lot size.

Lots = money at risk ÷ (stop in pips × pip value per lot)

On any JPY pair one standard lot moves ¥1,000 per pip. In dollars that is 1,000 ÷ the USD/JPY rate, for example about $6.67 at 150.00.

Example: $10,000 account, 1% risk = $100. Stop of 25 pips at $6.67 per pip per lot gives 100 ÷ (25 × 6.67) ≈ 0.60 lots. At 1:3.8 the target pays about $380.

Repeatable strategy

Step-by-step for the next trade

Written for longs, as in this trade. For shorts, mirror it: highs for lows, sell-side for buy-side. A “no” at any step ends the process.

  1. On the higher timeframe, mark the last swing low and the liquidity above.Trendline highs, equal highs and old highs are the buy-side. Set an alert just under the swing low.
  2. Was the low swept and rejected?You want a wick below the low and a close back above it. A full-bodied close below with follow-through is a breakdown, not a sweep.
  3. Is there untouched liquidity above to aim at?If the highs have already been run, there is no target and no trade.
  4. On the 15m, did a candle close above the lower high that produced the final low?This is the MSS. A further higher high afterwards (BoS) is a welcome second confirmation.
  5. Mark the order block, the imbalance, and the swing low beneath it.The OB is the last bearish candle before the leg that broke structure. It must be untouched.
  6. Mark every low between price and the order block.Wick lows and equal lows are where early buyers get stopped. Expect price to take them on the way to your entry.
  7. Does it pay at least 1:2 to the liquidity above?Entry at the OB top, stop under the swing low, target a few pips before the high.
  8. Place the orders and wait.Buy limit, stop and target together, sized for 0.5–1% risk. The wait can be many hours.
  9. Manage by structure, then record it.Cancel on a 15m close below the OB. Hold to target or stop. Screenshot the higher timeframe and the 15m.

Filters

When this setup should be skipped

Do not take it if

  • The higher-timeframe low was broken by a full candle body that held below it.
  • The 15m breaks up without a sweep first. That is the weak structure the post warns about.
  • The highs above have already been taken, so there is no liquidity to target.
  • The 15m “shift” breaks only a minor high inside the last leg down.
  • Price closes a 15m candle below the order block before or after the fill.
  • The liquidity target is closer than 1:2.
  • High-impact GBP or JPY news is due while the order is resting.

This trade had all of these

  • A higher-timeframe low swept by a long wick and recovered.
  • A double low on the 15m, the second running the first.
  • 15m MSS, followed by a further BoS.
  • An untouched order block with an imbalance on top.
  • Lows above the OB that were swept before the fill.
  • Untouched trendline highs and an old high above, about 3.8R away.

Keep this in proportion

This is the most aggressive of the three cases. The higher timeframe had printed two breaks down and had not shifted up, so the trade relies on the sweep and the 15m alone. Sweeps that fail look identical at first: a long wick, a bounce, then continuation lower. Track this variation separately in your backtest so you can see whether it holds up on its own.

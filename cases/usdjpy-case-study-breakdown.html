Case Study Breakdown

A long on USD/JPY built across three timeframes. The 1H sweeps a low and shifts bullish. The 30m marks the demand that move came from. Price rallies, then returns to that demand and takes the inducement low on the way. The 15m sweeps and shifts at the zone, and the entry is the 15m demand it leaves. The target is the high the pullback started from.

How to read this page

X marks a liquidity sweep. IDM is inducement: a pullback low that draws in early buyers. BSL is buy-side liquidity. $$ marks resting liquidity. The slides carry only timeframe titles, so the reasoning here is my reading of the charts. There is no price or time axis, so I give no pip distances or clock times. Reward-to-risk is measured from the position boxes.

Step 1 · Higher-timeframe bias

1H: a low is swept, then structure shifts up

What happened

  1. A sharp drop. Two large bearish candles erase the previous rally.
  2. The old low is swept (the long X line). A long wick runs under the low at the left of the chart. The candle closes back above it.
  3. A second, smaller sweep (the short X). A few candles later a minor low is taken and rejected.
  4. MSS. Price rallies and closes above the high the drop began from.

Why this matters

The drop looked bearish. It took the stops under the old low and then failed to hold below it. When the 1H then closed above the starting high, the whole drop became a sweep. The bias for the lower timeframes is long.

Rule to carry forward

A 1H sweep of a low followed by a 1H close above the high before the drop gives a long bias. Do not buy yet. Go down a timeframe and find where that move started.

Step 2 · Mark the zone

30m: the demand at the origin of the shift

What is marked

  • The sweep. The line at the lower left is the old low. The wick goes through it.
  • X. After the first bounce, a small low is taken once more.
  • 30m Demand and IMB. A large bullish candle follows that second sweep. The demand is drawn at its base and the imbalance sits above it.
  • $$ on a dotted rising line. Lows climbing in a row above the zone. This is trendline liquidity.
  • MSS, then BoS. Price closes above the high before the drop, pulls back, and breaks to a higher high.

Why this is the zone

It is the base of the candle that started the shift, and it formed right after a sweep. Everything above it formed later: the rising lows with $$ and the pullback low before the BoS. Those are liquidity, and they sit between price and the zone.

Rule to carry forward

Draw the 30m demand at the base of the displacement that led to the shift. Then mark every low that forms above it. Expect those lows to be taken before the zone is reached.

Step 3 · Wait for the return

30m: inducement taken, zone tapped

What happened

  1. The rally continued, then topped. After the BoS price ran to a peak and rolled over.
  2. The decline was steady. Lower highs and lower lows all the way down.
  3. The IDM low was taken. That is the pullback low from before the BoS. Anyone who bought there, or trailed a stop under it, was taken out.
  4. The 30m demand was tapped. One large bearish candle reached into the top part of the zone and price turned up at once.
  5. MSS. The bounce closed above the last lower high of the decline.

Why this matters

Buying the pullback low before the BoS was the obvious trade and it failed. The inducement page describes this exactly: the first clean low after a shift is bait, and the real zone is the one underneath it.

The peak of this rally is now the high the decline started from. It becomes the target on slide 5.

Rule to carry forward

Wait for price to take the inducement low and reach the 30m demand. Then change to the 15m and ask for a sweep and a shift there.

Step 4 · Sweep, shift, zone

15m: sweep and shift at the 30m demand

What happened

  1. The tap. A wick reaches into the 30m demand and price bounces hard.
  2. A 15m sweep (X). The bounce pauses and leaves a small low. A wick runs under it and closes back up. This low is above the low of the tap.
  3. Displacement and IMB. A run of bullish candles leaves an imbalance.
  4. MSS. Price closes above the high from before the final drop.
  5. 15m demand. Drawn at the base of the displacement, under the imbalance.
  6. IDM. Price goes sideways and leaves a pullback low above the zone.

Why this matters

This is the 1H sequence again in miniature: sweep, shift, zone. The 30m demand gave the location. The 15m gives the proof and a much smaller zone to trade from.

The new IDM low is the next piece of bait. It sits between price and the 15m demand, so expect it to be taken before the fill.

Rule to carry forward

At the HTF zone, wait for a 15m low to be swept and a 15m close above the last high. Draw the demand at the base of that move. Label the first pullback low after it as inducement, not as an entry.

Step 5 · Plan the trade

15m: entry, stop and target on one chart

What is marked

  • BSL. A line from the high at the far left, where the whole decline began. This is the peak from slide 3.
  • IDM (left). The 30m inducement level that price fell through.
  • 30m Demand, X, MSS, IMB, 15m demand, IDM. Everything from slide 4.
  • The position box. Entry at the top edge of the 15m demand. Stop at the X sweep low, where the line from the X meets the bottom of the grey box. Target at the BSL line.

Reading the box

The target is about 3.3 times the stop. Price has not come back yet. This is a resting limit order with all three prices fixed in advance.

The stop is under the 15m sweep low. It is not under the low of the tap into the 30m demand, which is lower. That keeps the stop small. It also means a second, deeper test of the 30m demand would stop the trade out.

Rule to carry forward

Target the high the pullback into the HTF zone started from. If the distance from the 15m demand to that high is less than twice the stop, there is no trade.

Step 6 · Result

Result: a deep fill, then the target

What happened

  1. One more push up. Before coming back, price spiked above the highs of the range. The slide does not label it. I read it as buy-side being taken before the pullback.
  2. The IDM low was taken. Price fell straight through it.
  3. A deep fill. Price went through most of the 15m demand. The wick stopped near the bottom of the zone, above the stop.
  4. A higher low. The first bounce was sold once more. That dip held above the zone.
  5. The rally. Price climbed, paused about halfway, and then one large candle went through the BSL line.
  6. Beyond the target. Price kept rising toward an unlabelled line higher up.

What to take from it

  • The order at the top edge filled, and the trade then went against the entry by most of the zone's height. That is normal for an edge entry.
  • The stop held because it was under the sweep low, not inside the zone.
  • The pause halfway is where a trader who moved the stop up too early, or closed out of boredom, lost the trade.

Everything as one

The whole trade on one page

The same three-step pattern runs twice. First on the 1H and 30m to give the bias and the zone. Then on the 15m, at that zone, to give the entry.

1 · BIAS

The 1H sweeps an old low and closes above the high before the drop. Long bias.

2 · LOCATION

The 30m demand at the base of that move. Price rallies, tops, and comes back through the inducement low to tap it.

3 · CONFIRMATION

On the 15m, a low is swept at the zone and price shifts up with an imbalance, leaving the 15m demand.

4 · ENTRY

Price takes the 15m inducement low and fills the buy limit at the top of the 15m demand. The fill goes deep and holds.

5 · TARGET

Price returns to the high the pullback began from and goes through it. About 3.3R.

THE LOGIC IN ONE SENTENCE

After a 1H sweep and shift, wait for price to come back to the 30m demand, let the 15m sweep and shift there, and buy the 15m demand toward the high above.

Order placement

Entry, stop loss and take profit

Entry

Buy limit at the top edge of the 15m demand.

  • The zone is the base of the 15m displacement, under the imbalance.
  • Price went through most of the zone after the fill. Expect drawdown on an edge entry.
  • A lower entry inside the zone would also have filled here, with a smaller stop. It would have missed the trades where price only touches the edge.

Stop loss

Below the 15m sweep low (X), plus a buffer.

  • The grey box ends at the X low. That low sits under the 15m demand and above the 30m demand.
  • If it breaks, the 15m sweep is undone and the shift is void.
  • The safer alternative is under the low of the tap into the 30m demand. It is wider and cuts the reward-to-risk.
  • Leave a few pips below the wick. Sell stops trigger on the bid, so no spread needs adding on a long.

Take profit

The BSL line: the high before the drop. About 3.3R.

  • It is the high the decline into the 30m demand started from.
  • No opposing supply zone is marked. The target is liquidity.
  • Price went well past it. The plan still exits at the line. Set the order a few pips before it.

Managing the position

The post shows a single entry and a single exit. The points below are my suggested handling, not something marked on the slides.

  • While waiting: cancel if a 15m candle closes below the X low, or if price reaches the BSL without filling you.
  • Once filled: leave the stop alone through the first bounce and the higher low. Move it only after price breaks the high made after the shift.
  • The line above the BSL: the slides leave it unlabelled. If you want to hold a part for it, close most at the BSL first.
  • News: check the calendar for USD and JPY releases before leaving an order or a position open.

Position size

Fix the money at risk first, then let the stop distance decide the lot size.

Lots = money at risk ÷ (stop in pips × pip value per lot)

On USD/JPY one standard lot moves ¥1,000 per pip. In dollars that is 1,000 ÷ the USD/JPY rate, for example about $6.67 at 150.00.

Example: $10,000 account, 1% risk = $100. Stop of 15 pips at $6.67 per pip per lot gives 100 ÷ (15 × 6.67) ≈ 1.0 lot. At 1:3.3 the target pays about $330. The 15 pips is an illustration. The slides show no prices.

Repeatable strategy

Step-by-step for the next trade

Written for longs. For shorts, mirror it. A “no” at any step ends the process.

Filters

When this setup should be skipped

Do not take it if

  • The 1H has not closed above the high before the drop.
  • Price has not yet come back to the 30m demand. Entries above it are inducement.
  • A 30m candle closes below the 30m demand.
  • The 15m breaks up without first sweeping a low.
  • The 15m break is a slow grind with no imbalance.
  • The 15m demand has already been touched since the shift.
  • The high above is closer than 1:2.
  • High-impact USD or JPY news is due while the order rests.

This trade had all of these

  • A 1H sweep of an old low and a 1H MSS.
  • A 30m demand at the origin of that move.
  • Inducement above the zone, taken on the way back.
  • A 15m sweep and MSS at the 30m demand, with an imbalance.
  • A 15m inducement low taken before the fill.
  • A clear high about 3.3R away.

Keep this in proportion

The stop here sat under a 15m low, above the low of the 30m tap. Price came close to the bottom of the 15m demand before turning. A slightly deeper dip would have been a loss on a trade whose idea was still intact. Decide in advance which stop you use, the tight one or the one under the 30m tap, and backtest that choice. Do not switch between them trade by trade.

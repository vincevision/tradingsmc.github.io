Four rungs, one supply, one ticket

One supply, drawn on the 2h at the origin of the 19→20 fall (≈ 159.50–159.90), cascades down four rungs of a replay ladder — 2h, 45m, 15m, 5m — each rung tightening the address. Three taps arrive at the 15m stack (00:30, 03:30, 05:00 on the 25th); the first two bring no shift and therefore no trade. The third sweeps the internal X at ≈ 159.42 as it enters the last box, the shift closes below the bos at ≈ 159.28, and the ticket — 159.350 entry, 159.466 stop, 158.997 target at the 2h demand area’s top — pays ≈ 1:3.0 before the fall sweeps the whole area to 158.733.

How to read this page

Five figures down the ladder: the 2h context with the supply above and the demand area below; the 45m carrying the same supply plus its own box; the 15m stack where three taps arrive and only one earns a shift; the refined ticket with its column aimed at the area’s top; and the delivery that sweeps the whole area. The lesson is not the cascade — it is that two of the three taps correctly produced nothing.

Step 1 · Location

The 2h supply and the area below

Two boxes on the highest rung, and everything else this case does is arithmetic between them.

The 2h rally tops on the 13th near 159.95–160.05, and the 19th’s second push nearly matches it before the displacement fall leaves: origin candles become the supply, ≈ 159.50–159.90 as the lower rungs will draw it. The fall runs to ≈ 158.45–158.5 and stops above the old ground that becomes the demand area ≈ 158.75–159.00 — the magnet under everything, and later the target’s address.

The 22nd’s bounce pushes twice into the supply’s floor (≈ 159.45, ≈ 159.55) and gets rejected both times: the zone’s first two votes on the higher rung, recorded before the lower rungs even open. Location is now fixed: sell-side ideas live at the supply, buy-side reasons live at the area, and the space between is where the ladder does its work.

Step 2 · The cascade

One rung down: the 45m

The supply survives the change of rung untouched; what the 45m adds is a tighter box inside its floor.

On the 45m the big box reads ≈ 159.51–159.91 — the 2h supply, same walls. Inside its right end sits the rung’s own contribution: ≈ 159.43–159.59, the origin candles of the fall from the 24th 05:00 push (wick ≈ 159.58), with the bos at ≈ 159.49 and the MSS at ≈ 159.15 naming that push’s failure. Every lower rung from here will aim at the overlap of these boxes, which is exactly where the 15m stack will sit.

Off-ladder rungs, on-ladder roles

The house ladder is 1m · 5m · 15m · 1H · 4H · Daily · Weekly, two rungs apart — but this replay ran 2h, 45m, 15m and 5m. That is fine, and it is the point of the pair table: what must never change is the roles — one rung for location, one for confirmation, one for entry. Rungs are a habit; roles are the rule. See Top-down analysis.

Step 3 · The return, graded

The 15m stack: three taps, one shift

The most disciplined frame in this archive: two perfect-looking taps that produced nothing, because nothing answered them.

The 15m carries three boxes where the 45m carried two: ≈ 159.46–159.62, ≈ 159.40–159.47, ≈ 159.35–159.46 — the cascade’s last tightening. The 24th’s afternoon high leaves the long X at ≈ 159.36, and the 22:00 drop’s low leaves the MSS line at ≈ 159.11 that the night rally breaks. Then the taps: 00:30, wick ≈ 159.50 into box one, sweeping the 159.36 X as it goes — and no shift follows, so no order exists. 03:30, wick ≈ 159.46 into box two — again no shift, again nothing. A tap is a question; twice the market changed the subject.

05:00 is different: the push wicks ≈ 159.45–159.46 into box three and sweeps the internal X at ≈ 159.42 — the sweep at the zone, the heavy door — and this time the answer comes inside the half-hour: bodies close below the bos at ≈ 159.28, the 04:30 low. Sweep, tap, shift: the confirmation model’s three beats in thirty minutes of tape, after two hours of correctly doing nothing.

Memorise

A liquidity sweep followed by a shift can be enough — but if that liquidity sweep came from a demand or supply zone, the shift that follows adds more probability. The 05:00 wick took the internal X from inside box three’s own floor; the close below ≈ 159.28 carries the full weight of that rule.

Step 4 · The order

The ticket

Eleven and a half pips of risk against thirty-five of reward, aimed at a line drawn on the 2h days earlier.

The refinement keeps the tap candle and the shift’s origin: 159.350–159.466, the axis tags. The floor is the entry — where the zone begins, not where the wick ended — and the 06:00 pullback candle tags it within the hour of the shift. The stop sits above the refined top, above the 05:00 tap wick, above every candle the morning printed: a body close there means box three is occupied and the tap was a break, not a question.

The target is the cascade’s other end: 158.997, the demand area’s top edge — booked a hair inside so the fill lands as the area’s defenders start paying, not after they finish. Risk 11.6 pips, reward 35.3: ≈ 1:3.0, measured at the refined box before the fill and cleared again at it. And then the test, because tests come: wicks to ≈ 159.45 through 06:00–08:00, inside the red, never closing above the stop. The position sits, and the stop stays the only message worth reading.

Step 5 · Delivery

Delivery through the area

A fall with a dashed trendline for company, a target tagged on the way past, and an area swept floor-ward before lunch.

After 08:00 the tape stops arguing: 159.35, 159.25, 159.15, 159.05 — displacement down the column, the owner’s dashed trendline keeping it company. At ≈ 10:45 it reaches 158.997 and the blue tag: the area’s top edge paid, ≈ 3.0 R in the book. The sweep does not stop at the book — it runs two long candles through the area to 158.733, its floor, near midday: liquidity under old demand collected the way liquidity over old supply started the whole cascade.

The capture holds price at 158.738 with the Open badge still lit — the same quiet signature as case 30: stop above 159.466, target tagged on the way through, nothing to manage, nothing to move, the pen already back in its holder.

Do it again

Repeatable steps

  1. Cascade the same supply down the rungs instead of redrawing it: each rung may tighten the address, none may move it.
  2. Keep the roles separate even off the standard ladder: location, confirmation, entry — 2h / 45m / 15m / 5m worked because the jobs never mixed.
  3. Mark the prior day’s high and the internal highs early; they are the sweeps’ addresses and the taps’ receipts.
  4. Grade every tap on its own: a tap with no shift following is a closed door, however clean the wick looks entering the box.
  5. When the shift finally answers, refine to the tap candle and lift the ceiling above every wick the session printed.
  6. Book at the higher-timeframe area’s top edge, drawn days earlier: the defence of that area is the event that pays the ticket.
  7. Expect the test: wicks back into the red after the fill are the stop earning its keep, not a reason to touch the order.

Discipline

When this trade does not exist

  • Tap without shift — the case’s own lesson. Taps one and two entered their boxes cleanly and produced nothing. Enter on taps and you own every raid the market runs.
  • The tap closes through. Any 15m close above 159.466 before the fill occupies box three and the stack above it; the cascade becomes history.
  • Rungs without roles. Off-ladder timeframes are fine; mixing location and confirmation on one chart is not. If one chart is doing both jobs, stand aside.
  • The area pre-swept. If an earlier leg already ran through 158.997 and bounced hard, the top-edge book sits on occupied ground — take a nearer book at ratio or skip.
  • Ratio under 1:2. From refined floor to chosen book, measured before the fill. A cascade does not earn the right to a bad price.

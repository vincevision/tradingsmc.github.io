The two-minute supply

Most of the cases on this site draw the zone and the trigger on neighbouring rungs of the same ladder — 4H location, 15m entry. This one compresses the whole idea into the smallest rungs there are: a supply zone built on the 2-minute chart, then honoured on the 5-minute. The zone does not care which chart drew it. What it needs is the same three things every zone on this site needs: a sweep of the liquidity sitting in it, a shift of structure away from it, and a target standing first in the way. All three arrived inside one hour of the night session.

How to read this page

Three frames from one replay of EUR/USD on FOREXCOM feeds: the first on the 2-minute, the next two on the 5-minute. The marks are the trader's own — X for a sweep, MSS and bos for shifts, the grey supply boxes, the red Protected high line, the pink LIQUIDITY SWEEP pill, and the red $$$ line naming the sell-side pool. Prices are quoted as the axis shows them. The replay ends with the short still open, so this page reports what the target did, not a closed ticket.

The night opens with price under a prior supply at 1.09830–1.09860, and the Protected high named at about 1.09835 — the close that would cancel every short idea below it. Into that supply the 2-minute prints two swept highs, X at about 1.09825 and X at about 1.09805: buy-side taken, fuel spent, no follow-through. That is the first lesson repeated from the sweep model — the sweep is the invitation, never the entry.

What follows is a staircase: MSS down at about 1.09745, a new supply left at 1.09780–1.09800; another sweep X at about 1.09750; another supply at 1.09760–1.09790; MSS again at about 1.09695 and about 1.09685; and finally an X at the low near 1.09615, where the drop takes its own sell-side liquidity. Each MSS leaves a zone; the zone that matters is the lowest one still untouched — the box at roughly 1.09740–1.09780. On the next chart it gets a name: 2MIN SUPPLY.

Step up one rung and nothing about the idea changes — only the candles get fatter. The 2-minute's lowest untouched supply is carried over as the 2MIN SUPPLY box, 1.09740–1.09780. After 04:30 the 5-minute rallies into it, printing bos marks on the way up, and between 05:15 and 06:15 it takes the high sitting inside the zone — 1.09777, flagged with the LIQUIDITY SWEEP pill and the shaded sweep window. Buy-side resting in a supply zone, collected.

Then the shift: bos down at about 1.09710, MSS confirmed near 1.09700. Sweep, shift, zone — the sequence from the full sequence, compressed into an hour on the smallest charts. And the target is already named on the board: the red $$$ line at the night lows, about 1.09597 — the sell-side pool the earlier staircase left behind. Per the origin-and-target rule, after a shift you target the next liquidity in the direction of travel, and here it is drawn before the order, not after.

Why a 2-minute zone is allowed to work on the 5-minute

A zone is not a timeframe artefact; it is a record of where orders caused a move. The 2-minute simply saw that displacement first. What the 5-minute adds is the trigger: its own sweep of the zone's high and its own shift down. Without that trigger the box is just a rectangle on a small chart — see BoS quality for what counts as the shift.

The ticket in the corner says it plainly: a short is on, 0.2, badge Open. From the shift, price delivers down to about 1.09620, then rebounds into a lower supply at 1.09660–1.09690 — the zone the shift's own displacement left — and taps it between 07:15 and 07:45. That tap is the moment of truth for the position: it comes back to within a few pips of where a confirmation entry would sit, and it is also the line the stop logic watches.

Then the floor goes. Price breaks the $$$ pool at 1.09597 and prints lows near 1.09540–1.09560 — target paid and exceeded, exactly what a sell-side pool below a shifted market is for. The replay's last candles retrace into the tapped supply again, around 1.09660, with the position still open. That ending is left honest on purpose: the setup's job was to reach the pool, and it did; what the ticket does after that is a management decision, not a chart pattern. See scaling and re-entry for the site's rules on exactly this moment.

Review

What this case proves

Done right

  • The zone was drawn at the displacement, on the chart that saw it first — the 2-minute.
  • The protected high (1.09835) was named before any entry existed.
  • The 5m sweep of the zone's own high (1.09777) was waited for; the rally into the zone was not sold blind.
  • The shift was taken from the 5m's own bos and MSS, not borrowed from the 2m.
  • The target was the next pool ($$$ at 1.09597), drawn before the order and honoured by the exit logic.
  • The open ending is reported as open — the chart shows what happened, not a tidy fiction.

The lesson in one line

Timeframes do not own zones — displacement does. A supply left by a 2-minute impulse is as real as one left by a 4-hour impulse; what turns it into a trade is the entry chart sweeping the liquidity inside it and shifting away. Draw the zone where you see it, pull the trigger where you trade.

Keep this in proportion

One replay session, one pair, and a position that was still open when the recording stopped — the giveback after the target is part of the record, not a footnote. Small-timeframe zones also fail more often than they pay; the stop above the swept high is what makes the failure cheap. This is an illustration of the sequence, not evidence of an edge.

Repeatable steps

Trading a zone from a smaller chart

  1. Mark the staircase on the small chart.Every MSS leaves a zone; keep only the untouched ones. Here: three 2m supplies under the prior supply at 1.09830–1.09860.
  2. Name the protected swing before anything else.The close that cancels the read — 1.09835 here. Write it down; it outranks your stop.
  3. Carry the lowest untouched zone up to your entry chart and label it by origin.2MIN SUPPLY, 1.09740–1.09780. The label is a promise: this zone needs its own trigger on this chart.
  4. Wait for the entry chart to sweep the liquidity inside the zone.The 5m took the zone's high at 1.09777 between 05:15 and 06:15. No sweep, no trade — a zone tapped from below without a sweep is just resistance.
  5. Wait for the shift on the entry chart.bos then MSS down through 1.09710 / 1.09700. The 2m's earlier shifts do not count here.
  6. Sell the near edge, stop above the swept high, target the next pool.Limit ≈ 1.09740, stop ≈ 1.09780–1.09800, target the $$$ at 1.09597. Measure the R before the order, not after the fill.
  7. Decide the management rule before the target prints.Pool reached, position open, retrace into a tapped zone: that is a plan question (scale, move stop, or hold), answered by your rules — see scaling & re-entry.

When to skip

Small zones, strict gates

  • If the rally into the zone never sweeps its internal high, stand aside — the liquidity that pays your stop is still sitting in front of you.
  • If the shift comes only on the zone's own timeframe and not on your entry chart, it is not your trade yet.
  • If the stop above the swept high leaves less than 1:2 to the next pool, skip: small-timeframe zones give small R, and this one only worked because the pool was far.
  • If the protected high (1.09835) closes at any point, every short below it is cancelled — including filled ones.
  • If the $$$ pool has already been taken before your entry, the target is spent; do not aim at a swept pool.

How to read this page

These are four of the five slides of the post, in the order the post tells the story: 4H first, then the 15m setup, then the reaction, then the cover board with every mark at once. The slides are stylised and carry no price axis, so this page quotes no prices and no reward-to-risk; the levels are named instead — protected high, OB, FVG, SSL — and the order rules are given in those names. The slide captions are the author’s; everything else is my reading. The fifth slide was not sent, so the close of the trade is not shown beyond the break of SSL.

Step 1 · Higher timeframe

The 4H says which side of the 15m to trade

What the slide shows

  1. Two large bearish candles. The 4H falls hard enough that nothing on the lower timeframes can argue with it.
  2. The first Sh at the rally high. The bounce that follows the fall shifts structure down on its way: the rally never becomes a reversal.
  3. The second Sh at a lower high. A second, lower shift. Bearish structure in two strokes: lower high, then continuation.
  4. “Liquidity was taken above.” The high over the first shift was swept before or as the structure turned. The buy-side above is clean, so nothing rests above to pull price up.
  5. The dashed $$$ line under the lows. Sell-side liquidity resting below on the 4H: the magnet the whole idea points at, two timeframes down.

Why it matters

Nothing is traded on this slide. Its only job is to answer one question: if the 15m offers a short and a long in the same session, which one is taken? With the 4H bearish, its highs swept and its sell-side untouched below, the answer is the short — and every 15m long in that session is a counter-trend scalp against this picture. That is the first line of the post’s caption made concrete: structure on the higher timeframe is what turns a pattern into a plan.

Rule to carry forward

Before any lower-timeframe setup is judged, name the higher-timeframe direction and the liquidity it leaves untouched. The setup trades in that direction or not at all.

Step 2 · Lower timeframe

Sweep, shift, and a zone worth waiting for

What the slide shows

  1. The X at the swing high. The 15m rallies into the 4H’s ceiling and takes the swing high — buy-side liquidity collected, exactly where the higher timeframe said it would be.
  2. The dashed protected high above it. The high of the sweeping candle. Nothing may close above it while the short idea lives; it is where the stop goes.
  3. MSS down. The fall out of the sweep closes below the last higher low. With the 4H bearish, this shift is with the trend, not against it.
  4. The OB, with Sh at its left edge. The last up candles before the displacement down: the bearish order block, drawn at the shift and left for mitigation.
  5. The FVG at the block’s near edge. The imbalance the displacement left below the OB. Entry at the block’s edge and entry at the gap’s top are nearly the same order, and the gap is the evidence the displacement was real.
  6. The dashed $$$ beside the FVG and the rising trendline of lows below. Internal liquidity in the way of the fall: the first magnets price meets on its way to the 4H sell-side.

Why it matters

This slide is the whole model in one frame, and it is why the post says the concepts must work together. The sweep alone is nothing — highs get swept in ranges too. The shift alone is nothing — shifts fail at higher-timeframe zones. But a sweep at the 4H ceiling, a shift with the 4H direction, and a zone left by that shift with an imbalance at its edge: that is a location, a trigger and an invalidation, which is what a plan is. Separately they are labels; together they are an order.

Rule to carry forward

Demand three things before a zone earns an order: a sweep of something that mattered, a shift with the higher timeframe, and an imbalance at the zone’s edge. Two of three is a watch, not a trade.

Step 3 · Delivery

The tap, the reaction, and the lows going

What the slide shows

  1. Mitigation into the OB. Price returns to the block and trades about halfway into it. A limit at the near edge fills; a limit deeper fills too, this time.
  2. The reaction. The block holds on the first tap and price turns down from inside it. No second visit, no drift through the top.
  3. The blue corridor. The fall travels through the internals marked on the previous slide — the $$$ and the trendline of lows are taken on the way, as internals are.
  4. The SSL line, then the break. The session lows are reached and then broken by one long candle near 01:30: the 4H sell-side from slide 1, collected.

Why it matters

Two habits visible here. First, the entry was not chased: the zone was drawn at the shift and price was allowed to come to it, which is the difference between a planned risk and a donated one. Second, the target was never the first thing in the way. The internals and the session lows all stood between entry and the 4H sell-side, and the trade was sized to reach the pool, not the first line. Compare the two gold replays on this site, where the first target sat under 1:2 twice: this post targets the liquidity that the higher timeframe named, and that is the version worth copying.

Rule to carry forward

Let the higher timeframe name the target. Internals in the way are magnets on the route, not destinations; size the idea to the pool.

What the slides leave out

No price axis, no stop line, no target line, no size, and no clock on the wait between shift and tap. Those five things are exactly the five a posted breakdown can afford to omit and a live account cannot. When you copy this setup on your own chart, supply them yourself: the numbers, the invalidation, the target, the risk, and the hours you are willing to wait.

EntrySell limit at the OB’s near edge, the FVG’s topThe edge of the block and the top of the gap are the same order here. The slide shows the tap going about halfway in, so an order at the edge fills with room to spare.

Measure1:2 minimum, on your own chartThe slides carry no axis, so no reward-to-risk is quoted here. On your chart: if the distance from the OB edge to the SSL pool is under twice the distance from the edge to the protected high, skip it.
SizeFixed riskWhatever the stop distance costs per lot, size it to 0.5–1% of the account. The wider the protected high sits, the smaller the position.

The gap is evidence, not a target

An FVG at the zone’s edge invites two mistakes: entering at the gap’s bottom instead of the block’s edge (a deeper, greedier fill that misses half the taps), and treating the gap as proof the trade cannot fail. Displacement leaves gaps; some zones with gaps still break. The stop does not move because the gap exists.

Review

What the post teaches, and what it cannot

What it does well

  • Direction first: the 4H slide comes before any 15m detail, so the setup is judged in context.
  • The sweep is of a level that mattered — the swing high under the 4H ceiling — not of any high.
  • The zone is drawn at the shift and labelled with its reason: “for mitigation”.
  • The protected high is named, which is the stop level in all but numbers.
  • The target is higher-timeframe liquidity, not the first line in the way.

What it cannot show

  • No prices, so no reward-to-risk: the 1:2 test happens on your chart or not at all.
  • No stop or target lines, so the exact invalidation and exit are inferred from the marks.
  • No wait time between shift and tap: an hour or a day changes the trade’s character.
  • No losing version. A posted breakdown shows the chain working; the failed-trade page shows the same chain breaking.
  • The fifth slide was not sent, so the trade’s close is unseen.

The caption is the point

“The key is knowing how these concepts work together, not just identifying them on a chart.” On this site that sentence has a home: the full sequence page. Identifying is being able to point at an X, an MSS and an OB. Knowing is being able to say which one must print first, which one invalidates the idea, and which one pays for it — in that order, before price arrives.

Repeatable steps

Run this the same way every session

  1. Name the higher-timeframe direction and its untouched liquidity.Shifts and lower highs for the direction; the pool above or below for the target. Write both before looking at the 15m.
  2. Wait for the 15m to sweep something that mattered in that direction.A swing high for a short into 4H bearish. A random high in a range is not it.
  3. Mark the protected point of the sweeping candle.That is the stop level. If it is too far for 1:2 to the pool, the setup is already dead.
  4. Wait for the shift with the higher timeframe.MSS down for a short. No shift, no zone, no order.
  5. Draw the zone the shift left, and check for an imbalance at its edge.OB plus FVG. The gap is the evidence the displacement was real.
  6. Place the order at the near edge, stop beyond the protected point, target at the pool.One order, three numbers, fixed risk. Then close the chart and let the alert work.
  7. Hold through the internals; exit at the pool or on the invalidation.Internals taken on the way are the trade working, not a reason to move the stop.
  8. Record it: the chain, the numbers, the wait, the outcome.Wins and losses alike. The chain is only as good as the log that tests it.

When to skip

The same setup, refused

Do not take it if

  • The higher timeframe is flat or pointed the other way.
  • The sweep took a level nobody was resting behind.
  • The shift came without an imbalance at the zone’s edge.
  • The protected point to the pool pays under 1:2.
  • The zone has already been mitigated once in the session.

This setup had

  • A 4H bearish structure with its buy-side already swept.
  • A 15m sweep of the swing high at that ceiling.
  • A shift with the 4H, leaving OB and FVG together.
  • A named protected high as the invalidation.
  • A higher-timeframe pool as the target, and price paying it.

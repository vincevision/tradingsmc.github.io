Frame 1 · Fuel

The 2h marks the fuel

What the frame shows

  1. A staircase, not a range. From above 1.1100 on the 13th, price falls in legs; the 2h side is short before any zone exists.
  2. X one, ≈ 1.1028. The 15th’s swing high, swept by the 16th’s wick — the buys from the first bounce cleared out.
  3. X two, ≈ 1.1020. The 16th’s high, swept by the 17th’s tall blue candle tagging the same area. Two sweeps into the same neighbourhood: the zone is earning its box.
  4. MSS ≈ 1.0969. Across the 17th into the 18th, price closes below the pullback’s last higher low. The bracket is the gate; the live tag 1.09673 sits just under it — the shift is fresh at the cursor.

Why it comes first

Because the sweeps are the reason the box will matter. A supply drawn on a staircase with no liquidity taken above it is just a rectangle; two X lines into the same band say who is trapped and where their stops sit.

Rule to carry forward

Mark the sweeps before the zone. If you cannot put an X on a taken high inside or under the band you are about to draw, the band is a hope, not a supply.

Frame 2 · Context

The box goes where the drop began

What the frame shows

  1. One box, ≈ 1.1008–1.1024. Drawn at the last candle up before the MSS leg — the origin of the drop — and stretched right across the rest of the session.
  2. Both sweeps inside its floor. The X at ≈ 1.1020 sits just under the box’s lower edge; the zone and the liquidity tell one story instead of two.
  3. The first address below: 1.09478. A plus-handle line under the 18th’s lows — where the drop would be going if it went. Marked now, before any entry, so the reward is known before the risk.

Why it comes second

Because the box decides the area and the line below decides whether the area is worth trading at all. From the box’s floor to 1.09478 is roughly 60 pips of room; a ticket written at the floor with a 6-pip stop is a trade. The same box with a pool 10 pips down is not.

Rule to carry forward

Draw the target’s address in the same sitting as the zone. If the pool below does not pay several times the stop the zone implies, the setup is finished before the lower timeframe is even opened.

Frame 3 · Pools

The pools get names

What the frame shows

  1. $$$ at ≈ 1.0954. The 18th’s low, the first sell-side cluster below — promoted from an idea to a line with a label.
  2. A deeper pool at 1.09376. The next address if the first one breaks; marked, not targeted.
  3. The 2h tap. On the 22nd into the 23rd the retracement rallies into the box’s floor and wicks ≈ 1.1009 — inside the zone, under its top. On the 2h this is a tap to watch, not an entry: the 2h has no shift of its own here, only the older MSS.
  4. Live at 1.09924. The cursor catches the retracement off the floor — the moment the 15m chart becomes the working chart.

Why it comes third

Because the pools convert the zone from a ceiling into an invoice. $$$ is what the short is paid for; 1.09376 is what it might print through; the difference between them is greed, and greed gets marked but not booked.

Rule to carry forward

Book the first pool, mark the second. The booked line is the one the size is computed from; the marked line is the one you watch get printed through without moving anything.

Frame 4 · The order

The 15m writes the ticket

What the frame shows

  1. The 15m sweep. The 19th’s early rally wicks into the 2h floor and takes the last 15m swing high — the small x. Fuel, on the working timeframe.
  2. The 15m MSS. The same rally fails and price closes below ≈ 1.0989: the shift that opens the ticket window, an hour or two after the sweep.
  3. The red block, 1.10055–1.10112. The last candles up before that MSS — the 15m order block sitting inside the 2h zone’s floor. Two timeframes, one band, again.
  4. The column to 1.09538. Entry at the block’s near edge (white tag), stop above its top (red tag), target the $$$ pool (blue tag). Reward drawn before the order exists.

Why it comes fourth

Because everything above this frame is context and everything in it is arithmetic. The 2h named the side and the area; the 15m sweep and shift named the timing; the block’s near edge names the price. Selling the 2h tap in frame 3 would have meant a 40-pip stop; selling this block means 5.7.

Orders, as tagged

Entry: 1.10055 — sell limit at the 15m block’s near edge, inside the 2h floor.
Stop: 1.10112 — above the block’s top; the close that says the 15m shift never happened. Risk 5.7 pips.
Target: 1.09538 — the $$$ pool. Reward 51.7 pips. About 9.1 R.

Frame 5 · Delivery

Filled on the pip, printed through

What the frame shows

  1. The tap, to the pip. The 22nd–23rd rally wicks exactly 1.10055 — the limit’s price — and closes back under the block. A limit at the near edge either fills clean or not at all; this one filled clean.
  2. Through the booked line. One leg cuts 1.09538; the wick reaches ≈ 1.0940, below even the marked 1.09376 pool. Nothing was moved on the way down.
  3. Open at 1.09365. At the cursor the tool still shows the 0.2 short running — about 69 pips from the entry, ≈ 12 R floating. The booked plan was 9.1; the extra is the market’s money until a line says otherwise.

Why the open position is not the point

The invoice this page quotes is 1.10055 → 1.09538, the line drawn in frame 4. Whether the runner was closed at the tag, trailed to the 1.09376 pool, or still sat open at the cursor, the decision that made the money was the wait: three and a half days between the 15m MSS and the tap, with no order in the market.

Keep it in proportion

9.1 R on a 5.7-pip stop is a 15m stop inside a 2h zone — the tightest stop the model allows and the first one a deeper tap would take out. Two winning taps in a fortnight, gold and euro, describe a style. The stopped case sits beside them on purpose; backtest the gate before the size follows the R.

Review

What earned the print, and what to watch

  • Every mark preceded the order. Both X lines, the MSS bracket, the box, both pools and the red block all exist in frames 1–4; the fill in frame 5 touches none of them out of order. That is note 12’s gate on two timeframes.
  • The stop sat beyond the 15m structure, not inside it. 1.10112 is above the block the shift left; a stop at the 2h box top (≈ 1.1024) would have tripled the risk for no extra idea.
  • The target was the first pool, and the print through changed nothing. 1.09538 booked, 1.09376 marked, ≈ 1.0940 printed. The marked line was watched, not chased.
  • Watch: the open position past the booked line. At the cursor the 0.2 short still runs below its own target. Leaving a winner open past its line is a management decision with its own rules — name them before the next tap, or close at the tag.
  • Watch: no imbalance labelled on either timeframe. As with the gold tap, the shifts here are taken on closes. By the site’s own definition a close-only shift is the weaker kind; when the gap is missing and the block is thin, stand aside.

Repeatable

The sequence, as this trade ran it

  1. Mark the sweeps on the higher timeframe. X on every high the pullbacks take into the band you suspect. Two X lines into one neighbourhood is a supply earning its box.
  2. Draw the box at the origin of the drop the MSS confirms, stretched right. Its floor is where the lower timeframe goes to work.
  3. Book the first pool below, mark the second. The booked line sets the size; the marked line sets nothing.
  4. Drop to the working timeframe and wait for its own sweep and MSS. The 2h tap is a notification, not an entry.
  5. Sell the tap of the block the shift left — limit at its near edge, stop above its top, target at the booked pool.
  6. Journal the frames. Marks, box, pools, ticket, delivery. Five frames here; four on the gold tap. A trade with fewer frames than decisions is a trade missing its reasons.

The same six lines as the routine on key points and the gold tap’s repeat block: the higher timeframe supplies the marks, the shift supplies the timing, the shift’s timeframe supplies the target.

Memorise

A liquidity sweep followed by a shift can be enough — but if that liquidity sweep came from a demand or supply zone, the shift that follows adds more probability. Seen three ways in Exhibit D: two shorts on the euro, and the same gate in the mirror for longs.

Discipline

When this trade does not exist

  • No sweep into the zone. A tap of the floor that takes no high first leaves its buys above your entry — the stopped case’s invoice, one timeframe down.
  • No 15m MSS before the tap. Selling the 2h tap on the 2h chart means a 40-pip stop and a hope; the model’s whole edge here is the 15m shift buying a 5.7-pip one.
  • The tap closes above the block. A 15m close over 1.10112 cancels the shift; the limit comes off, or never goes on.
  • The first pool is too close. Under 1:2 from the block’s edge to $$$, the gate passed but the trade cannot pay for the taps that will fail.

Waiting for the Real Zone

A long on AUD/USD with the H4 trend. Price reached H4 demand, the M15 shifted bullish, and then the chart offered two entries that looked right and were early. The trade was taken at the third: the M15 demand zone at the origin of the shift, after the liquidity above it had been cleared.

  • Long
  • H4 → M15
  • Target: H4 swing high
  • TP hit · ≈ 1:3.2

How to read this page

X marks a liquidity sweep. IDM is inducement: the first pullback low after a shift, which draws in early buyers. $$$ marks resting liquidity not yet taken. 0.5 is the midpoint of the H4 leg. The post's cover slide was not among the screenshots, so the title above is mine. Reward-to-risk is measured from the position boxes.

Step 1 · Bias and location

H4: bullish, back at demand, not an entry yet

What happened

  1. The rally began with a sweep. The small X at the bottom left marks a low that was run before price turned up.
  2. Price made a strong leg higher. A run of bullish candles to a swing high. The line drawn across the top marks that high. It becomes the target.
  3. Demand sits at the midpoint. The red 0.5 line is the halfway point of the leg. The demand zone's top edge is on it, so the whole zone is in discount.
  4. Price came back and wicked through the zone (X). One candle's wick ran the full depth of the demand zone and slightly below its low, then closed back above. The next candle is bullish.

Why this is “not an entry yet”

Everything on this chart is favourable: bullish structure, a pullback into discount, a demand zone, and a sweep of the zone's own low. But an H4 candle covers four hours. This slide tells you where to look and which way. It cannot tell you where buyers actually stepped in, or where a stop of sensible size would go.

Rule to carry forward

H4 gives bias and location only. When price reaches H4 demand in discount, move to the M15 and start again from the beginning: what was swept, has structure shifted, where is the zone.

Step 2 · Confirmation

M15: price takes the low, then structure shifts

What happened

  1. A range formed above the H4 zone. Hours of sideways movement left a clear low. The long X line runs along it.
  2. The range low was swept into H4 demand. One hard drop took out the low, went the full depth of the large olive box and slightly beneath it. This is the H4 wick from slide 1, seen up close.
  3. Price reversed just as hard. A V-shaped recovery back into the range.
  4. A second, smaller sweep (short X). Price dipped once more and ran a minor low left by the recovery, then turned.
  5. MSS. The next leg closed above the high of the recovery. It left an imbalance (IMB).
  6. M15 demand is drawn at the origin. The smaller box labelled Demand covers the base of that leg, from under the imbalance down to the low of the small sweep.
  7. IDM. The first small pullback low after the shift is marked as inducement.

Why this matters

This is the sequence from the other case studies: liquidity taken inside a higher-timeframe zone, then a shift with displacement. The M15 demand is the refined zone inside the H4 one. From here there is a reason to plan a long.

The IDM label is the new idea. That first pullback low is the obvious place for early buyers to put their stops. Until it has been taken, the pullback has not really started.

Rule to carry forward

After the MSS, mark two things: the demand zone at the origin of the shift, and the first pullback low above it. The zone is where you buy. The low is liquidity that price will probably take on the way there.

Step 3 · The entries that were early

A small structure forms. It looks like the entry.

What happened

  1. Price ranged for about ten hours. After the shift it went sideways near the highs. The dotted line marks the top of that range.
  2. The range high held. A late push came close to the earlier high and failed.
  3. Price dropped through the inducement. A sharp fall took out the IDM low and the lows of the range.
  4. It bounced before reaching demand. A strong bullish candle followed straight away. This is the “small structure”: inducement taken, a sharp rejection, a higher-timeframe bullish bias. It looks like the entry.
  5. The bounce left a low ($$$). That low sits just above the M15 demand zone. Anyone who bought the bounce has a stop under it.

Why it was not the entry

  • Price turned in empty space. There is no zone where it bounced. The M15 demand is still beneath it and still untouched.
  • The bounce created fresh liquidity directly on top of that zone.
  • The move down had taken the inducement and had not yet reached the place the shift came from.

The slide shows the plan that respects this: entry at the top edge of the M15 demand, stop below the zone, target at the H4 high.

Rule to carry forward

A reaction is only an entry if it happens at your zone. If price bounces above an untouched zone, the bounce has made a new low for price to come back and take. Keep the order at the zone.

Two early entries on this chart

The first was buying H4 demand on touch in slide 1, which was followed by the hard drop through the zone. The second was buying this bounce. Both were in the right direction. Both were stopped or deep in drawdown before the move began.

Step 4 · Fill, management and outcome

Only then does price reach the real zone

What happened

  1. The $$$ low was taken. Shortly after midnight on the chart, price fell through it. Buyers of the bounce were stopped out.
  2. Price entered the M15 demand and went deep. The limit order at the top edge filled, and price carried on more than halfway through the zone.
  3. The bottom of the zone held. A few candles based inside it, then price left sharply.
  4. The move was not a straight line. Price rallied, ranged for a couple of hours, and gave back a large part of the first leg before continuing.
  5. Target reached. Price climbed to the H4 high and wicked through it.

What to take from it

  • The wait was about thirteen hours from the shift to the fill. The trade itself took roughly nine more.
  • The fill was deep. As with Bitcoin, a stop inside the zone would have been hit. It has to go under the whole zone.
  • Three layers of liquidity were cleared before the entry: the inducement low, the range lows, and the $$$ low. Each one was a group of early buyers.
  • The mid-trade pullback was a test of management. Price retraced deeply on the way to target. A stop moved to breakeven too soon is at risk on a pullback like that.

Rule to carry forward

Before acting, list what has to happen first. Here it was: inducement taken, liquidity above the zone taken, price inside the zone. Two of three is not enough.

Everything as one

The whole trade in a single picture

A schematic of the M15 sequence, not a copy of the chart. The H4 is already bullish and price is arriving at H4 demand.

1 · SWEEP INTO H4 DEMAND

Price takes the range low (X) and drives through the H4 demand zone, then reverses hard. Buying the H4 zone on touch was the first early entry.

2 · SHIFT

After a second small sweep, the M15 closes above the recovery high. The imbalance and the M15 demand mark where the move began.

3 · INDUCEMENT AND A FALSE START

Price ranges, drops through the IDM low and bounces above the zone. Buying that bounce was the second early entry. It leaves the $$$ low.

4 · THE REAL ZONE

Price takes the $$$ low and trades deep into the M15 demand. The limit order fills about thirteen hours after the shift.

5 · TARGET

Price rallies, pulls back once, and reaches the H4 swing high. About 3.2R.

THE LOGIC IN ONE SENTENCE

With H4 bias, buy the M15 demand at the origin of the shift, and only after the inducement and the lows above the zone have been taken.

Order placement

Entry, stop loss and take profit

Entry

Buy limit at the top edge of the M15 demand zone.

  • The zone is the base of the leg that made the MSS, directly under the imbalance.
  • Place it after the shift and leave it through the range and the bounce.
  • Price went more than halfway through the zone. The top-edge entry filled early and sat in drawdown before turning.
  • Buys fill on the ask. Add the spread to the limit price.

Stop loss

Below the bottom of the M15 demand zone, plus a buffer.

  • The bottom of the zone is the low of the small sweep that launched the MSS. If it breaks, the shift is void.
  • On the chart the grey box ends a fraction below the zone. That gap is the buffer.
  • It is not under the H4 zone's low. That would be safer and would roughly double the risk on this chart.
  • Do not tighten it into the zone. This trade needed most of that room.

Take profit

The H4 swing high. About 3.2R.

  • It is the high marked on the H4 slide: the top of the leg price is pulling back from.
  • With H4 bullish, a return to that high is the minimum expectation for the next leg.
  • The top of the M15 range lies on the way and is taken first.
  • Price wicked through the level here. Set the order a few pips before it regardless.

Managing the position

The post shows a single entry and a single exit. The points below are my suggested handling, not something marked on the slides.

  • While waiting: cancel if an M15 candle closes below the demand zone, or if price reaches the H4 high without filling you.
  • Once filled: expect drawdown inside the zone. Leave the stop where it is.
  • On the way up: trail only behind a confirmed M15 higher low after a new high. This trade pulled back deeply mid-way, and a breakeven stop moved after the first rally may or may not have survived it.

Position size

Fix the money at risk first, then let the stop distance decide the lot size.

Lots = money at risk ÷ (stop in pips × pip value per lot)

On AUD/USD one standard lot moves $10 per pip, because the pair is quoted in US dollars.

Example: $10,000 account, 1% risk = $100. Stop of 20 pips gives 100 ÷ (20 × 10) = 0.50 lots. At 1:3.2 the target pays about $320.

Repeatable strategy

Step-by-step for the next trade

Written for longs. For shorts, mirror it. A “no” at any step ends the process.

  1. Is H4 structure bullish, with a demand zone at or below the 0.5 level?Mark the zone, the midpoint and the swing high. Set an alert at the zone's top edge.
  2. Price is in H4 demand. Do not buy yet.Move to the M15.
  3. Did the M15 take a low inside the H4 zone and reverse?A range low or swing low swept, followed by a sharp recovery.
  4. Did a candle close above the recovery high with displacement?This is the MSS. It should leave an imbalance.
  5. Mark the M15 demand at the origin and the inducement above it.The zone runs from under the imbalance to the low that launched the shift. The first pullback low after the MSS is the IDM.
  6. Place the buy limit at the top of the M15 demand.Stop below the zone, target at the H4 swing high. Check it pays at least 1:2. Size for 0.5–1% risk.
  7. Has the inducement been taken?If not, the pullback has not begun. Do nothing.
  8. Did price bounce above the zone?That is not your entry. Mark the bounce low as new liquidity and keep the order where it is.
  9. Filled at the zone. Hold, then record it.Exit at target or stop. Log each early entry the chart offered and what happened to it.

Filters

When this setup should be skipped

Do not take it if

  • The H4 demand sits above the 0.5 level. You would be buying in premium.
  • An H4 candle closes below the H4 demand zone.
  • The M15 shifts without first taking a low.
  • The M15 demand has already been touched since the shift.
  • An M15 candle closes below the M15 demand.
  • The H4 high is closer than 1:2 from the zone.
  • High-impact AUD or USD news is due while the order is resting.

This trade had all of these

  • Bullish H4 structure and demand in discount.
  • A sweep of the M15 range low and of the H4 zone's low.
  • An M15 MSS with an imbalance.
  • An untouched M15 demand at the origin of the shift.
  • Inducement and a further liquidity low, both taken before the fill.
  • A clear H4 high about 3.2R away.

Keep this in proportion

In hindsight the “real zone” is obvious because it is the one that worked. In real time, the bounce in step 3 could have been the start of the move, and the order at the zone would have been left unfilled. Waiting for the origin zone means accepting missed trades in exchange for better entries. Whether that exchange pays is something only your own record of filled, missed and stopped trades can show.

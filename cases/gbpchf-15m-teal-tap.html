The tap that paid the teal

How to read this page

Three frames: the marked canvas at the first pause (1), the bounce arriving at the box with both X lines, both BoS brackets, the low pool and the teal band named (2), and the refined ticket with its column running into the teal (3). One supply box pays two graded sweeps — at its top for leg two, at its floor for this trade — which is the cleanest illustration on this site of why zones are drawn once and waited on.

Step 1 · Structure and origin

The top, the first leg, the box

Two legs down, one box between them, and a pool under everything. The canvas is complete before the trade exists.

The range tops at equal highs ≈ 1.10160 and leg one leaves from the small supply: a clean fall to ≈ 1.09730. The pullback that follows rallies to ≈ 1.10090–1.10110 — the first X line — and its wick is sweep A: liquidity taken at the big box’s top edge before leg two even starts. Then leg two: a body close below ≈ 1.09700 (the BoS bracket) takes the first leg’s low, wicks print ≈ 1.09630, and the market pauses.

What leg two leaves behind is the whole trade: the big grey supply, ≈ 1.09950–1.10100, the origin candles of the displacing fall. Drawn once, on the 23rd, at ≈ 09:00–12:00. Nothing about it changes for the next fourteen hours — which is the point. A zone is not a suggestion that follows price; it is an address price must return to.

Step 2 · The return and the trigger

The fourteen-hour return

Most missed trades are not missed — they are escaped. This bounce took half a day to arrive, and the box never moved an inch.

From ≈ 1.09630 the market grinds upward for fourteen hours: small candles, overlapping wicks, no displacement — a bounce, not a reversal, and the checklist’s step 6 in real time: wait for the return. At 24th 04:30–05:00 it arrives: a wick to ≈ 1.10000, through the second X at ≈ 1.09955 — the internal high resting along the box’s floor — and into the supply’s lower edge. One wick. Sweep B, taken at the zone: the heavy door of the memorised rule, stamped on the same box that sweep A paid at its top a day earlier.

The shift follows within the hour: a body close below ≈ 1.09850 — the bounce’s last higher low — with displacement. And beneath it all, the map was already drawn: the white line at 1.09630, the pool this whole move exists to raid, and the teal demand ≈ 1.09550–1.09620 standing just under it, its top edge the honest place to book.

Memorise

A liquidity sweep followed by a shift can be enough — but if that liquidity sweep came from a demand or supply zone, the shift that follows adds more probability. Sweep B came from the supply’s own floor; the shift at 1.09850 carries the full weight of that rule.

Step 3 · Delivery

The ticket and the delivery

Fifteen pips of risk against thirty-six of reward, measured while the bounce was still a wick.

The refinement keeps the tap candles and the fall’s origin: the red box, 1.09953–1.10106. Entry at its floor, stop above its top, target 1.09596 — the teal band’s top edge, booked a hair inside the zone so the defenders pay on their way in rather than being asked to pay at their doorstep. Risk ≈ 15 pips, reward ≈ 36: ≈ 1:2.3, cleared before the tap, re-cleared at the fill.

The delivery is unhurried and complete: through the BoS level, through the dotted 1.09700, across the white pool line at 1.09630 — the raid paid — and into the teal, where the 08:00–09:30 wicks tag 1.09596 exactly. At the capture price sits 1.09684, inside the column’s lower half, target tagged, stop never threatened by so much as a close. One zone, drawn once, waited on for fourteen hours, paid twice.

Do it again

Repeatable steps

  1. Draw the box when the shift leaves it, not when you feel like trading: origin candles of the displacing leg, refined to bodies.
  2. Mark every X the box owns — internal highs along its floor, pullback highs at its top. They are the taps’ addresses.
  3. Name both magnets below before the return: the pool line and the opposing zone’s edge. Book at the edge; let the pool be the reason, not the hope.
  4. Measure the ticket at the box, before the tap: under 1:2, the box stays on the chart and the order stays out of it.
  5. Wait. Hours if needed. A grind bounce arriving is not danger — it is the return the checklist waits for.
  6. One wick into the box, then the shift: sweep at the zone plus a body close beyond the bounce’s last opposite point. Both, or nothing.
  7. After the fill, the stop is the only message the position sends. Everything else inside the column is noise with candles.

Discipline

When this trade does not exist

  • The bounce closes through. Any 15m close above 1.10106 before your fill occupies the supply and cancels the shift; the box becomes resistance history, not an order.
  • The tap never comes. A bounce that stalls at the second X without wicking into the box leaves the limit unfilled — and unfilled is not missed, it is the gate working.
  • Sweep without shift. A wick through the second X with the bounce’s higher lows intact is a raid, not a trigger; wait for the BoS at ≈ 1.09850 or its equivalent.
  • The teal pre-defends. If an earlier leg already tapped the teal band and bounced hard, the top-edge book sits on occupied ground; take the pool-line book or stand aside.
  • Ratio under 1:2. From the box floor to your chosen book, measured before the tap. The fourteen-hour wait does not earn the right to a bad price.

How to read this page

Every mark is the owner’s — the X on the swept equal highs, the two bos labels, the grey supply and demand, the red refined block, the blue reward column and its arrow. Read it with the live checklist open: section 6 maps all ten steps onto these three frames.

Step 1 · Context and liquidity

The canvas and the pool

Before any entry exists, the chart says three things: where the money is resting, who took it, and what the taking cost them.

The rally from 3,540 is honest structure — the first bos label at ≈ 3,637 is the market clearing its own higher lows on the way up. Near the top it builds equal highs at ≈ 3,675: two touches, the red X line, and above them the stops and breakout buys of everyone who thinks 3,675 is a ceiling worth buying over. That cluster is the pool.

On the 23rd, ≈ 09:00, a spike to ≈ 3,690 takes the pool in one wick — and buys nothing. No follow-through, no higher close: the sweep is a raid, not a rally. Everyone lifted above 3,675 is now trapped above price. This is the fuel sweep of the whole case, and note where it happened: in no-man’s-land, above any zone you could draw at that moment. Per the rule this site memorises, a sweep like this followed by a shift can be enough — but it is the lighter door.

The replay is paused at 3,616.62 with both boxes already drawn: the supply above, the demand below. Nothing has been entered. The canvas is waiting for the shift.

Step 2 · Shift and origin

The shift, and the block it left behind

A sweep is a raid. A shift is the market changing sides in public. Only the second one earns an order.

After the spike, price falls to ≈ 3,630 and rallies again to ≈ 3,672 — a lower high, a retest of the X pool from below that fails to clear it. Then the 24th opens with displacement: strong bearish candles, wicks that do not overlap, and a body close below ≈ 3,617 — the second bos label on frame 1. That close is the shift: the last higher low of the pullback is gone, taken by bodies, not by a wick.

Every displacement leaves an origin. The leg from 3,672 down to 3,617 started at the last up-candles before the fall — the grey box ≈ 3,645–3,660 on frame 1. That box is the supply: the order block the shift left behind. It is fresh (price has not been back to it since it formed), it sits in premium of the leg that made it, and it faces a target with room: the demand floor below.

Refinement comes next, on frame 2: inside the grey box the red box keeps only the origin candles’ bodies — 3,645.12 to 3,661.87, the two axis tags. Grey is where you look; red is where the order lives.

Memorise

A liquidity sweep followed by a shift can be enough — but if that liquidity sweep came from a demand or supply zone, the shift that follows adds more probability. Here the fuel sweep (3,690 over the X) came from no-man’s-land; watch what upgrades this trade in step 4: the trigger sweep happens at the zone itself.

Step 3 · The order, measured early

The ticket, drawn before the tap

The order is decided while price is still below the entry. If you cannot measure it there, you cannot trade it later.

Frame 2 carries the whole ticket in three axis tags: 3,661.87 in red (the block’s top, the invalidation line), 3,645.12 in white (the block’s floor, the entry), 3,595.31 in blue (the demand’s top, the target). Risk 16.75. Reward 49.81. ≈ 1:2.97, computed while the replay sits at 3,616.62 with no tap yet — which is the only honest moment to compute it.

The blue column also answers the oldest question in zone trading: why book at the demand’s top and not its middle? Because the demand is where the opposing defenders live. A target placed inside a zone asks those defenders to pay you; a target placed at the zone’s edge asks price only to reach them. The difference here is the whole of the blue column’s lower half — money left on the table in exchange for a target that does not fight.

Step 4 · Trigger and delivery

The wait, the tap, the delivery

Nothing happens until price comes back on its own. The tap is the trigger; chasing is a different trade with a worse price.

From 3,616.62 the replay runs: on the 24th, 06:00–12:00, a rally climbs into the red box and wicks to ≈ 3,648 — inside the block, above its floor, below its top. Read that wick twice, because it does two jobs: it is the tap that fills a limit at 3,645.12, and it is a sweep taken at the zone — the return leg’s own high, grabbed at the supply’s floor before the fall. That is the heavier door of the memorised rule: the trigger sweep came from the zone, so the shift behind it carries more probability than the frame-1 sweep alone ever did.

What does not happen matters as much: no candle closes above 3,661.87 before the fall. The block holds its top; the invalidation line is never touched; the stop never works for a living.

Then delivery, in one leg: the rejection candle leaves the block, takes 3,620 and 3,600 in its body, crosses the target 3,595.31 — paid — and prints wicks ≈ 3,562–3,565 inside the demand 3,575–3,595 before closing ≈ 3,568.91. The demand’s first defenders show as small bullish candles at the low: the reason the ticket booked at the zone’s top, printed through in full, ≈ 3 R in the box and more on the wick.

The maths

Entry, SL & TP — and why they sit there

Entry 3,645.12
Sell limit at the refined block’s floor: the first price the zone offers on the tap. Not the middle, not a market order after the wick — the edge, or nothing.
Stop 3,661.87
The block’s top, plus spread and a small buffer. A body close above the refined supply means the supply failed and the shift is cancelled — the idea, not just the price, is wrong there. Risk 16.75.
The wider stop, priced
Above the X at 3,675 the risk is ≈ 30 and the ratio falls to ≈ 1:1.7; above the 3,690 sweep spike it is ≈ 45 and ≈ 1:1.1. Those stops protect against wicks; they do not protect the idea, and they price the trade out of its own edge. The invalidation line is the stop; comfort is not a level.
Target 3,595.31
The demand’s top, 49.81 away: ≈ 1:2.97. Booked where the opposing zone begins, not inside it — defenders are not payers.
Cancel rules
Off if a candle closes above 3,661.87 before the fill; off if the target is reached first; untouched stop after the fill — no early break-even, no widening.

Do it again

Repeatable steps

  1. Mark the pool first: equal highs or lows, range edges, session extremes — the lines where stops rest.
  2. Wait for the sweep of that pool and note where it happened: at a zone (heavy door) or in no-man’s-land (light door).
  3. Wait for the shift: a body close beyond the last opposite point, with displacement, after the sweep.
  4. Draw the block the shift left — the origin candles of the displacing leg — and refine it to bodies.
  5. Measure the ticket now: entry at the block’s edge, stop at its invalidation line, target at the opposing zone’s edge. Under 1:2, stop.
  6. Wait for the return. One tap, no close through the block. No chase, ever.
  7. Place it with its cancel rules and leave it alone; record the result, win or lose, frames included.

Discipline

When this trade does not exist

  • No shift after the sweep. The 3,690 raid without the bos-down close at 3,617 is a spiked market, not a short — the pool was taken but nobody changed sides.
  • A close through the block before the tap. Any candle closing above 3,661.87 cancels the supply and the order with it; the shift has failed.
  • The block already tapped. A second visit to the same supply is a used zone; the defenders have paid once and left.
  • The demand too close. With the lower box at 3,595 instead of 3,595.31’s edge — or a nearer opposing zone — the ratio falls under 1:2 and the gate passes a trade that cannot pay for its stops.
  • The tap that never comes. Price falling from 3,616 without returning is not a missed trade; the checklist’s step 6 says no trade is also a position.

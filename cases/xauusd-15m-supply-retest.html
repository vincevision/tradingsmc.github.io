Step 1 · Structure

The retracement sweeps two highs, and that is the warning

What the chart shows

  1. MSS near 3,904.5 at the left edge. The 15m shifted down early. The evening begins as a down move, not a range.
  2. The X at about 3,906.5. A wick to about 3,907.5 takes that high: buy-side liquidity collected at the ceiling before the real fall.
  3. The fall, the bounce, the second line at about 3,899.8. Price drops to about 3,878.5 and rallies back to 3,899.8, a lower high under the swept ceiling.
  4. bos at about 3,872.7, then the X at about 3,872.4. The next leg closes below that low, and around 16:00 a wick re-sweeps it. Break, then sweep of the break: the sell-side is clean.
  5. MSS up at 3,877.13. The rally closes above the last lower high. The retracement is on.
  6. The wick through 3,899.8 at 19:00, to about 3,901.3. The second marked high is taken. Two X highs swept on one retracement.

Why it matters

A retracement that sweeps one high is a pullback. A retracement that sweeps two is a pullback that has eaten the liquidity above it, and liquidity eaten above a down move is what makes the next fall travel. Every level that could stop the short on the way down has already been removed on the way up. That is why the supply formed by the next fall deserves an order, and why the sketch in the next screenshot points down.

Rule to carry forward

Count what the retracement sweeps. Two swept highs against the trend are the setup loading; the shift back with the trend is the trigger.

Step 2 · The shift and the plan

The close below 3,877.13, and a plan drawn in calm

What the chart shows

  1. The fall from 3,901.3. One leg, no drama, straight back through the retracement.
  2. The close below the MSS at 3,877.13. This is the trigger: the 15m has shifted down again, with the trend, after both highs were swept. Everything before this was context.
  3. The wick to about 3,861.5. It tags the top of the lower demand, 3,862.87, and bounces. The first fall always probes what is below; that probe is not the trade.
  4. The grey box, 3,884.31 to 3,896.86. The supply left by the fall that just shifted structure. Drawn now, while price sits at 3,877, ten points away.
  5. The third X at about 3,893.3. The last internal high inside that supply: the liquidity sitting above the future entry.
  6. The sketch. A zigzag up into the box, then one long arrow down to about 3,865.5. The entire trade in two strokes: retest, then fall into the demand.
  7. The two grey boxes below. A thin one near 3,862.5–3,866.5 and the labelled demand 3,852.48–3,862.87: the floor the arrow points at.

Why it matters

Compare this with most losses: the zone, the stop level and the target are all on the chart while price is ten points away and nothing is urgent. When price finally arrives there is nothing left to decide. The sketch also names the end of the trade before it starts, which is the habit that keeps a winner from being given back.

Rule to carry forward

Draw the trade at the shift, not at the tag. If the zone, the invalidation and the target cannot be drawn in calm, they will not survive contact.

Step 3 · Delivery

Price arrives, and the plan becomes three numbers

What the chart shows

  1. The rally from the 3,872 tag to about 3,885. Straight into the bottom edge of the supply. No sweep of the X at 3,893.3 on the way: price enters the zone from underneath without taking the internal high.
  2. The supply turns red, edges labelled 3,896.86 and 3,884.31. The zone is live. The labels are the order: sell at the near edge, which for a short is the bottom one.
  3. The blue box, 3,884.31 down to 3,860.50. The corridor under the supply: the internal range and imbalance the fall has to travel through. It is drawn before the fall, not after it.
  4. The targets on the axis. 3,862.87 in white and 3,860.50 in blue at the corridor floor, the demand 3,852.48–3,862.87 below that, and the sketch arrow ending near 3,865.5.

Why it matters

A sell limit at 3,884.31 fills on this rally as price crosses the edge on its way up. That is the whole execution: no market order, no chasing the tag, no decision at the candle. The corridor matters too, because it tells you what “going well” looks like mid-trade: price inside the blue box, making lower highs along it, is a trade to hold; a close back above 3,884.31 is not.

Rule to carry forward

For a short, the near edge of supply is its bottom. Put the limit there and let the rally deliver it; a close back over that edge before the fall is the setup asking to be re-checked.

Step 4 · Outcome

Two points in, then the whole corridor

What the chart shows

  1. The tag at about 3,886.5. Two points into a twelve-point zone. The limit at 3,884.31 filled on the way up; the top at 3,896.86 was never tested, and neither was the X at 3,893.3.
  2. The fall along the dashed line. Lower highs inside the blue corridor, exactly the “going well” picture from the last section.
  3. The displacement candle through 3,860.50 and 3,862.87. Corridor floor and demand top go in one candle. Both labelled targets are hit here.
  4. The wick to about 3,847.5 and the bounce to 3,855. Below the demand bottom, 3,852.48, on a wick: the demand did its job as a floor on the first test, and the move is over.

Why it matters

Three lessons. One: a shallow tag is enough; the zone does not owe you a test of its far edge, which is why the stop lives beyond the whole zone and not beyond the tag. Two: the corridor gives a hold rule for free, and this trade never broke it until the targets were already paid. Three: the X at 3,893.3 stayed above the entry the entire trade and was never taken. On this site’s inducement page that liquidity above a short is usually taken first; here it was not. Log it as the exception it is, not as permission to ignore it next time.

Rule to carry forward

When the target zone is hit on displacement, take it. The wick beyond it, to 3,847.5, was never the target; demanding it is how a paid trade becomes a given-back one.

Everything as one

The whole evening in ten lines

  2. The ceiling high at 3,906.5 is swept by a wick before the real fall.
  3. The fall breaks the low at 3,872.7 (bos) and then re-sweeps it (X at 3,872.4).
  4. The retracement rallies: MSS up at 3,877.13.
  5. It sweeps the second marked high, 3,899.8, at 19:00 (wick 3,901.3).
  6. It then closes back below 3,877.13: the shift down, with both highs already eaten.
  7. The supply 3,884.31–3,896.86 is drawn at the shift, with the X at 3,893.3 inside it and the sketch: retest, then fall to the demand.
  8. Price rallies through the near edge and fills the sell limit at 3,884.31, tagging 3,886.5.
  9. The fall runs down the blue corridor along its dashed internal line.
  10. Displacement breaks 3,860.50 and 3,862.87; the wick prints 3,847.5; the bounce ends it at 3,855.

Read against the full sequence: sweep, shift, zone, target, in that order, with the zone and the target drawn at the shift. Read against your 5m gold long, it is the mirror image: there the sweeps were of lows and the zone was demand; here the sweeps are of highs and the zone is supply. Same routine, other side of the chart.

What is still missing

As with the 5m case, no higher timeframe appears in any of the four screenshots. A 15m supply inside 4H demand is a scalp with a floor one candle away; the same 15m supply inside 4H supply is the best short on the board. One 4H or 1H screenshot before the session is the difference, and it costs nothing to capture.

Same warning as the 5m case: the first target is too close

1:1.6 to the demand top is under this site’s 1:2 floor. The trade only earns its place because the demand zone sits behind that first line and pays 1:2.4. Two gold cases in a row with a sub-1:2 first target is now a pattern in the log, not an accident: name the zone, not the line in front of it, when you size the idea.

The tighter stop, and why it stays a temptation

A stop above the internal X at 3,893.3 risks about 9.7 instead of 13.1 and lifts the reward-to-risk to about 1:2.2 and 1:3.2. It also dies the first time that X does what Xs do and gets swept. The rule does not change: the stop sits beyond the structure that invalidates the idea, which here is the whole supply zone.

Review

What worked, and what to fix

Done well

  • Both swept highs marked with an X before the shift down, so the setup could be counted, not felt.
  • The supply drawn at the shift, ten points away from price, with both edges labelled to the cent.
  • The plan sketched in two strokes: retest, then fall to a named floor.
  • The corridor drawn before the fall, giving a free hold rule mid-trade.
  • Targets on the axis before the entry, and the move closed when they were paid.

To fix or answer

  • No position line or filled ticket in any of the four screenshots. If the short was never placed, this session belongs in the log as a missed execution, and the miss has a cause worth naming.
  • The first target is under 1:2 again. Size the idea to the zone behind the line.
  • The X at 3,893.3 was left above the entry and never taken. It worked; that is the exception, and the inducement page explains why it usually will not.
  • Still no higher-timeframe screenshot. Third request: capture the 1H or 4H before the session.

Repeatable steps

Run this the same way every session

  1. Name the trend from the last shift, and mark the ceiling and floor it left.Here: MSS down near 3,904.5, ceiling 3,906.5, floor 3,872.7.
  2. Let the retracement run and count what it sweeps.One swept high is a pullback; two is a loaded setup. Do not trade the retracement itself.
  3. Wait for the close back through the shift level, with the trend.The close below 3,877.13. Before it, the supply does not exist yet.
  4. Draw the zone the shift left, both edges, while price is away from it.3,884.31 to 3,896.86, drawn at 3,877.
  5. Mark the internal liquidity inside the zone and the floor below it.The X at 3,893.3 above the entry; the demand 3,852.48–3,862.87 as the target.
  6. Place the three numbers as one order.Limit at the near edge, stop beyond the whole zone plus buffer, target inside the opposing zone. Require 1:2 to that target or skip.
  7. Hold while price respects the corridor; re-check on a close back over the near edge.Lower highs inside the fall range are the trade working.
  8. Take the target when it is hit on displacement, and close the plan with it.The wick beyond is not yours. The next idea starts from the new structure.

When to skip

The same setup, refused

Do not take it if

  • The retracement swept nothing on its way against the trend.
  • The zone has already been tagged once in the session.
  • The opposing zone pays less than 1:2 and nothing sits behind it.
  • The higher timeframe is buying into the same demand you are targeting.
  • High-impact news for the currency lands inside the trade.

This setup had

  • Two swept highs counted before the trigger.
  • A shift with the trend after both sweeps.
  • A fresh zone, edges labelled to the cent, drawn at the shift.
  • A named demand floor paying about 1:2.4.
  • A corridor that gave a hold rule and was never broken upward.

Keep this in proportion

One replay evening on gold, one short, and no visible ticket. The marking is the best on the site so far: sweeps counted, zone drawn in calm, targets named early. None of that is proof of profit. Keep the log to fifty setups with the losses and the missed executions counted, and add the higher-timeframe screenshot so the context lives on the page too.

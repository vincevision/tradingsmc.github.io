Case study · XAU/USD

Textbook Institutional Flow

How to read this page

On these charts X marks a liquidity sweep: a wick runs a high and price turns back. The slides have no price axis, so levels are described by structure. The reward-to-risk figure is measured from the position boxes: the blue box is a little under four times the height of the grey one. Times are as printed on the charts.

Step 1 · Bias

4H: sweep the high, shift down

This slide sets the direction for everything that follows.

What happened

  1. An old high was left standing. The first peak on the left is the reference high. Buy stops from sellers and breakout orders from buyers collect above it.
  2. Price came back and swept it (X). Days later a candle pushed above that high and could not hold there. The following candles closed back underneath. The buy-side liquidity was taken and there was no continuation.
  3. Structure shifted bearish (MSS). The sell-off that followed closed below the swing low between the two peaks. The 4H is no longer making higher lows.
  4. A pullback began. The last few candles on the slide are a bounce off the low. That bounce is what the lower timeframes zoom into.

Why this matters

  • Sweep, then shift. A break of structure that comes straight after a liquidity grab is more trustworthy than one that comes from nowhere. The high was used to fill sell orders.
  • The bounce is now a selling opportunity. After a bearish 4H MSS, rallies are pullbacks until proven otherwise. The job is to find where the pullback ends.
  • The trade is with the higher timeframe. Everything below is about timing a short in the direction the 4H has already chosen.

Rule to carry forward

Take shorts only after the 4H has swept a high and closed below the last swing low. Then wait for the pullback. Do not sell the breakdown candle itself: it is the most expensive place to enter.

Step 2 · Location and liquidity

1H: the pullback runs an internal high

The 1H shows how far the bounce travels and what it takes on the way.

What happened

  1. The drop left an internal high. Partway down the sell-off, one candle made a small high before price continued lower. The long X line starts from it. Traders who sold the drop tend to put stops above a high like this.
  2. Price climbed back in a slow grind. Small overlapping candles for many hours. Compare that with the speed of the drop: the pullback is corrective, the drop was impulsive.
  3. First sweep (long X). A large bullish candle finally pushed through the internal high. By then price had recovered well over half of the drop, so it was in premium.
  4. Second sweep (short X). Price made one more marginal high above the candle that did the first sweep, with wicks only.
  5. A bearish candle followed. The last candle on the slide is the first decisive move down since the pullback began.

Why this is the location

There is no supply box drawn on this slide. The point of interest is the liquidity itself. The pullback has reached the upper half of the bearish leg and has taken the stops that were resting inside it. Sellers now have both a good price and the orders they need to fill against.

Rule to carry forward

In a bearish pullback, mark the internal highs left inside the drop. The short setup begins when price is above the 50% level of the leg and one of those highs has been swept. Before that, the pullback is still shopping for liquidity.

Common mistake

Selling the first touch of the internal high. Price swept it and then went on to take a second high on the 1H and a third on the 15m. The sweep says “watch”. Only a structure shift says “sell”.

Step 3 · Confirmation

15m: three breaks up, a sweep, then the shift

What happened

  1. The 15m was bullish on the way up. Three BoS labels mark three higher highs. On this timeframe alone, it looks like an uptrend worth buying.
  2. The 1H internal high was taken (long X). The long horizontal line is the same high from slide 2. The third leg up runs straight through it.
  3. A higher low formed, then one more high (short X). Price dipped, then pushed up again and took the high it had just made. This is the second sweep.
  4. MSS. Price then closed below the higher low that sat between the two sweeps. For the first time in the whole rally, a 15m low failed.

Why this matters

Each break of structure on the way up recruits buyers and gives them a place to put stops. By the third one, the lows underneath are loaded with sell stops and late buyers are entering at the highs, exactly where the 4H wants to sell. The MSS is the first evidence that the 15m has stopped disagreeing with the 4H.

Rule to carry forward

Let the lower timeframe trend against you into the higher-timeframe liquidity. The signal is the sequence: the HTF high is swept, then a candle closes below the most recent 15m higher low. Use the low that produced the final high, not a minor one inside the last leg.

Step 4 · Refinement and order placement

Entry: order block under a protected high

This slide is frozen shortly after the shift. Everything needed to place the order is on it.

What is marked

  • A third X and the protected high. Just before the drop, one wick took out a small high. That wick is the last high made before the displacement, so it becomes the protected high. If price trades above it, the bearish shift has failed.
  • OB. The last bullish candles before the displacement candle. This is where the selling that broke structure began.
  • IMB. The displacement candle left an imbalance directly under the order block. A pullback that fills it arrives at the bottom of the OB.
  • MSS. The broken higher low, a little below the OB.
  • EQL. Equal lows left during the rally. Two lows at the same price mean a thick layer of sell stops underneath. It sits between entry and target and helps pull price down.
  • Demand. The zone at the base of the 15m rally. It is the first place buyers are likely to respond, so the target is its top edge.

Why the entry is here

The order block is the origin of the move that broke structure, it sits in premium on every timeframe shown, and the imbalance beneath it gives price a reason to come back. Entry is a sell limit at the bottom edge of the OB, the first price a pullback reaches after filling the imbalance.

The stop on the chart sits just above the top of the order block, a little under the protected high. That keeps the risk small. Measured against it, the distance down to demand is about 3.8 times the risk.

Rule to carry forward

After a bearish MSS, mark the last bullish candle before the displacement and the high above it. Sell the bottom edge of that order block only while the protected high is intact and only if an imbalance separates the OB from current price.

Step 5 · Management and outcome

Result

What happened

  1. Price went sideways first. For roughly two hours after the shift it ranged underneath the order block without reaching it.
  2. One push filled the order. Around 06:00 on the chart a candle ran up through the imbalance and touched the bottom of the OB. The next candle was strongly bearish.
  3. The protected high was never tested. Price did not travel past the lower part of the order block.
  4. Sell-off through the equal lows. Price fell in a steady staircase, cleared the EQL, and kept going.
  5. Target reached. The bottom of the blue box is the top edge of demand. Price hit it and wicked slightly inside.

What to take from it

  • The fill was shallow again. As in the USD/JPY trade, price only just reached the near edge of the order block. A sell limit in the middle of the OB would have been left behind.
  • The range before the fill was a test of patience. Nothing in those two hours changed the plan: the protected high was intact and the order was resting.
  • Equal lows were fuel, not the exit. The target was the demand zone beyond them. The EQL is a sensible place for a partial if you take one.

Rule to carry forward

While waiting for a fill, the only question is whether the protected high has been broken. If it has, cancel. If it has not, leave the order alone.

Everything as one

The whole trade in a single picture

A schematic of the 15m sequence, not a copy of the chart. The 4H has already swept its high and shifted bearish before this picture starts.

1 · SWEEP THE HTF HIGH

The 15m rallies out of demand with three breaks of structure and runs the 1H internal high. Stops above the pullback are taken in premium.

2 · SWEEP AGAIN

A higher low forms, then price takes the high it just made. A last small sweep sets the protected high.

3 · SHIFT

A displacement candle closes below the higher low. It leaves an imbalance under the order block.

4 · ENTRY

After a two-hour range, price fills the imbalance and touches the bottom of the order block, where the sell limit is resting.

5 · TARGET

Price sells off through the equal lows to the top of the demand zone that started the rally. About 3.8R.

THE LOGIC IN ONE SENTENCE

Sell the first pullback after the 15m flips bearish at swept HTF highs, risk the order block, and take profit at the first demand below.

Order placement

Entry, stop loss and take profit

Entry

Sell limit at the bottom edge of the 15m order block.

  • Place it when the MSS candle closes.
  • The bottom edge is where the imbalance ends, so it is the first price a pullback reaches.
  • Price only touched that edge here. A deeper order would not have filled.
  • Sells fill on the bid, which is the charted price. No spread adjustment is needed on the entry.

Stop loss

Just above the top of the order block, plus spread.

  • This is where the grey box ends on the chart: above the OB, slightly below the protected high.
  • Reason: if price trades through the whole order block, the sellers there have been absorbed.
  • A short is closed by a buy stop, which triggers on the ask. Gold spreads can be wide, so add the spread to the stop.
  • More conservative: above the protected high. That is the true structural invalidation. On this chart it would have cut the trade to roughly 1:3.

Take profit

Top edge of the demand zone. About 3.8R.

  • It is the first opposing zone: the base of the rally that carried price up into the highs.
  • The equal lows sit above it, so price has liquidity to reach for on the way down.
  • The target is the near edge. Buyers may respond anywhere inside the zone.
  • A short's take profit fills on the ask. Set it a little above the zone edge, by the spread plus a small margin.

Managing the position

The post shows a single entry and a single exit. The points below are my suggested handling, not something marked on the slides.

  • Leave the stop alone until price closes below the post-MSS low and prints a new 15m lower high. Then it can trail to just above that lower high.
  • Optional partial at the equal lows. Decide before entry.
  • Cancel the limit if the protected high breaks, or if price covers about half the distance to demand without filling you.

Position size on gold

Fix the money at risk first, then let the stop distance decide the lot size.

Lots = money at risk ÷ (stop in dollars × 100)

One standard lot of XAU/USD is normally 100 ounces, so a $1 move in gold is $100 per lot. Check your broker's contract size, because some differ.

Example: $10,000 account, 1% risk = $100. A stop of $5.00 gives 100 ÷ (5 × 100) = 0.20 lots. At 1:3.8 the target pays about $380.

Repeatable strategy

Step-by-step for the next trade

Written for shorts, as in this trade. For longs, mirror it: lows for highs, discount for premium, supply for demand. A “no” at any step ends the process.

  1. Has the 4H swept a high and closed below the last swing low?Both are needed. This gives the bearish bias. Mark the leg from the swept high to the new low and its 50% level.
  2. On the 1H, mark the internal highs inside the drop.These are the liquidity the pullback is likely to reach for. Set an alert at the nearest one above the 50% level.
  3. Has the pullback swept an internal high in premium?It should arrive as a corrective grind, slower than the drop. No sweep, no setup.
  4. Drop to the 15m and mark the most recent higher low.The rally will show several breaks of structure up. Keep moving the line to the low that produced the latest high.
  5. Did a candle close below that higher low with displacement?This is the MSS. It should leave an imbalance. A wick below the low does not count.
  6. Mark the order block and the protected high.The OB is the last bullish candle before the displacement. The protected high is the highest point just before the drop. The OB must be untouched since the shift.
  7. Does it pay at least 1:2 to the first demand below?Measure entry at the OB's bottom edge, stop above the OB (or above the protected high), target at the top edge of demand.
  8. Place the orders.Sell limit, stop and target together. Size for a fixed 0.5–1% of the account. Add the spread to the stop.
  9. Manage by structure, then record it.Cancel if the protected high breaks before the fill. Once in, hold to target, stop, or a new lower high you can trail above. Screenshot the 4H, 1H and 15m.

Filters

When this setup should be skipped

Do not take it if

  • The 4H has not shifted. A sweep of a high without a close below the swing low is not a bias.
  • The pullback is still in the lower half of the 4H drop.
  • The 1H pullback closes above the high the 4H swept. The bearish idea is over.
  • The 15m “shift” breaks only a minor low, or leaves no imbalance.
  • The protected high is taken before the order fills.
  • Demand is so close that the trade pays less than 1:2.
  • High-impact USD news is due before the pullback is likely to fill. Gold spreads widen sharply around it.

This trade had all of these

  • 4H sweep of a high followed by a bearish MSS.
  • A corrective 1H pullback into the upper half of the drop.
  • The 1H internal high swept, then two more highs.
  • 15m MSS on a displacement candle with an imbalance.
  • An untouched order block under an intact protected high.
  • Equal lows and an unmitigated demand zone below, about 3.8R away.

Keep this in proportion

The tight stop above the order block is what produces 1:3.8, and it is also the easiest part to get wrong. Gold often wicks through an order block toward the protected high before turning. Test both stop placements in your own backtest and compare the results across at least 50 trades before choosing one.

How to read this page

The slides have no price axis, so levels are described by structure (zone edges, swing highs and lows) and not by price. The reward-to-risk figure is measured from the position boxes on the chart: the blue box is roughly three times the height of the grey one. Times are as printed on the charts; the slides do not state a timezone.

Step 1 · Location and bias

HTF zone tapped

This slide answers the first question of any trade: which direction, and from where?

What happened

  1. Sell-side liquidity was swept. At the bottom left, a horizontal line marks an earlier low. A long wick drives below it and price closes back above. Stops under that low were taken and nobody followed through to the downside.
  2. Structure shifted bullish (MSS). Price then closed above the swing high that had produced the low. On the 4H that turns the bias from bearish to bullish.
  3. A demand zone was left behind. The 4H demand box is drawn around the last consolidation before the candle that broke structure. That is where the buying that changed the trend originated.
  4. Price rallied, then came back. After several days of higher highs, price retraced the whole way down and wicked into the zone for the first time. The final candle on the slide is a strong bullish reaction out of it.

Why this zone is worth trading

  • It did something. It caused the MSS. Zones that break structure carry more weight than zones that merely paused price.
  • It is fresh. This is the first return. A zone that has already been tapped has had its orders used.
  • It is in discount. The zone sits in the lower half of the leg from the swept low to the high. Longs are bought cheap, not chased.
  • It was born from a sweep. Liquidity grab, then shift, then zone. That order of events is what gives the zone its quality.

Rule to carry forward

Only look for longs when price is inside a fresh HTF demand zone that caused a break of structure and sits in discount. Until price gets there, there is nothing to do. The 4H tap is a reason to watch, not a reason to buy.

Step 2 · Confirmation

30m market shift

Dropping to the 30m shows how price arrived at the 4H zone and whether buyers actually showed up. This slide is the “confirmation” in the title.

What happened

  1. The delivery was bearish. From the 30m supply at the top, price made a clean sequence of lower highs and lower lows. Inside the HTF pullback, the 30m trend was down.
  2. 30m supply is marked at the origin. It is the base of the sell-off. A dotted line runs from a later high that already wicked up toward it, so buy-side liquidity is resting right at the lower edge of that zone. This becomes the target.
  3. The last low was broken into demand (BoS). The dashed BoS line marks the final 30m low. Price pushes under it and lands inside the 4H demand. On the 30m this looks like bearish continuation. In context, it is a run on sell-side liquidity into a HTF buy zone.
  4. The break failed to continue. There was no follow-through below the BoS. Price reversed from inside the zone.
  5. MSS. A strong bullish leg closed above the last lower high, the high that had produced the final low. The 30m sequence of lower highs is over.

Why this matters

A HTF zone can fail. Buying the first touch means paying for a wide stop below the whole 4H zone and accepting that price may slice straight through. Waiting for the 30m shift costs a few pips of entry price and buys proof that the 4H demand is being defended. It also produces a much tighter stop, which is where the 1:3 comes from.

Rule to carry forward

Inside the HTF zone, wait for three things in this order: a low is taken, price fails to continue lower, and a candle closes above the last lower high with displacement. A wick through the high does not count.

Common mistake

Calling an MSS on a break of a minor internal high. The high that matters is the one that led to the lowest low. On this slide the MSS line starts from that swing, not from the small bounces underneath it.

Step 3 · Refinement and order placement

15m entry

The 30m gave permission. The 15m gives the price. This slide is frozen just after the shift, before the pullback, which is when the limit order is placed.

What is marked

  • $$$ on the descending line. Each lower high on the way down sits on a trendline. Breakout traders and trailing stops cluster above lines like this, so it is buy-side liquidity and part of what draws price upward.
  • BoS into 4H demand. The same sweep as slide 2, seen in more detail.
  • X = liquidity sweep. After the first bounce out of the 4H demand, price dipped once more and a wick ran the small lows left by that bounce, then closed back up. X marks that sweep. It cleared the last sell-side liquidity sitting under price, and the displacement candle followed straight after. The solid line drawn from the sweep's low runs to the bottom of the grey risk box, so that low is also the trade's invalidation.
  • MSS. One large bullish candle closes through the lower high. That is displacement: speed and range, not a slow grind.
  • IMB (imbalance). The displacement candle moved so fast that it left a gap in price delivery just above the order block. Price tends to return to rebalance these gaps, which is why a pullback is expected at all.
  • 15m OB. The last bearish candle area before the displacement. It sits directly under the imbalance, so a pullback that fills the IMB arrives at the OB.

Why the entry is here and not elsewhere

The order block is the origin of the move that broke structure. It is inside the 4H demand, below the midpoint of the new bullish leg, and it has an imbalance above it to pull price back. Entry is a buy limit at the top edge of the OB, which is the first price the pullback reaches after filling the imbalance.

The opposing 30m supply is far enough away to pay about three times the risk. If supply had been sitting just above the MSS high, the same pattern would not have been worth taking.

Rule to carry forward

After the MSS, mark the last opposing candle before the displacement. Take it only if it has an imbalance next to it and has not been touched since. Place the limit order immediately; do not chase the breakout candle.

Step 4 · Management and outcome

Result

What happened

  1. Price paused at the MSS high. A few small candles drifted sideways and down. No new low was made.
  2. The imbalance was filled. Price traded back through the IMB.
  3. The OB was tapped by a wick. One candle dipped into the top of the 15m order block and closed back above it. The limit order filled there. Price never came close to the X sweep low.
  4. Expansion. Price rallied in two legs with a shallow pullback between them, taking the highs along the trendline on the way.
  5. Target reached. The top of the blue box is the lower edge of the 30m supply. Price hit it and pushed slightly into the zone.

What to take from it

  • The fill was shallow. Only a wick entered the OB. An order at the middle or bottom of the OB may not have filled. Entering at the top edge costs a little reward-to-risk and gets you into more trades.
  • The pullback took time. There were roughly two to three hours on the chart between the shift and the fill. The setup rewards patience with a resting order, not screen-watching.
  • The target was the zone edge. Taking profit at the first touch of supply meant the trade was closed before any reaction from sellers. What price did afterwards is not shown and is not the trade's concern.

Rule to carry forward

Once filled, do nothing until price either reaches the target or makes a new structural high. Moving the stop early on a 15m wobble is the usual way a 1:3 winner turns into a breakeven.

Everything as one

The whole trade in a single picture

A schematic of the sequence, not a copy of the chart. Read it left to right.

1 · SWEEP INTO THE ZONE

The 30m downtrend breaks its last low and lands in the fresh 4H demand. Sell stops are taken where higher-timeframe buyers are waiting.

2 · SHIFT

A final liquidity sweep (X) clears the lows under price, and a displacement candle closes above the last lower high. The 30m has turned to agree with the 4H.

3 · ENTRY

The displacement leaves an imbalance above a 15m order block. Price returns, fills the imbalance and taps the top of the OB, where the buy limit is resting.

4 · TARGET

Price expands through the trendline highs to the lower edge of the 30m supply that started the sell-off. The trade closes there at about 3R.

The logic in one sentence

Buy the first pullback after lower-timeframe structure flips inside a higher-timeframe demand zone, risk the low that made the flip, and take profit at the nearest opposing zone.

Order placement

Entry, stop loss and take profit

Entry

Buy limit at the top edge of the 15m order block.

  • Place it as soon as the MSS candle closes.
  • The top edge is where the imbalance ends, so it is the first price a pullback reaches.
  • In this trade only a wick reached the OB. A deeper order risked no fill.
  • Add the spread to a buy limit. Buys fill on the ask, which is above the charted price.

Stop loss

Below the low of the X liquidity sweep, plus a small buffer.

  • The X sweep is the low that launched the MSS. The liquidity there has already been taken, so price has no reason to return. If it does break, the bullish shift is void and the reason for the trade is gone.
  • This is lower than the bottom of the OB. A stop tucked just under the OB is exposed to a slightly deeper wick.
  • Buffer: a few pips below the wick. Sell stops trigger on the bid, so the spread does not need to be added on a long.
  • More conservative: below the sweep low inside the 4H zone. Safer, and it lowers the reward-to-risk.

Take profit

Lower edge of the 30m supply. About 3R.

  • It is the first opposing zone, and the origin of the sell-off. Sellers may be waiting there.
  • The trendline highs ($$$) and the earlier high near the zone edge are liquidity that pulls price up to it.
  • The target is the near edge, not inside the zone. Do not ask price to push through sellers to pay you.
  • Set the order a touch below the edge. Price often turns a few pips short of a zone.

Managing the position

The post shows a single entry and a single exit. The points below are my suggested handling, not something marked on the slides.

  • Leave the stop alone until price closes above the MSS high and prints a new 15m higher low. Then it can trail to just under that higher low.
  • Optional partial at the MSS high or at the trendline liquidity if you want to pay yourself early. Decide this before entry, not during.
  • Cancel the limit if price reaches about half of the way to target without filling you. The reward that justified the trade is no longer there.

Position size

Fix the money at risk first, then let the stop distance decide the lot size.

Lots = money at risk ÷ (stop in pips × pip value per lot)

On USD/JPY one standard lot moves ¥1,000 per pip. In dollars that is 1,000 ÷ the USD/JPY rate, for example about $6.67 at 150.00.

Example: $10,000 account, 1% risk = $100. Stop of 15 pips at $6.67 per pip per lot gives 100 ÷ (15 × 6.67) ≈ 1.0 lot. At 1:3 the target pays about $300.

Repeatable strategy

Step-by-step for the next trade

Work down the list in order. Each step is a yes or no. A “no” ends the process for that pair. For shorts, mirror everything: supply for demand, highs for lows.

  1. Mark the 4H structure and its zones.Find the last break of structure. Mark the demand zone that caused it. Check it is untouched and in the lower half of the leg.
  2. Wait for price to enter the zone.Set an alert at the top edge. No alert, no analysis.
  3. Drop to the 30m and map the way in.Mark the lower highs and lower lows of the pullback, the last low, and the supply zone at its origin. That supply is your likely target.
  4. Did price take a low inside the zone?You want the last 30m low run while price is in HTF demand. No sweep means the liquidity is still below you.
  5. Did a candle close above the last lower high with displacement?This is the MSS. It must be a close, from a large-bodied candle, through the high that produced the lowest low.
  6. On the 15m, is there an order block with an imbalance beside it?Mark the last bearish candle before the displacement. It should be unmitigated and sit inside the HTF zone.
  7. Does the trade pay at least 1:2 to the first opposing zone?Measure entry at the OB top, stop under the sweep low that launched the MSS, target at the near edge of supply. Under 1:2, skip it.
  8. Place the orders.Buy limit, stop loss and take profit together. Size the position for a fixed risk of 0.5–1% of the account.
  9. Manage by structure, then record it.Hold until target, stop, or a new structural high lets you trail. Screenshot the 4H, 30m and 15m and log the result, win or lose.

Filters

When this setup should be skipped

Do not take it if

  • The 4H zone has already been tapped and reacted once.
  • Price closes a 4H candle below the bottom of the demand zone.
  • The “shift” is a wick through the high, or a slow overlapping crawl with no imbalance.
  • The opposing supply is so close that the trade pays less than 1:2.
  • Price runs to target without pulling back. The trade is missed, not chased.
  • High-impact news for USD or JPY is due before the pullback is likely to fill.

This trade had all of these

  • Fresh 4H demand that caused a 4H MSS, in discount.
  • Sell-side liquidity taken on the way into the zone.
  • 30m MSS on a single displacement candle.
  • Imbalance sitting directly on a 15m order block.
  • A second liquidity sweep (X) just before the shift, giving a clear low for the stop.
  • Unmitigated 30m supply about 3R away, with trendline liquidity along the path.

Keep this in proportion

This is one trade, chosen for a post because it worked. The same pattern also loses: the zone fails, the order block is run, or price never pulls back. What makes it a strategy is taking every valid occurrence with the same risk and counting the results. Collect at least 50 examples in backtesting, losers included, before trusting the numbers.

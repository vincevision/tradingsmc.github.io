How to read this page

Four slides, one idea per rung: structure on the H4, liquidity on the M30, confirmation on the M10, delivery at the pool. The deck’s own labels are kept — X for a swept high, MSS for a shift, BoS for a break of structure, Eql for equal lows, Sh for the shift line, OB for the order block, FVG for the fair value gap — and each slide is read against the ten-step checklist, which is where the deck’s one omission (no stop loss drawn) gets fixed.

Rung 1 · Bias

H4: the structure that owns the bias

Nothing on the lower rungs is allowed to contradict this slide. It sets the only direction the trade may take.

Read it in the house order. The rally into the 13th prints a high, and the wick above the small X takes whatever rested over it — the fuel sweep. What follows is not a pullback: the MSS bracket marks the shift, the close beyond the last higher low that turns owners into sellers. The BoS bracket at the 17th–20th lows is continuation — structure broken in the same direction, twice confirmed.

Then the detail most readers skip: the rally of the 19th–20th. It climbs to a second X on the 22nd and the 24th wicks back into it. That is not a reversal; that is liquidity being built — equal-ish highs at premium in a market whose H4 structure is down. Stops rest above that X, and a bearish H4 with buy-side above it has told you exactly where the next raid goes: up into supply, take the X-adjacent stops, then continue down. The M30 slide is that raid in close-up.

Rung 2 · Liquidity

M30: naming the liquidity

One rung down, the question changes from “which way” to “whose money, and where”.

The staircase from the left is the H4 rally in miniature, and its top is the raid: the spike around 09:00–12:00 wicks above the prior high at the X — the M30 taking the buy-side the H4 slide built — and closes back down. A raid, not a rally: same signature as the H4’s own fuel sweep, one rung lower.

Below, the Eql line names the other pool: two equal lows from the earlier session, sell-side resting beneath them, untouched. In this method a target is never a guess — it is a pool with a line under it, and here the line is drawn before the entry exists.

Between them sits the quiet hero of the slide: the dashed Sh line at the last lower high of the weak bounce. It is the threshold, not a trade: until something closes beyond it, the bounce is still a bounce. The M10 slide exists to break it — and to prove the break with displacement.

Rung 3 · Confirmation

M10: the tap, the MSS, the FVG

The entry rung does not predict. It waits for three stamps on one passport: the tap, the shift, the gap.

The tap. From the 15:00 lows the M10 rallies into the 18:00–19:00 window and wicks up into the OB — the order block sitting at the supply’s right edge, the origin candles of the earlier decline. One tap, at premium, into a zone the H4 bias already owns. Note what the tap also is: a sweep of the rally’s own highs, taken at the zone — the heavier door of the memorised rule.

The shift. The MSS bracket under the rally’s last higher low (17:00–18:00) is the line the bounce owned. The first strong bearish candle after the tap closes beneath it — the M10 answering the M30’s Sh line from one rung below.

The gap. That same displacing candle leaves the FVG the arrow points at: wicks that do not overlap, an imbalance in the fall. In this house the FVG is not an entry — it is the receipt: proof the break was bought by size, not drifted into. Tap, MSS, FVG: three stamps, and only now does the column become a ticket.

Rung 4 · Delivery

Delivery to the pool

A target is a promise the pools make. This slide is the promise kept.

From the tap, the displacement does not drift — it travels: candle after candle inside the column, the FVG never needing to be revisited, until the last bearish candle tags the Eql line around 20:30–21:00. The equal lows that the M30 slide named before any entry existed are exactly where the move died, because that is where the sell-side rested and where the defenders of the next bounce were waiting.

Read the geometry once more, top to bottom: supply and OB untouched above (the risk side never worked), column travelled in full, pool tagged below. That is what “the market moves from pool to pool” looks like when every rung agreed before the first candle of the fall printed.

The house rule, supplied

The stop the deck forgot

The carousel draws entry and target and no stop. On this site a ticket with two prices is not a ticket.

Where the stop goes, and why

Above the supply box’s top edge — the invalidation line. A body close above the supply means the zone that owns this trade has failed and the H4 raid story is wrong; nothing narrower protects the idea, and nothing wider (above the M30 X, above the H4 wick) prices the trade out of its own edge. The deck’s column measures reward; the stop above the supply measures the only risk that matters — being wrong about the zone, not being early.

And the ratio test still applies before the tap, not after: from the OB’s edge to the Eql line must clear 1:2 against that stop, measured on the M10 while price is still below the entry. On this slide it plainly does — the column is several times the height of the boxes above it — which is why the deck’s omission costs the reader a habit, not this particular trade.

Do it again

Repeatable steps

  1. On the H4, name the bias the hard way: sweep, then MSS, then BoS — all three, or the rung is silent.
  2. Mark the pool the rally built against that bias (the right-hand X): that is where the raid will go.
  3. One rung down, name both pools: the buy-side to be swept above, the sell-side to be paid below (Eql). Draw the target now.
  4. Draw the shift line (Sh): the last opposite point the bounce owns. It is a threshold, not a trade.
  5. On the entry rung, wait for the tap of the OB at the zone’s edge — one tap, at premium or discount, never chased.
  6. Demand the two stamps after the tap: MSS through the shift line, and an FVG as the receipt of displacement.
  7. Write the ticket with its stop above the zone’s invalidation line, check 1:2 before the tap, then let the column run to the pool.

Discipline

When this trade does not exist

  • The H4 rung disagrees. No MSS or no BoS after the H4 sweep means no bias to inherit; an M10 tap without an H4 wind is a coin flip in a nice costume.
  • The raid never happens. If the M30 never sweeps the right-hand X, the move down has no fuel taken and the supply stays untested for a reason.
  • The tap closes through. A body close above the supply cancels the zone and the ticket; the tap must be a wick’s visit, not a tenant moving in.
  • MSS without the gap. A drift through the shift line with overlapping wicks is not displacement; the FVG is the receipt, and no receipt means no stamp.
  • The pool is too close. If the Eql line sits under 1:2 from the OB against the invalidation stop, the geometry fails the gate even when the story is perfect.

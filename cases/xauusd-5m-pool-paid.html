Step 1 · Structure and origin

The fall that leaves the map

Ninety minutes of tape write down everything the trade will need: fuel, proof, an address, and a reason.

The session’s top prints at 03:40, ≈ 3,540.5. The 05:00 push takes the 04:10 internal high at the X (≈ 3,537) — buy-side fuel — and pays for it with a lower high: ≈ 3,538–3,539 against 3,540.5. A sweep that fails to make a new high is a sweep spent; the roll begins. It proves itself in two closes: below ≈ 3,523.5, the pullback low (MSS), then below ≈ 3,512.4, the 02:00 low (BoS) — displacement both times, no negotiation.

The fall ends where falls end: at a wick, ≈ 3,484.5 at 07:00, and the red sss line drawn through it names the sell-side pool the next leg down must raid. Behind the fall, its origin candles become the grey supply, ≈ 3,525.5–3,530.5, extended right. And then the bounce — small, quick, honest — climbs off the low and puts its 07:30 wick inside the box’s lower half. Frame 1 freezes on that wick: the zone asked its question.

Step 2 · The return and the trigger

The tap, the fill, the shift

The confirmation model in its tightest clothing: one wick into the box, one limit at the refined floor, one close to seal it.

The 07:30 wick to ≈ 3,529.5–3,530 is the tap — into the box’s lower half, at the zone, the heavy door of the memorised rule. The refinement keeps the tap candle and the fall’s origin and lifts the ceiling: 3,525.54–3,531.15, the top above the tap wick and above the grey box’s own roof, so that every definition of “the supply failed” lives above the stop. The limit rests at the floor, 3,525.54, and the bounce’s second push — the one that makes its lower high — fills it at 07:50–08:00.

Then the answer, within three candles: a body close below ≈ 3,521.5, the bounce’s last higher low. Fill and shift shake hands in the same quarter-hour — the entry at the tapped edge, the confirmation arriving with the trade already on, exactly the ordering the house model allows when the shift came first and left the zone behind. Below it all, the ticket had been measured since 07:00: risk 5.61 handles, reward 40.51 to the pool line, ≈ 1:7.2 — the ratio a tight refined box and a far pool can pay.

Memorise

A liquidity sweep followed by a shift can be enough — but if that liquidity sweep came from a demand or supply zone, the shift that follows adds more probability. The X sweep at ≈ 3,537 started the fall; the tap at the box’s floor and the close below ≈ 3,521.5 are this trade’s own sweep-and-shift pair, printed at the zone.

Step 3 · Delivery

Forty handles to the pool

From the refined floor to the pool line in forty minutes of one-directional tape — the raid the sss line promised at 07:00, collected on schedule.

After the shift close, the fall never prints a candle back inside the refined box: 3,520, 3,515, 3,510 — displacement with a dashed trendline drawn along it, the owner’s pen keeping the fall company. At ≈ 08:35–08:45 it reaches the sss line and the blue tag at 3,485.03: the pool raided, the target paid. The wick keeps going to 3,479.47 at the capture — overshoot, the sweep’s receipt, not extra reward: the ticket booked at the line and let the raid finish loud.

The position still flags Open on the tape at the capture, which is the quiet point of the frame: with the stop above 3,531.15 and the target tagged on the way through, there was nothing to manage, nothing to move, nothing to watch except the pen. Five and a half handles of risk, forty and a half of reward, one wick into the box to start it.

Do it again

Repeatable steps

  1. When a sweep prints a lower high against the prior swing, treat the fuel as spent: the roll that follows is the context, not the trade.
  2. Draw the box at the displacement’s origin and the pool line at the fall’s wick — both on the same pass of the pen, before any bounce exists.
  3. Let the bounce tap the box before the ticket exists; refine to the tap candle and lift the ceiling above it, so the stop sits above every definition of failure.
  4. Rest the limit at the refined floor, not at the wick’s high: the zone’s edge is the address, the wick is only the knock.
  5. Ask for the shift inside the hour of the tap — a body close beyond the bounce’s last higher low. Fill without it is a prediction; fill with it is the model.
  6. Book at the pool line, drawn hours earlier: the raid is the event that pays you, and a far pool under a tight box is where 1:7 lives.
  7. After the fill, one message matters — the stop. Trendlines drawn along the fall are company, not management.

Discipline

When this trade does not exist

  • The bounce closes through. Any 5m close above 3,531.15 before the fill occupies the supply; the box becomes history and the limit stays out of it.
  • Tap without shift. A wick into the box with the bounce’s higher lows intact is a raid on the box, not a trigger from it — wait for bodies below ≈ 3,521.5 or their equivalent.
  • The pool pre-raided. If an earlier leg already swept the sss line and bounced hard, the 3,485.03 book sits on occupied ground; take a nearer book at proper ratio or stand aside.
  • No fuel above. A fall whose top never swept anything (no X, no lower high against a swept swing) leaves the box without a reason; the bounce may keep climbing through it.
  • Ratio under 1:2. From refined floor to chosen book, measured before the tap. A 5.6-handle stop is permission only because the pool sat 40 handles away — never the other way round.

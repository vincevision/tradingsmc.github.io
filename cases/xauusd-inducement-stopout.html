Case study · XAU/USD · 5m · a losing trade, kept on purpose

Stopped by the inducement above

Every other case on this site ends at the target. This one ends at the stop, and it stays on the site for exactly that reason. A short was entered at a 5m supply band while an inducement line still rested above it — unswept buy-side liquidity sitting between the entry and the zone above. The rally did what liquidity always does: it took the inducement first, ran the stop on the way, and only then dropped to 3,6970 without the trade. Direction right, timing wrong, tuition paid. The lesson now lives in note 12 and on the pinned note every case page carries: if there is inducement, always wait for confirmation.

Why a losing trade gets a case page

Winners show the sequence; losers show which gate matters most. This frame is the mirror image of the inducement case that paid: same market habit, same lines, opposite order of operations. Read them together and the rule stops being a slogan — it becomes the difference between the two tickets. The companion write-up also lives in the losing-trades section of Why this trade failed.

The board was not the problem. From above 3,7740 the 5m falls through a proper staircase — supply at ≈ 3,7660–3,7700, then the wide band at ≈ 3,7580–3,7620, then the band at ≈ 3,7480–3,7520 — with the protected high named at ≈ 3,7745 and each leg breaking structure down (bos, MSS marks through the morning). The first inducement, at ≈ 3,7620, even got swept on the way (X at ≈ 3,7600): the gate was paid there, and the fall continued exactly as the model says.

Then the low near 3,7200 around 15:00, and the pullback begins. The short goes in at the third band, ≈ 3,7480–3,7520 — a real zone, in a real downtrend, with the stop above it. Everything except one line: that band's own INDUCEMENT at ≈ 3,7520, the buy-side resting just above the zone, had not been swept. The order was placed with the toll booth still in front of it, and the stop parked inside the toll.

What the inducement did

The toll, collected at the stop

From 18:00 the rally works higher in the only way pullbacks work: it takes what is resting. First the tap of the third band (the red box at ≈ 3,7480–3,7540), which is already through the entry; then the sweep of the 3,7520 inducement; then continuation into the second band and on to ≈ 3,7640 (the red box at ≈ 3,7580–3,7640). A stop above the entry band — anywhere near 3,7540–3,7560 — is gone by 20:00. The loss is booked.

And then, at 21:30, with the inducement paid and the second band tapped, price collapses: 3,7400, 3,7200, 3,7000, a printed low near 3,6970 — the move the short was meant to ride, starting roughly ninety minutes after the stop was hit. This is the part that stings and teaches at once: the read was never wrong. The order of operations was. Liquidity in front of a zone does not care that the zone is right; it gets taken first, and stops resting in its path are part of the payment.

The rule, in the owner's words

"If there is inducement, always wait for confirmation." Confirmation here is not a feeling: it is the sweep of that very line plus the shift away from it — the two marks that turned the same board into a paying trade an hour later, one band higher.

The counterfactual

The version that waits

Hold the same board and change only the patience. The rally sweeps 3,7520, then sweeps on into the second band's own inducement at ≈ 3,7620 and tags ≈ 3,7640 around 20:00–21:00. That is the confirmation: two inducements paid, the second band tapped from below, and the shift down printing after 21:00 (the MSS legs of the collapse). A short from the second band — sell limit at ≈ 3,7580–3,7600, stop above ≈ 3,7640 — enters eighty points higher than the stopped one, with the toll already collected in front of it.

What does that ticket make of the collapse to 3,6970? The old low near 3,7200 first (≈ 1:8 against a ≈ 50-point stop), then the print through (≈ 1:13). The market offered both trades on the same frame; the gate decided which one got paid. This is precisely the sequence the paying case study walked through — sweep of the inducement, tap of the band, delivery to the pool — and precisely what note 12 now says in one line: new sweep plus protected line intact equals entry; anything earlier is donation.

The frame's open ticket at the far right (a short from the 19th's recovery, ≈ 3,737.5) is the discipline re-applied after the lesson: a new leg, a new sweep, a new zone — not revenge on the old one.

  • The stopped ticket's risk was not the problem — ≈ 50–60 points is honest for a 5m band. The placement of the stop inside unswept liquidity was.
  • Waiting did not cost the trade; it moved the entry 80 points better and put the stop behind paid liquidity instead of in front of unpaid liquidity.
  • Both tickets are logged. The loss is a tuition line with a receipt: the rule it bought is now written into the routine.

Review

What the loss proves

Done right, even in a loss

  • Direction from structure, not hope: the staircase and protected high were marked before the entry.
  • A named zone and a named stop — the loss was sized, not blown.
  • No re-entry in anger during the rally; the next ticket waited for a new leg and a new sweep.
  • The loss was written up, not buried: frame, levels and rule kept together.

Done wrong, precisely once

  • Entered at a zone with its inducement still resting above — liquidity in front of the entry, stop inside it.
  • No confirmation on the entry chart: neither the sweep of 3,7520 nor a shift away from the band had printed when the order went in.
  • The pullback's first job — taking that inducement — was treated as unlikely instead of expected.

Keep this in proportion

One stopped trade on one frame; the counterfactual ticket is a reading of what the same board offered, not a booked result. Waiting for confirmation removes some winners too — pullbacks sometimes reverse without sweeping anything, and those moves leave without you. The gate is paid for by the stops it prevents, and this frame is the invoice.

Repeatable steps

The inducement gate, before every zone entry

  1. Mark the zone and its protected line as usual.Notes 3–4: the line that cancels the idea outranks everything below it.
  2. Draw the inducement: the nearest liquidity resting between price and your zone.Above a supply you intend to sell; below a demand you intend to buy. Here: ≈ 3,7520 over the third band.
  3. If it is unswept, do nothing at the zone.No limit, no market order. The pullback's first target is that line, and your stop sits in its path.
  4. Wait for the sweep and the shift on your entry chart.The line taken, then structure away from the zone (bos/MSS with displacement). That pair is "confirmation" — nothing less.
  5. Enter on the zone the confirmed move leaves you.Often one band higher (or lower) than the zone you first wanted — as here, the second band at ≈ 3,7580–3,7600 instead of the third.
  6. Stop behind the paid liquidity, target the next pool.Above the swept high for shorts; below the swept low for longs. Re-measure the R from the new entry before the order goes in.
  7. If stopped anyway, write the frame up the same night.Which gate was skipped? This page exists because that question got answered honestly once.

When to skip

What the gate does not forgive

  • Unswept inducement in front of the zone: skip until it is taken — the entire lesson of this page.
  • A sweep of the inducement with no shift after it: the toll was paid but the move failed; the zone is now suspect, not confirmed.
  • A stop that must sit inside resting liquidity to keep the R: if the only affordable stop is in the toll, the trade is too big or the zone too close — skip.
  • Entering because the drop "already started": the collapse here began after the stop-out; chasing it would have been a second tuition fee.
  • Re-entering the same zone after being stopped by its inducement: the zone failed its gate once; demand a fresh sweep and a fresh zone.

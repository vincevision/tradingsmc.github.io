Concept

Internal and external structure, and their sweeps

External structure is the main swings that set the trend. Internal structure is the smaller swings inside one of those legs. Each has its own liquidity and its own sweeps. Knowing which one you are looking at tells you whether a move is the trend changing, or only a pullback collecting orders on its way to your zone.

Part 1

Two structures on one chart

Filled dots are external swing points. Open dots are internal swing points.

External structure

  • The main swing highs and lows on the timeframe you are reading.
  • It decides the trend: higher highs and higher lows, or lower lows and lower highs.
  • A candle close beyond an external point is a real break of structure (BoS), or a change of direction if it is against the trend.
  • The protected low or high is always an external point.

Internal structure

  • The smaller highs and lows inside one external leg, mostly inside the pullback.
  • It shows how the pullback is developing and when it is ending.
  • A close beyond an internal point is an internal break. It is an early sign, not a trend change.
  • Internal highs and lows are where inducement forms.
Part 2

How to mark them the same way every time

The labels need one fixed rule, or you will redraw them to suit the trade you want.

  1. Start from the last break of structure.Find the last candle close above an external high (uptrend).
  2. Confirm the external low.The lowest point of the pullback before that break becomes the external higher low. A low is only confirmed as external once the high after it has been broken.
  3. Mark the external high.The highest point reached after the break, once price starts pulling back.
  4. Everything between them is internal.Every smaller high and low between the external low and the external high belongs to internal structure, however clear it looks.
  5. Update only on a close.The external points move only when a candle closes beyond one of them. Wicks do not move them.

This is a judgement, so keep it simple

Traders draw internal and external structure slightly differently, and no version is the official one. What matters is that you use one rule, the same way, on every chart. The rule above is the one this site uses: a swing point is external when it caused, or was made by, a break of structure.

Two kinds of break

Internal breakExternal break (BoS)
What closesA candle beyond a small high or low inside the legA candle beyond a main swing high or low
What it meansThe pullback is turning, or only pausingThe trend continues, or has changed
Changes your direction?NoYes, if it is against the trend
How you use itAs the shift for an entry, but only at a marked zone and after a sweepTo set long or short, the protected point and the target

Rule

An internal break against the external trend is a pullback until an external point breaks. Do not sell an uptrend because a small low gave way. That move is usually price coming down to your demand.

Part 5

The four sweeps, and what each one tells you

A sweep is always the same event: price trades beyond a level, takes the stops and closes back. Where it happens decides what it means.

1

Internal sweep during the pullback

Price takes an internal low on the way down, before it has reached your zone. This is inducement being cleared.

  • Meaning: the pullback is still running.
  • Action: none. Keep waiting for the zone.
2

Internal sweep at the zone

Price takes an internal low inside or just into your demand, then turns.

  • Meaning: the pullback may be finishing here.
  • Action: wait for the shift, then place the order. The stop goes below this sweep.
3

External sweep with the trend

Price runs above the external high and takes the stops there.

  • Meaning: the target has been reached.
  • Action: the trade is over. A close above gives a new BoS; a wick only can start a deeper pullback.
4

External sweep against the trend

Price dips below the external low, closes back above it, and then shifts up.

  • Meaning: the largest pool of sell stops has been taken and the trend has held. This is the strongest sweep.
  • Action: trade it with the sweep model. If candles close below instead, it is a break and the trend has changed.
Part 6

Price moves from internal liquidity to external liquidity

After a break, price comes back inside the range to collect internal liquidity and fill a zone. Then it leaves for the external liquidity on the other side. Then the cycle repeats.

Pullback

Price works down through internal lows. Each one taken is an internal sweep. None of them is a reason to buy yet.

Zone

The last internal sweep happens at demand. The shift that follows is an internal break in the direction of the external trend.

Expansion

Price runs to the external high. That is where the trade is closed, because that is where the opposite orders are waiting.

Rule

Enter on internal liquidity being taken. Exit at external liquidity. If you are entering where external liquidity sits, you are probably the liquidity.

Part 8

Internal on one timeframe is external on the next

The two words are relative to the chart you are on.

  • The internal structure of the 4H is roughly the external structure of the 15m.
  • So when the 15m breaks its own external high inside a 4H demand, the 4H sees only an internal break. Both are true.
  • Name the timeframe every time: “4H external high”, “15m external low”. It removes most of the confusion.
  • Take direction and target from the higher timeframe’s external structure. Take the entry from the lower timeframe’s structure. See top-down analysis.

Common mistakes

  • Calling an internal break a trend change and trading against the external trend.
  • Buying the first internal sweep in the middle of the pullback, before price has reached a zone.
  • Putting the target at an internal high. Price can stall there, but the draw is the external high.
  • Putting the stop behind an internal low that has not been swept. It is liquidity, and the pullback is likely to take it.
  • Treating a close below the external low as a sweep. A sweep closes back inside; a close beyond is a break.
  • Re-labelling the external points after the fact so the trade looks valid.

Checklist

  1. Where are the external high and low?Marked on the higher timeframe, from the last break of structure.
  2. Which way did the last external break go?That is the only direction I trade.
  3. Is the current move internal?Yes while price is between the two external points. I treat it as a pullback.
  4. Where is the internal liquidity?Internal lows above my zone. I expect them to be taken first.
  5. Has an internal sweep happened at my zone?Yes, with the protected low still intact. Now I wait for the shift.
  6. Where is the external liquidity?Above the external high. That is the target.
  7. What cancels it?A candle close below the external low.
